# Culture Builder: The Handwritten Five (November)

Five handwritten notes a week, mailed to their houses, until you have written to everyone.

## Objective

Write five handwritten notes a week to staff, and mail them to their homes rather than dropping them in a mailbox at school. Twenty minutes on a Friday. Start in November, when the tank is emptiest and the stretch to Thanksgiving is the longest run of the year without a break. Do it every week and you will have written to every adult in a mid-sized building by spring.

## Time Needed

20 minutes a week. Same slot, every week.

## Supplies / Setup

- Notecards and envelopes. Use the ones in the supply closet.
- Home addresses from your office, handled with the same care as any other staff data.
- Stamps. This is the one place a school budget line is worth spending, and it is a few dollars a month. If there is genuinely no budget, buy them yourself and never mention it.
- A printed staff list you check off. Everyone in the building is on it.

## How It Works

1. **Block the twenty minutes and treat it as untouchable (weekly).** Friday afternoon works because the week is fresh in your memory. Put it on the calendar and defend it the way you would defend an observation.
2. **Pick five from the list, not from memory (2 minutes).** Always the list. Memory hands you the five people you already appreciate publicly, which means the same eight people get every note you write all year.
3. **Name one specific thing (12 minutes).** Two or three sentences. A moment you saw, or a moment somebody told you about. "Thank you for all you do" is not a note, it is a card, and everybody knows the difference. If you cannot think of anything specific for a person, that is not a reason to skip them, it is a reason to go be in their room next week.
4. **Address it to their house (4 minutes).** This is the part that makes it land. A note in a school mailbox is professional. A handwritten envelope that shows up at home, on a Tuesday, in November, is something people keep on the fridge.
5. **Check the list and keep going (2 minutes).** When the list is complete, start it over. If you are still on the first pass in April, the list will tell you honestly how well you protected the twenty minutes.

## Why It Works

- November is when this matters. There is no break between early November and Thanksgiving in most calendars, morale is at its lowest measured point of the year, and almost nothing you spend money on will move it as much as this.
- Mailing to the house changes the meaning. It arrives in the part of someone's life that school does not usually reach, and it is read by their family.
- Working from a list rather than memory is what makes it equitable. Every leader has a default set of people they notice, and this is the correction for it.
- Specific is the whole payload. A generic note actually costs you credibility because it proves the exercise is a routine rather than attention.
- Handwriting is doing work that a well-written email cannot do. It is slow, it is obviously personal, and it cannot be forwarded.

## Script for the Leader

There is no announcement for this one. If a staff member mentions theirs:

"I write five a week. Not a program, I just noticed I was thinking things about people and never saying them. Yours took me about four minutes and I meant every word of it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Handle home addresses carefully and get whatever approval your district requires before you start. Ask the office rather than assuming.
- If someone is uncomfortable receiving mail at home, note it and send theirs to school instead. One quiet exception costs nothing.
- Include every adult. Custodial, food service, office, paraprofessionals, part-time, and the itinerant staff who are in your building one day a week and belong to nobody.
- Do not photograph them or post them. The moment this becomes visible evidence of your leadership, it stops being a gift.
- Do not skip a week and plan to write ten the next. Ten notes in one sitting produces ten mediocre notes.
- Keep going past November. Starting in November is the point, stopping in December is the failure.
