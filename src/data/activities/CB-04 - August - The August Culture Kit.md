# Culture Builder: The August Culture Kit (August)

Four weeks of August printed, scheduled, and boxed before anyone walks in.

## Objective

Assemble everything August needs while you still have the margin to do it. August is the month where you will have the least time and the most impact, and it is the month a new habit either takes or quietly dies. Culture work does not fail because leaders stop believing in it. It fails because the cards were not cut.

## Time Needed

25 minutes, plus printer time.

## Supplies / Setup

- Printer and cardstock.
- One box or four folders, labeled Week 1 through Week 4.
- The four August activities: Speed Connections, Your Seat at This Table, First Day Photos, and This Year I'm All In On.
- Your calendar.
- Painter's tape and markers.

## How It Works

1. **Calendar holds first (4 minutes).** Put all four on the calendar right now, each attached to the specific meeting or moment it lives in. Not "Week 2" — "Aug 14 staff meeting, first 25 minutes." An activity without a slot is a wish.
2. **Print and cut (10 minutes).** Placemat cards, prompt cards, commitment half-sheets. Print ten percent more than your headcount. Cut them now, put each set in its folder, and never think about it again.
3. **Claim the wall space (5 minutes).** Walk the building and physically tape off where "Our Table" and the "We're All In On" wall are going. Deciding this in July prevents the August version of the conversation, which is standing in a hallway holding forty cards saying "we'll find a spot."
4. **Assign the one thing you cannot do (3 minutes).** Name a photographer. Not for social media — for you. Someone whose actual job that week is capturing what the room looked like.
5. **Write your own answers (3 minutes).** You go first in three of these four. Draft your placemat, your six words, and your "all in on" commitment now, on paper, while you have time to make them honest. Doing it in the parking lot five minutes before is how leaders end up saying something safe.

## Why It Works

- Culture activities die on logistics, not on will. Every leader means to run them. The ones who do are the ones who cut the cards in July.
- Wall space decided in advance is wall space that exists. Wall space decided in the moment becomes a table in the back of the lounge nobody looks at.
- Pre-writing your own answers is what makes the modeling real. Vulnerability you improvised under time pressure reads as generic, and staff can tell the difference immediately.
- A named photographer means you finish August with artifacts you can use with families, your board, and next year's hiring pool. Nobody ever regrets having the photos.
- Front-loading the whole month means that when the first real crisis of the year lands in week two, the culture work still happens, because it is already done.

## Script for the Leader

For the photographer ask:

"I need one thing from you this month. Be the person with the camera. Not for social media — for us. I want us to have an actual record of what this year looked like at the start, so in May we can see it."

## Facilitator Tips

- The box lives in your office, not the workroom. Things in the workroom get borrowed.
- If you only get one of the four done, do Week 1. Speed Connections sets the relational floor for everything after it.
- Add a small stack of blank cards to each folder for the people who join in September. Late hires who missed the wall are the fastest way to create a two-tier staff.
- Extension: while the printer is running, write the one-paragraph email you will send families in week three with the photo of the completed wall. Ninety seconds now, and August's family communication is done.
