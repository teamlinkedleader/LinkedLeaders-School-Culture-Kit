# Culture Builder: Consultancy Protocol (Bonus)

One colleague brings a real dilemma and the group is banned from giving advice for the first ten minutes.

## Objective

Give one staff member structured help with a genuine, current, unresolved problem in their practice. The protocol's power comes entirely from its constraints: the presenter stays quiet while the group discusses, and the group must ask questions before offering anything. It is the most useful thirty minutes available in a school and almost nobody runs it because the silence feels wrong.

## Time Needed

25 to 30 minutes per dilemma. Fits inside a staff meeting or a team block.

## Supplies / Setup

- One presenter with a real dilemma, briefed in advance to bring something unresolved rather than something already solved.
- A group of four to seven. Mixed roles works better than same-grade.
- A timekeeper who is not the presenter and not you.
- The steps posted visibly, because the group will want to break them.

## Sample Prompts

- **The presenter frames it as a question.** Not "here is what is happening" but "how do I…" or "what am I missing about…"
- **Clarifying questions.** Factual only. "How many students?" "When did it start?" Anything that begins "have you tried" is not a clarifying question.
- **Probing questions.** Aimed at helping the presenter think, not at leading them somewhere. "What would have to be true for that to work?" "What is the part you keep coming back to?"
- **Discussion, presenter silent.** "What I heard is…" "What if…" "I wonder whether…"

## How It Works

1. **Presenter describes the dilemma and asks their question, 5 minutes.** Uninterrupted. They end with the actual question they want help with, which is often not the question they started with.
2. **Clarifying questions, 3 minutes.** Facts only. The facilitator's job is to block disguised advice, which will start immediately and will sound like "have you considered."
3. **Probing questions, 5 minutes.** Questions that help the presenter think rather than steer them. Still no suggestions. The presenter answers briefly.
4. **Group discusses the dilemma while the presenter stays silent and takes notes, 10 minutes.** This is the part that feels wrong and does the work. The group talks about the problem as though the presenter is not there. The presenter is not allowed to defend, explain or correct, and hearing colleagues think about your problem without having to respond is where the insight comes from.
5. **Presenter reflects, 3 minutes, then everyone stops.** They say what they heard and what they will try. No group response, no wrapping up, no reassurance. Then it ends.

## Why It Works

- The presenter's silence during the discussion is the whole mechanism. Being able to listen without defending is what lets someone hear something they would otherwise argue with.
- Delaying advice past the halfway point means the advice, when it comes, addresses the real problem rather than the first version of it.
- Separating clarifying from probing questions is what stops the group solving it in ninety seconds with the wrong solution.
- It builds a norm that asking for help is normal practice rather than an admission. That norm is worth more than any individual dilemma solved.
- It is short, repeatable, and needs no materials, so it can become routine rather than an event.

## Script for the Leader

"I will go first, with something I am actually stuck on rather than something I have already worked out. Here is the rule that will feel wrong: once I have finished and you have asked your questions, you are going to talk about my problem for ten minutes as if I am not in the room, and I am not allowed to say anything. Not to defend myself, not to explain, not to correct you. I just take notes. That is the part that works."

## Facilitator Tips

- Present first yourself, with a genuine dilemma. Nobody will bring a real problem if the leader has modelled bringing a safe one.
- Block disguised advice hard in the clarifying stage. "Have you tried" is the phrase to listen for.
- Enforce the presenter's silence gently but absolutely. It will be broken in the first minute out of habit.
- Do not let the group reassure the presenter. Warmth is not the point and it dilutes the usefulness.
- End on time and do not add a group debrief. The abruptness is part of the design.
- Run it monthly with a rotating presenter and it becomes the most valuable routine in the building.

## Display Idea

The five steps and their timings, posted in whatever room your team meets in, so the protocol can be run by anyone without you facilitating it.
