# Culture Builder: Pass the Baton (October)

One object moves through the building all year, and it only moves with a reason attached.

## Objective

Start a staff-to-staff recognition chain that runs the rest of the year without you. One ordinary object, passed weekly from whoever holds it to whoever they choose, with a written reason recorded in a log that travels with it. It takes ten minutes to launch in October and by March it is one of the things people describe when someone asks what it is like to work at your school.

## Time Needed

10 minutes to launch. About two minutes a week after that, and none of them are yours.

## Supplies / Setup

- One object with some character. A trophy from a thrift shop, an absurd hat, a painted brick, a hard hat, an old award nobody claims. Free or nearly free, and slightly ridiculous is better than dignified.
- A notebook or clipboard that lives with the object. This is the part that matters most.
- Two rules, written on the inside cover: you hold it one week, and you cannot pass it to whoever passed it to you.
- Your own first entry, already written.

## How It Works

1. **Launch it yourself with a real entry (5 minutes).** At a staff meeting, hold up the object, explain the two rules, and pass it to the first person. Read your log entry out loud. Make it specific and make it about something most of the room did not see, because that establishes what the log is for.
2. **Write the reason before you pass it (each holder, two minutes).** The current holder writes in the log: the date, who they are passing it to, and the specific thing that person did. Specific is the standard. "Because she works so hard" gets gently corrected the first two times and then never happens again.
3. **Pass it in person, in front of somebody (each week).** Not left on a desk. The handoff is public, even if public just means the two people plus whoever is in the hallway. Some schools do it at the end of a staff meeting, which works fine, but a hallway handoff on a Tuesday works better.
4. **Do not manage it (all year).** After the launch you do not steer it, remind people, or suggest recipients. If it stalls for a week, wait. If it stalls for three, ask the last holder privately whether they are stuck rather than announcing anything.
5. **Read the log out loud in May (10 minutes, once).** At the last staff meeting of the year, read the whole thing. Thirty-plus specific moments of adults noticing each other, in their own words, is the best end-of-year staff meeting content that exists and it cost you nothing.

## Why It Works

- Peer recognition outperforms leader recognition because it cannot be dismissed as management technique. A colleague has nothing to gain by noticing you.
- The log is the real artifact. The object is just the excuse that makes the log get written.
- The rule against passing it back is what keeps it from becoming a two-person game, which is exactly what happens without it.
- Leaving it unmanaged is what makes it belong to the staff. The moment a leader starts nudging it toward the people who "deserve a turn," it becomes an assignment.
- It reaches people you cannot reach. Staff pass it to the paraprofessional who covered a room, the custodian who fixed something at 6 a.m., the person who had a hard week, and you would not have known about any of it.

## Script for the Leader

"This ridiculous thing is going to move around this building all year. Two rules. You hold it for a week, and you can't pass it back to whoever gave it to you. Before you pass it, you write in this notebook why. Specific. Not 'she's great,' but what she actually did. I'll start. Last Thursday Marisol covered two classes during her prep and never told me, and I only found out because a kid mentioned it. Marisol, it's yours."

## Facilitator Tips

- Make the object slightly absurd. A serious trophy makes people self-conscious. A hard hat spray-painted gold makes people laugh and then write something sincere.
- Hold the specificity standard for the first month. After that it is self-enforcing, because everyone reads the log.
- If it goes quiet, do not announce a relaunch. Find the last holder and ask quietly if they are stuck on who to give it to. Nine times out of ten that is all it is.
- Do not exclude anyone. Classified staff, part-time staff, the itinerant speech therapist. If someone cannot receive it, the chain is a hierarchy with a hat on.
- Photograph the handoffs when you happen to catch them. In May you will want them.
- Keep the notebook when it fills up. Starting year two with year one's log on the table is a genuinely great moment.
