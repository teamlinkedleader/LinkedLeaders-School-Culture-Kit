# Culture Builder: The Two Who Cannot Work Together (February)

Deal with the staff conflict everyone is working around, before another year forms around it.

## Objective

Take the two staff members your building has quietly reorganised itself around and get them to a working agreement. Every school has at least one pair. Meetings are arranged so they are not both there. Duties are set so they do not overlap. New staff learn within a fortnight not to mention one to the other. It is treated as weather rather than as a problem, and the cost is paid by everyone else in small daily increments.

## Time Needed

25 minutes with the two of them, after separate conversations first.

## Supplies / Setup

- Two separate ten-minute conversations before the joint one. Never open with the joint meeting.
- A room that is not your office if your office is where difficult things happen.
- Paper. The agreement gets written down in the room.
- Your own honest answer to how long you have known about this and done nothing.

## How It Works

1. **See each of them separately first (10 minutes each, before).** One question: what would need to be true for you to work with them effectively. Not what went wrong, which produces a history lesson you cannot adjudicate. What would need to be true. Listen and do not carry messages between them.
2. **Open the joint meeting by naming the cost, not the conflict (4 minutes).** Do not relitigate the origin. Name what it is costing other people: the meetings arranged around them, what new staff learn in their first fortnight, the students who have both of them. Make the reason for the meeting the school rather than their feelings.
3. **Ask for behaviours, not feelings (8 minutes).** You are not asking them to like each other and you should say so plainly. You are asking what each will do and not do. Reply to an email within a day. Raise a problem directly rather than through a third person. Not discuss the other with staff who report to either of them.
4. **Write it down in the room and both keep a copy (6 minutes).** Specific, behavioural, short. Six lines is plenty. An agreement that stays verbal is one both parties remember differently within a fortnight.
5. **Set a review date and hold it (2 minutes).** Four to six weeks, diarised before anyone leaves. Without the review the agreement decays quietly and you have spent your credibility for nothing.

## Why It Works

- The cost of an unaddressed staff conflict is not paid by the two people involved. It is paid in small daily increments by everyone routing around them, and by any student who has both.
- Asking what would need to be true, rather than what happened, is the move that makes this tractable. History is unresolvable and you were not there.
- Explicitly not requiring them to like each other lowers the stakes enough for both to engage. Professional conduct is achievable; forced reconciliation is not.
- Behavioural commitments can be kept and checked. "Being more positive" cannot be either.
- February is the right month. There is enough year left for a changed pattern to matter, and the accumulated cost is by now obvious to everyone.

## Script for the Leader

Opening the joint conversation:

"I am not here to work out what happened three years ago and I am not going to try. I am also not asking you to like each other. What I am telling you is what this is costing other people. I have watched staff arrange meetings so you are not both in them, and I have watched a new teacher learn in her second week not to mention one of you to the other. That is not fair on them and it is on me for leaving it this long. So today we write down how you two will operate. Behaviours, not feelings."

## Facilitator Tips

- Never open with the joint meeting. Separate conversations first, always.
- Do not carry messages between them beforehand. You become a third party in the conflict the moment you do.
- Acknowledge your own delay out loud. Everyone in the building knows how long this has run, and pretending otherwise costs you credibility in the room.
- Keep the agreement to behaviours that can be observed. If you cannot tell whether it happened, it does not go on the paper.
- If one party will not engage, that is now a performance conversation and a different meeting. Do not let the joint session drift into one.
- Hold the review. An unreviewed agreement is worse than none, because it teaches both of them that the process is theatre.

## Display Idea

None, obviously. This one stays between three people, and treating it otherwise would be a serious breach.
