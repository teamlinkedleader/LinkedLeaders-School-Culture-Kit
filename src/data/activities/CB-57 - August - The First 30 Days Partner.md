# Culture Builder: The First 30 Days Partner (August)

Every new hire gets a person, not a binder, and six human things to get done in a month.

## Objective

Make sure nobody new to your building spends their first month figuring out how things work by guessing. Most schools have an onboarding process that is entirely compliance, entirely paper, and entirely finished by the second day. This gives every new staff member one named partner and six specific human tasks to complete together in thirty days. It takes 20 minutes to set up and it is the single cheapest retention move available to you.

## Time Needed

20 minutes to set up, before staff report. About 15 minutes of partner time per week, absorbed into the day.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your list of new staff, including anyone new to the building even if they are not new to teaching. Transfers get overlooked constantly and they struggle just as much.
- A list of veteran staff who are actually generous, not just senior. These are different lists and you know which is which.
- One index card per new hire, with the six tasks written on it.
- A spot to post the pairings where everyone can see them.

## How It Works

1. **Pair deliberately, not by grade level (10 minutes of setup).** Match each new hire with one veteran chosen for temperament, not proximity. The best partner is often not on the same team, because a partner who is not adjacent to the new hire's evaluation or daily politics is safer to ask a dumb question.
2. **Write the six tasks on a card (5 minutes of setup).** Not compliance. Human. A version that works: eat lunch together twice. Walk the whole building including the boiler room and the loading dock. Meet the custodian, the head cook, and the office manager by name. Sit in each other's rooms for ten minutes. Name the three unwritten rules of this building. Tell the new hire the story of one thing this school does that nobody explains to outsiders.
3. **Hand them out in front of everyone (3 minutes).** At the first full staff meeting, name the pairs out loud and hand over the cards. Public pairing means the veteran actually follows through and the new hire knows it is real rather than a favor.
4. **Take the new hires to lunch yourself in week two (leader, 30 minutes, once).** Not a check-in, no agenda. Ask what has surprised them and what nobody told them. Write down every answer, because a new hire's first two weeks are the only time anyone will ever see your building with clear eyes.
5. **Close it at 30 days (2 minutes).** Partners sign the card, the new hire keeps it. Ask each new hire for one thing that should be on next year's card. That is how the card gets better without you having to think about it.

## Why It Works

- New staff leave over isolation far more than over workload. A named person to ask stupid questions is worth more than any binder you will ever assemble.
- Choosing veterans for generosity rather than seniority is the whole game. The wrong partner teaches a new hire the building's cynicism in a week.
- The six tasks are human on purpose. Everything on the compliance checklist gets done anyway because it has to. Nothing on this list gets done unless it is assigned.
- Public pairing creates accountability in both directions and quietly tells veteran staff that welcoming people is a real job here.
- The new hire's fresh-eyes list is free consulting on your own building, and it has a shelf life of about three weeks before they stop noticing.

## Script for the Leader

For the staff meeting where you announce the pairs:

"We have four people new to this building this year. Every one of them has a partner, and I picked the partners on purpose. Here's the card, six things, thirty days, none of it is paperwork. Veterans, this is not a favor you're doing me. Nobody in this room learned how this place actually works from a handbook, they learned it from a person, and this year we're being deliberate about who that person is."

## Facilitator Tips

- Include transfers, returning staff after a long leave, and long-term subs. Anyone who does not know where the good copier is belongs on this list.
- Do not pair a new hire with their own mentor of record if your state has an induction program. Two roles on one person turns the safe relationship into an evaluated one.
- Do the lunch yourself. Delegating it tells four people exactly how much of a priority they are.
- Write down the fresh-eyes answers and act on one of them within a month, visibly. That is what proves you asked for real.
- Repeat a light version in January for mid-year hires. A person who starts in January without any of this has the hardest job in the building. See the bonus bank activity on welcoming a mid-year hire.
