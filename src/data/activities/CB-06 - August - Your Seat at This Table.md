# Culture Builder: Your Seat at This Table (August)

A relationship-building activity that affirms every staff member's unique value, and makes it visible to the whole team.

## Objective

Begin the year by naming what each person brings to the school community. Not their job title, but their unique strengths, gifts, and contributions. Build a culture of belonging from the very first meeting by showing every person: there is a seat at this table, and it has your name on it.

## Time Needed

20–25 minutes.

## Supplies / Setup

- Pre-printed placemat cards, half-sheet or full-sheet, with the prompts printed on them.
- Pens or markers.
- A display wall, table runner, or board labeled "Our Table."
- Your own placemat, completed in advance.

## Sample Prompts

Each card has four fill-in sections:

- My name.
- My role.
- What I bring to this team that you might not know about.
- This year, I'm showing up for you by.

## How It Works

1. **Distribute the placemats at the start of a pre-service staff meeting.** Give staff 5 minutes to fill them out individually.
2. **Pair up with someone you don't normally work with.** Share what you wrote, not to be edited, just to be heard. Four minutes.
3. **Each pair introduces their partner to the group** using one thing from their placemat. Not a full read-aloud, just one highlight per person.
4. **Post all placemats on the display wall.** Invite staff to walk the table during a break and read each other's.
5. **Leave the display up for the first month of school** as a daily reminder of who is in the room and what they bring.

## Why It Works

- It surfaces hidden strengths: the librarian who is a certified grief counselor, the para who speaks four languages, the custodian who coaches youth basketball.
- Being introduced by someone else feels more meaningful than introducing yourself.
- The "showing up for you by" prompt creates early relational accountability.
- Classified and certified staff participate equally. Every role has a placemat.
- The display keeps the activity alive past the meeting. Culture is built between meetings, not just during them.

## Script for the Leader

"Every one of you walked into this building this year with something to offer, something that goes beyond your job description. Today we're going to name some of that. Because culture isn't built by job titles. It's built by the people in the room. And every single one of you has a seat at this table."

## Facilitator Tips

- Complete your own placemat and share it first. Model vulnerability and specificity.
- For the "what I bring that you might not know" prompt, push people past generic answers. If someone writes "hard work," invite them to get more specific: "What's an example of that?"
- Photograph the completed display. Share it in your next parent communication as "Meet the team that's showing up for your kids."

## Display Idea

A wall, table runner or board titled "Our Table," with every placemat posted and no separation between classified and certified staff. Left up for the first month so it is read in passing rather than at a meeting.
