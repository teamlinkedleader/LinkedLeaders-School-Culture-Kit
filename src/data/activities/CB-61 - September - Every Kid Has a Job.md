# Culture Builder: Every Kid Has a Job (September)

Every student in the building holds one real, named job that the place would actually miss.

## Objective

Give every student a role with a name, a description, and a consequence if it does not get done. Belonging is not only about being liked, it is about being needed, and a kid who holds a job that matters has a reason to be there on the days when nothing else is working. This is a 20-minute classroom block in September, run building-wide on the same day.

## Time Needed

20 minutes of class time. About 10 minutes of leader prep.

## Supplies / Setup

- Chart paper or a section of wall per classroom for the job board.
- Index cards, one per job.
- A short list of your own school-wide jobs, decided before you ask teachers to do theirs.
- The rule, written on the board: a job is only a job if the room notices when it does not happen.

## How It Works

1. **Name the school-wide jobs first (leader, 10 minutes of prep).** Before teachers assign anything in their rooms, name three or four building-level jobs held by students, publicly, with real responsibility. Morning announcements. Greeting visitors at the office. Walking new students to class for their first week. Then say who holds each one by name.
2. **Set the standard in the room (3 minutes).** Teachers explain the rule: a job is real if its absence is noticed. Being "line leader" for a week is a turn, not a job. Being the person who checks that every chair is down and the door is locked is a job.
3. **The class builds the job list, not the teacher (7 minutes).** Ask students what this room actually needs done every day and every week. They will find things the teacher never thought to delegate, and the list is better for it. Write one job per index card.
4. **Match jobs to people on purpose (7 minutes).** Enough jobs that every single student holds one. No exceptions, no leftovers, no student holding "helper." Match deliberately: the kid who is always moving gets the job that requires moving, the kid who notices everything gets the job that requires noticing, and the kid nobody picks gets a job that puts them in contact with people.
5. **Post the board and hold it (3 minutes).** Every name, every job, at student eye level. Rotate on a fixed schedule, monthly or quarterly, and announce the rotation in advance so it reads as a promotion rather than a removal.

## Why It Works

- Being needed is a stronger form of belonging than being liked, and it is available to every kid, including the ones who are not socially easy.
- The absence test is what separates this from classroom chores. A job nobody misses teaches a student that their contribution is decorative.
- Letting students name the jobs surfaces real work and gives them ownership of the list before they own a role on it.
- Deliberate matching is where the leadership happens. The student who is hardest to reach is usually the one whose job placement matters most, and the placement should be made on purpose rather than by drawing names.
- Every student holding one is non-negotiable, because a job board with three names on it is a board that tells the other twenty-five students where they stand.

## Script for the Leader

For the morning announcement when you launch it:

"Starting today, every student in this school has a job. Not a chore, a job. Something this building actually needs done, and something we'd notice if it didn't happen. Mine is that I'm at the front door every morning. Ms. Rivera's is that she reads these announcements. Yours goes up on the board in your room today, and it's real. If your job doesn't feel real, come tell me and we'll fix it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not let "helper" or "assistant to" appear on any board. Those are the jobs given to students adults have not thought about, and students know exactly what they mean.
- Rotate on a schedule that is announced in advance. An unannounced rotation feels like being fired.
- Secondary schools: it works, but it has to run through advisory and the jobs have to be genuinely adult in weight. Managing the class calendar, running the room's part of an all-school project, onboarding transfers.
- Watch the kid who quietly does not do their job. That is usually information about something else, and it is worth a conversation rather than a replacement.
- Refresh in January so mid-year arrivals get a job in their first week rather than waiting for a rotation.

## Display Idea

The job board goes at student eye level with the student's name and job title in the same size type. A visitor should be able to tell who runs what in that room without asking the teacher.
