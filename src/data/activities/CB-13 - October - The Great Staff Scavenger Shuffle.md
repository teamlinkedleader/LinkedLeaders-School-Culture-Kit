# Culture Builder: The Great Staff Scavenger Shuffle (October)

A relationship-building, movement-based team challenge.

## Objective

Break down silos, spark fresh connections across grade levels, and see your school through new eyes, while having a blast. The mixing is the whole point: staff who have worked in the same building for six years and never spoken end up on the same team with twenty minutes and a shared goal.

## Time Needed

20 to 25 minutes, including the return and the judging.

## Supplies / Setup

- Mixed teams of five or six, built in advance rather than chosen in the room.
- A single "find list" document with a photo collage of 10 to 15 items from around the school: bulletin boards, murals, quirky objects, classroom displays, signage, mascot statues.
- A timer, and phones, which everyone already has.
- A team colour or team name each, which does more for the energy than it sounds like it should.

## Sample Prompts

Example items for the find list. The key is choosing from **all parts of campus** so teams have to move and explore:

- The bulletin board with the blue border and the inspirational quote.
- The hallway artwork made by kindergarteners.
- The science poster with the skeleton diagram.
- The staff values poster near the main office.
- The student artwork that includes the school mascot.

## How It Works

1. **Form mixed teams deliberately, before the meeting.** Mix grade levels, classified and certified staff, and new and veteran employees. Left to choose, people pick the person they already eat lunch with, and the activity does nothing.
2. **Distribute the find list and the rules.** Find as many items as you can within the time limit. To earn credit, the team must find the item and take a group selfie with it clearly visible, with at least four members in the picture. Keep all photos on one team member's phone for quick review.
3. **Set the boundaries and go.** Fifteen to twenty minutes, the entire campus unless otherwise noted. Bonus points for creativity, speed and team spirit.
4. **Return to home base.** All teams back in the room when the timer ends; late teams lose a point a minute. A designated judge reviews each photo roll and counts the confirmed finds.
5. **Declare a winner and build the collage.** Afterwards, collect the best photos from each group into a single digital collage for the newsletter, the staff room wall, or the end-of-semester highlight reel.

## Why It Works

- Movement and a deadline do more for cross-role connection in twenty minutes than a structured mixer does in an hour.
- Deliberately mixed teams put a first-year para and a twenty-year veteran on the same problem, which is a conversation neither would have started.
- Photographing the building makes staff look at a place they stopped seeing years ago.
- The collage extends the activity past the meeting, which is where most one-off team-building dies.

## Script for the Leader

Read aloud at the meeting:

"Today we're doing something fun to remind us that we all share this building, and each other, even when we're tucked into our own corners. You'll be going on a scavenger hunt across campus in mixed teams. Your goal: find as many items as you can and prove it with a team selfie. You've got 20 minutes, so hustle, laugh, enjoy the building, and get to know some colleagues you might not usually work with."

## Facilitator Tips

- Build the teams yourself and post them. This is the single decision that determines whether the activity works.
- Choose items from every corner of campus, deliberately including the parts of the building some staff never visit.
- **On the prize:** the original version offers lunch on the school leader. That is lovely and it costs money, so it is optional rather than part of the design. Free alternatives that land just as well: the winning team picks the next staff meeting's start time, gets first choice of duty slot, or gets a duty covered by administration.
- Silly awards cost nothing and are worth doing regardless: Most Spirited Team, Best Dramatic Pose, Most Unexpected Interpretation.
- Check the route is accessible for every member of staff before you set the boundaries, and adjust the list rather than expecting someone to opt out.

## Display Idea

One collage in the staff room with every team represented, plus a copy in the newsletter. Photos of adults laughing in their own building is the most persuasive recruitment material a school has.
