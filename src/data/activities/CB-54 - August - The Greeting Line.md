# Culture Builder: The Greeting Line (August)

Ten minutes at the front door every morning for the first three weeks, learning names out loud.

## Objective

Be the first face students see, every single morning, for the first three weeks of school, and use that time to learn names on purpose. This is the cheapest, most visible culture move a leader has, and almost every leader abandons it by week two because the office pulls them back. The design here exists to make it survive contact with your actual calendar.

## Time Needed

10 minutes a day, every morning for the first three weeks.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your calendar, blocked. Same ten minutes daily, recurring, named "Front Door." It has to look like a meeting or it will get eaten by one.
- A pocket card with the names of students you do not yet know, refreshed weekly from your roster.
- A posted rotation sheet by the main entrance, with three or four staff signed up alongside you each day.
- Nothing in your hands. No radio in your face, no phone, no coffee you have to manage.

## How It Works

1. **Block it and post the rotation before day one (10 minutes of setup).** Put your name on every day of the three weeks. Put three or four staff names on each day next to yours. Post it by the door where students and families can read it, because a posted schedule with names is a promise you cannot quietly drop.
2. **Stand where the students actually enter (daily).** Not the lobby, not behind the desk. The door, or the bus loop, or the car line, wherever the volume is. If you have two entrances, alternate and say which one you will be at.
3. **Greet by name whenever you can (daily).** Name plus one word. "Morning, Andre." "Hey Jasmine, good luck today." Handshakes and fist bumps are fine but they are not the point. The name is the point.
4. **Learn three new names a day (daily).** When you do not know a student, ask. "I don't know your name yet and I should. Tell me again tomorrow if I blow it." Write the three on your pocket card that night. Three a day is 45 students in three weeks.
5. **Count it out loud on Fridays (2 minutes).** Tell staff how many names you have added. It is a small, honest number and it tells the building you are doing the same work you asked them to do in "Every Student Claimed."

## Why It Works

- Visibility is the fastest trust you can build, and the front door is the highest-traffic place in the building. Ten minutes there reaches more people than an hour anywhere else.
- Students who are greeted by name at the door enter the building differently, and staff can see it happening from thirty feet away. That is why the staff rotation matters more than your presence alone.
- Asking a student for their name, and admitting you do not know it, is worth more than knowing it. Kids read the admission as respect.
- The posted rotation is what makes it stick. A private intention dies in week two. A sheet on the wall with your name on all fifteen days does not.
- Doing it in August establishes the pattern. Students will keep looking for you at that door in March, and you will find out fast how much it mattered when you are not there.

## Script for the Leader

For the staff meeting where you post the rotation:

"I'm at the front door for ten minutes every morning for the first three weeks, and I'd like three of you with me each day. I've put the sheet by the door so you can hold me to it. My goal is to learn three names a day I don't already know, and I'll tell you Friday how I did. If you see me not out there, come get me."

## Facilitator Tips

- Delegate the radio. If you are the person who gets pulled for every issue in the building, hand the radio to your AP for those ten minutes and mean it.
- Do not use the door time for business. A staff member who catches you there with a question gets "come find me at 9:15." Once it becomes a meeting spot, it stops being a greeting line.
- Car line and bus loop count, and in many schools they reach more families than the front door does. Parents watching you greet their kid by name is a bonus you get for free.
- If you genuinely cannot do mornings, do dismissal instead and keep everything else the same. Do not do both and do neither well.
- Week four is the test. Most leaders quit here. Decide now whether you are extending it all year at a reduced frequency, and tell staff which, rather than just fading out.
