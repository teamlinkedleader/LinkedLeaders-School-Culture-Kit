# Culture Builder: The Student Culture Council (April)

Give a dozen students one real decision, not a suggestion box.

## Objective

Hand a group of students one genuine decision about school culture and then implement whatever they decide. Most student voice in schools is consultative theatre: students are asked, adults decide, and students notice within about two meetings. The distinguishing feature here is that you do not get to overrule the outcome, which is exactly why the decision has to be scoped carefully before you hand it over.

## Time Needed

25 minutes for the first meeting. About 20 minutes a month afterwards.

## Supplies / Setup

- Twelve students chosen deliberately, not elected. Election gives you twelve confident students who already like school.
- One decision that is genuinely yours to give away, with a real budget or a real change attached.
- The honest constraints, written down before the meeting.
- Somewhere to meet that is not your office.

## How It Works

1. **Choose for range, not for polish (7 minutes of prep).** Ask staff for the student who complains most, the one who is never picked, one from each year group, one who has been here two months, one who struggles academically, one who is quietly influential. A council of head students tells you what head students think, which you already know.
2. **Give them one real decision and say what the constraints are (5 minutes).** Be specific and honest. "You decide what we do with the courtyard, you have £200, it has to be safe, and it cannot need staff to supervise it." Constraints are not a limitation on student voice, they are what makes the decision real rather than a wish.
3. **Say plainly that you will not overrule it (2 minutes).** Then do not, including when they choose something you find mildly embarrassing. The first decision is a test of whether this is real, and the whole thing lives or dies on it.
4. **Let them work while you say very little (8 minutes).** Your job is to answer factual questions about constraints. It is not to steer, and adults steer without noticing. If you find yourself explaining why an idea is difficult, stop talking.
5. **Implement it visibly and name them (3 minutes to plan).** Announce what is changing and who decided it, by name. The point is not the courtyard. The point is that the school demonstrably changed because twelve students decided it should.

## Why It Works

- Students identify consultative theatre almost immediately. One implemented decision buys more credibility than three years of surveys.
- Choosing for range rather than election is what makes the council informative. The students who are not thriving know things about your school that thriving students cannot tell you.
- Honest constraints make the exercise real. Unlimited scope produces fantasy, and then adults have to say no, which teaches exactly the lesson you were trying to avoid.
- Committing not to overrule is the whole mechanism, and it is why the scoping matters. Give away only what you can genuinely live with.
- Naming the students publicly turns one decision into a permanent, visible piece of evidence that the school listens.

## Script for the Leader

First meeting:

"Twelve of you, and I picked you deliberately rather than running an election, because I did not only want the people who already like it here. Here is the deal. The courtyard is yours to decide. There is two hundred pounds, it has to be safe, and it cannot need a teacher standing in it. That is the whole list of rules. I am not going to overrule what you decide, including if I think it is a bit daft, because if I did that once none of you would ever take this seriously again. And when it is done I am telling the whole school it was you."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not elect them. Elections select for confidence and popularity, which is the sample you least need.
- Scope the decision so you can genuinely live with any outcome. If there is an answer you cannot accept, say so before they start, not after.
- Include a student who is difficult. They are usually the most useful person in the room and it is the clearest signal about what this is for.
- Speak as little as possible. Count how often you talk in the first meeting and aim to halve it in the second.
- Implement within a month. A decision that takes a term to appear reads as a refusal.
- Give them a second decision after the first one lands. One is a gesture, a sequence is a structure.

## Display Idea

Whatever they change, with a small sign naming the students who decided it and the date. It is the cheapest possible evidence that this school does something with what students say.
