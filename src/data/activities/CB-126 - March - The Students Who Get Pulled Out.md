# Culture Builder: The Students Who Get Pulled Out (March)

Count what your most-supported students are missing to get the support.

## Objective

Work out what students are giving up in order to receive their services. Speech, reading intervention, counselling, language support, occupational therapy. Each is scheduled by a different adult solving a different timetabling puzzle, and nobody holds the total. The students receiving the most support are frequently the students missing the most of everything that makes school feel worth attending, and no one has ever added it up.

## Time Needed

25 minutes with whoever schedules services.

## Supplies / Setup

- The pull-out schedules, all of them, in one place for the first time.
- A blank timetable grid per student for the four or five with the most services.
- Your special education lead, EL lead and counselor, in the same room.
- The class timetable, so you can see what is happening when they leave.

## How It Works

1. **Build one grid per student (10 minutes).** Take the four or five students receiving the most services and map every pull-out onto a single week. This is usually the first time anyone has seen the total, because each service was scheduled in isolation.
2. **Write what they are missing, not just when (6 minutes).** Not "Tuesday 10:15". What is happening in the room at Tuesday 10:15. Then look for the pattern. It is remarkably often the same subject every week, or the class read-aloud, or the part of the day the class does something together.
3. **Look for who is missing break or lunch (3 minutes).** Some students are receiving services in the only unstructured social time they have. That trade is almost never made deliberately; it is what was left when the timetable was solved. It is also the trade most likely to cost a student their friendships.
4. **Ask the students (3 minutes to plan).** One question: what do you hate missing. They answer instantly and specifically, and it is usually not the academic subject an adult would guess.
5. **Change one thing, this term (3 minutes).** Rotate a pull-out so it does not hit the same lesson every week. Move a session off break. Group two services together so a student leaves the room once rather than three times. One structural change beats a conversation about how unfortunate it all is.

## Why It Works

- Every service is scheduled by a different person solving their own constraint, so nobody is holding the total. The total is the problem.
- Missing the same subject every week for a year is a compounding loss that shows up later as a gap nobody can explain.
- Being pulled from break trades academic support for social isolation, and it is usually an accident of scheduling rather than a decision anyone made.
- Asking students produces immediate, specific answers and takes about a minute each. Nobody asks them.
- Rotating a schedule costs nothing and removes the compounding, which makes it the highest-return change available here.

## Script for the Leader

To the scheduling team:

"I want to put all the pull-out schedules on one grid per child, because I do not think anyone in this building has ever seen the total for a single student. Each of you has solved your own puzzle well. What I am worried about is what it adds up to, and whether we have four kids who miss the same lesson every Tuesday all year without any of us noticing."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do the grids before the meeting if you can. The picture does the persuading and the room only has to decide what to change.
- Include counselling and any behaviour support in the count. They are pull-outs too and are frequently left off the tally.
- Ask the students directly and take the answer at face value. If a child says the thing they hate missing is the class story, that is the finding.
- Talk to families before changing anything with an IEP attached, and follow whatever your process requires.
- Watch for the student who is pulled out so often they have no consistent adult. That is a belonging problem produced by support.
- Recount in September, because next year's schedule will be built the same way unless someone holds the total again.

## Display Idea

None public. These grids name individual students and their services, so they stay with the team and are not wall material under any circumstances.
