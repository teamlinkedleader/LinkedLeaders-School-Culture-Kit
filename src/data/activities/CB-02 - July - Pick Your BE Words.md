# Culture Builder: Pick Your BE Words (July)

Twelve words that give your building a shared language, chosen in one sitting before anyone needs them.

## Objective

Choose one BE word per month for the year and build the wall before staff arrive. A BE word is a single plain-language behavior word, like BE Visible, BE Grateful or BE Reachable, that gives you and your staff a shared vocabulary for the month. It gives you something to say in announcements, something specific to recognize people for, and a hook to hang each month's culture work on.

## Time Needed

20–25 minutes.

## Supplies / Setup

- The default twelve-month grid below, printed or copied onto your paper.
- Your one-sentence culture statement from Week 1.
- Twelve half-sheets of cardstock, or one printed set.
- Wall or board space where staff actually walk: the lounge, the mailboxes, the copier. Not the front office.

**The default grid.** Start here and swap freely.

| Month | BE word | What the month demands |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| July | BE Ready | Preparation, before anyone is watching |
| August | BE Visible | Presence in hallways and classrooms |
| September | BE Consistent | Routines that hold once the newness wears off |
| October | BE Reachable | Being findable when the first hard things happen |
| November | BE Grateful | Naming people out loud |
| December | BE Patient & Kind | Grace through the tired weeks |
| January | BE Intentional | Restarting on purpose, not by default |
| February | BE Authentic & Real | Dropping the performance |
| March | BE Steady | Holding through the longest stretch |
| April | BE Generous | Giving credit away |
| May | BE Memorable | Ending in a way people carry |
| June | BE Proud | Naming what was actually built |

## How It Works

1. **Read the grid against your calendar (3 minutes).** Go month by month and ask one question: what does this month actually demand of my people? Not what sounds nice. What the month costs them.
2. **Swap what does not fit (8 minutes).** Two rules. Behavior words, not value words: BE Reachable, not BE Excellent. Nothing that sounds like it came off an evaluation rubric. One word, two at the most.
3. **Check each word against your sentence (5 minutes).** Read your Week 1 culture statement, then read your twelve words. If a word does not serve that sentence, it is decoration. Cut it and pick again.
4. **Make the wall (5 minutes).** Month and word, big. Only the current month goes up large. The other eleven live in a small column beside it so people can see what is coming and know this is a system, not a stunt.
5. **Set the reminders (2 minutes).** Twelve recurring calendar entries, first of each month: "Flip the BE word. Name two people who lived last month's."

## Why It Works

- Shared language is the cheapest culture tool there is. "That was a very BE Reachable thing you did for that family" does work that a paragraph of generic praise cannot.
- Behavior words are observable, which means recognition gets specific. Specific recognition is the only kind people believe.
- Choosing all twelve in July means you never have to invent one in a hurry in February, which is exactly when you would skip it.
- Posted by the copier beats laminated in the office. Culture lives where people already stand.
- The recurring calendar reminder is the whole difference between a system and a good intention. Most BE walls die in October because nobody scheduled the flip.

## Script for the Leader

"Every month this year has a word. It isn't a theme and it isn't a slogan, it's a behavior. It's the one thing I'm asking us to be intentional about that month. August is BE Visible, and that one's mine to model first. You should expect to see me in classrooms and hallways, not behind my office door. If you don't, call me on it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not announce all twelve at once. Reveal the month's word at the start of the month. The column on the wall does the foreshadowing for you.
- If a month goes sideways and the word stops fitting, change it. Announce the change out loud rather than quietly swapping the card. Naming it models the flexibility you want from them.
- Recognize classified staff against the words by name, early and often. If the custodian and the front office secretary never get named, staff will read the wall as a teacher thing and stop looking at it.
- Keep this page. Next July you will re-run this in ten minutes instead of twenty-five, because you will only be changing three or four words.
