# Culture Builder: How the Year Ends for Staff (May)

Design the last week so people leave carrying something other than exhaustion.

## Objective

Plan the final week for adults with the same care you plan the first one. The last week of school is usually a checkout list, a rushed meeting, and a sudden silence, and whatever a staff member feels walking out on that last day is what they carry through the summer and into their decision about coming back. This is 25 minutes that changes what they leave with.

## Time Needed

25 minutes to plan in May, plus about ten minutes per staff member in the last week.

## Supplies / Setup

- The current checkout list, in full, which is usually longer than anyone has audited.
- The last week's calendar, including any meetings scheduled into it.
- Your notes from January's stay conversations and April's re-recruiting.
- The Pass the Baton log from October, if you ran it, which is the year's record of what people did for each other.

## How It Works

1. **Cut the checkout list (6 minutes).** Go through it and remove anything that is habit rather than requirement, and anything that could have been done in April. Then publish the shortened version early enough that people can finish it before the last day rather than during it.
2. **Take one meeting out of the last week and put nothing in its place (3 minutes).** The end-of-year meeting that reviews next year's initiatives is the single most demoralising possible use of that week. Whatever it is, it can wait until August.
3. **Tell every staff member one specific thing, individually, before they leave (10 minutes each, across the week).** Not a card, not a group email, not a speech. Find them, and name one thing they did this year that mattered. For most people this is the only individual feedback they receive all year that is not evaluative.
4. **Say goodbye properly to whoever is leaving (4 minutes to plan).** Retirements, resignations, non-renewals, the paraprofessional whose position was cut. How you treat departures is watched extremely closely by the people staying, and it is read as a preview of how they would be treated. Name them, thank them specifically, and do not let anyone slip out unmentioned.
5. **Read something back on the last day (2 minutes).** The baton log, the year's wins from the January check, or the family hope cards from August's open house. Something in other people's words rather than yours, about what the year was.

## Why It Works

- The last day is disproportionately weighted in memory. Whatever the ending feels like colours the whole year in retrospect, and it is the emotional state in which people decide whether they want another one.
- Checkout lists expand by accretion and nobody has ever audited one. Cutting it is free relief at the exact moment relief is scarcest.
- An individual, specific, non-evaluative sentence from the leader is rare enough that people repeat it verbatim years later. It costs you ten minutes each.
- How departures are handled is the most closely watched thing a leader does. A colleague who leaves unmentioned tells everyone remaining exactly what their own departure would be worth.
- Ending in other people's words rather than the principal's avoids the trap of the last day becoming a speech about the leader's year.

## Script for the Leader

For the individual conversations:

"Before you go. In February when Ray's mother died and he was out for three weeks, you covered his classes without being asked and you never mentioned it to me. I found out from a kid. That is the kind of thing that decides what this place is actually like to work in, and it does not show up anywhere on your evaluation. Thank you. Have a real summer, and I will see you in August."

## Facilitator Tips

- Publish the shortened checkout list two weeks early. The relief lands better in advance than on the day.
- Do not schedule a next-year planning meeting into the last week. It is the most common and most costly mistake of the month.
- Do the individual conversations even for the staff you find difficult, and especially for them. That is the one people notice.
- Never let someone leave the profession or the building unnamed, including non-renewals. You can be honest and gracious at once.
- Do not use the last day to announce anything about next year. Anything announced then is heard through the filter of exhaustion.
- Write down what you said to each person. In August you will want to remember, and next May you will want not to repeat yourself.

## Display Idea

Nothing new. The last day is the moment to bring back what was already made: the baton log, the hope wall, the traditions charters, the six-things cards. A year of the building's own artefacts, read back on the way out.
