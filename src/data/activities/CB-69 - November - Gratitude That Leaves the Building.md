# Culture Builder: Gratitude That Leaves the Building (November)

Thank the people who serve your school from outside it, by name, in person.

## Objective

Identify every person outside your building who makes your school work, then thank them specifically and in person during November. Bus drivers, crossing guards, the district maintenance crew, the delivery drivers, the SRO, the nurse who splits four buildings, the volunteers, the neighbor who lets the cross country team run through their lot. These people are essential, invisible, and thanked by nobody, and fixing that costs you 25 minutes.

## Time Needed

25 minutes to build the list and write. Deliveries happen across the week.

## Supplies / Setup

- Chart paper and a genuine willingness to be surprised by how long the list is.
- Note cards.
- Your office staff and your head custodian in the room. They know who comes and goes far better than you do.
- Names. Not roles. "The bus drivers" is not a list, it is an excuse.

## How It Works

1. **Build the list with the people who actually know (10 minutes).** Sit with your office manager and your custodian and name everyone. Ask directly: who comes into this building or serves these kids who does not work for us? Who shows up before six? Who do we call when something breaks? Who has been doing this for years without anyone learning their name?
2. **Get the names right (5 minutes).** Chase down the actual name of every person on the list. This step is the entire activity. A thank-you addressed to "our bus drivers" is a poster. A thank-you addressed to Denise is a thank-you.
3. **Write specifically (7 minutes).** Say what they do and what it makes possible. "You have the second route and you get here at 6:40 even in January, and I have never once had to think about whether our kids would get here." Specific means you actually know what their job is, which most people thanking them do not.
4. **Deliver in person, in the place they work (across the week).** Go to the bus loop at 6:40. Walk out to the crossing guard. Catch the delivery driver at the dock. The delivery is the point, and doing it where they work rather than summoning them to your office is what makes it read as respect.
5. **Post the list where staff can see it (3 minutes).** Not the notes, the list of names. Most of your staff do not know these people exist either, and a posted list of the twenty people outside the building who make the place run is a quiet, useful education.

## Why It Works

- The people at the edge of a school are structurally invisible. They report to somebody else, they are not at the staff meeting, and no recognition system in your district includes them.
- Getting the name right is the difference between gratitude and a gesture, and everybody on the receiving end can tell instantly which one they got.
- Going to where they work removes the power dynamic. A principal walking out to a bus at 6:40 in November is saying something that a card in an envelope does not.
- Building the list with your office manager and custodian tells them that their knowledge of the building is worth something, which it is.
- Posting the list changes how staff and students treat these people for the rest of the year, at no additional cost.

## Script for the Leader

Delivered in person, briefly:

"Denise, I'm not here about anything. I just realized I've been principal here for two years and I've never once said thank you to you by name. You're here at 6:40 every morning and I have never had to wonder whether our kids would get to school. That's yours. Have a good Thanksgiving."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not make it a ceremony or invite them to an assembly. Most people on this list would rather be thanked at their truck than applauded in a gym.
- Include the people you do not personally like or find difficult. The list is about function, not affinity.
- Ask your custodian who you are forgetting after you think the list is finished. There will be two more.
- Do not photograph the deliveries for social media. If you want to post something, post the list of roles and thank them publicly without turning the individual moment into content.
- Repeat it, briefly, in May. These are also the people nobody says goodbye to in June.

## Display Idea

A posted list in the staff room and the front office, headed "People Who Make This School Work And Don't Work Here," first names and roles. It changes the way twenty adults get greeted for the rest of the year.
