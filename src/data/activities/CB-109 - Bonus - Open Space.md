# Culture Builder: Open Space (Bonus)

Hand the agenda to your staff and find out what they actually need to talk about.

## Objective

Run a meeting where the participants set the agenda in the first ten minutes and then run the sessions themselves. Open Space is the most uncomfortable facilitation method a school leader can use and the most informative, because what your staff choose to put on the wall is a direct readout of what is actually on their minds. Within the Collaborative Impact Framework this is the Clarify protocol for when you do not yet know what the problem is. Use it when you need the faculty to tell you what matters rather than confirming what you think matters.

## Time Needed

60 minutes. Ten to build the agenda, two rounds of about 20, and a close.

## Supplies / Setup

- A large blank wall, sticky notes or half sheets, and markers.
- Two or three spaces people can spread into, and a note-taker per session.
- Nothing pre-planned. No agenda, no slides, no topics of your own. That is the method.
- Your nerve, which is the actual prerequisite.

## Sample Prompts

The only prompt needed is the invitation:

- "What is one thing about this school that you want to spend twenty minutes talking about with whoever else cares about it?"

## How It Works

1. **Explain the four principles and the one law (5 minutes).** Whoever comes is the right people. Whatever happens is the only thing that could have. Whenever it starts is the right time. When it is over it is over. And the law of two feet: if you are not learning or contributing, go somewhere else, and that is not rude.
2. **Staff post topics and claim a time and place (10 minutes).** Anyone who wants to convene a session writes the topic on a sheet, says it out loud, and puts it on the wall against a slot. If nobody posts anything, wait. The silence is uncomfortable and it usually breaks in about ninety seconds.
3. **Everyone chooses where to go (2 minutes).** Nobody is assigned. Some sessions will have eight people and some will have two, and both are legitimate outcomes.
4. **Run two rounds of about twenty minutes.** Each session has a note-taker who captures what was discussed and anything the group wants to happen next. One page, no format.
5. **Post every page and say what you will do (10 minutes).** All the notes go on the wall. Then you respond to what emerged: what you can act on, what you cannot and why, and what needs a further conversation. Responding is the price of running this method.

## Why It Works

- What your staff put on that wall is the most honest agenda you will ever see, and it will not match the agenda you would have written.
- Self-selection means every session has people who genuinely care about that topic, which is why twenty minutes produces more than an hour of whole-group discussion.
- The law of two feet gives people explicit permission to leave, which removes the resentment that builds in mandatory sessions and surfaces which topics have real energy behind them.
- Small sessions are not failures. A topic two people care deeply about is worth twenty minutes and you would never have scheduled it.
- The leader having no topics is the whole signal. It communicates trust more loudly than any statement about trust.

## Script for the Leader

"I have no agenda today. That is not a joke and it is not me being lazy. Anyone who wants to spend twenty minutes on something about this school can write it on a sheet, say it out loud, and stick it on that wall. Then you all go wherever you want. If you are in a session and you are not getting anything out of it, get up and leave, and nobody will think you are rude. At the end I am going to read every page and tell you what I am going to do about it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Bring no topics of your own. If you seed the wall, you have run a normal meeting with extra steps.
- Sit through the initial silence. It is deeply uncomfortable and it always breaks.
- Do not attend the session about your own leadership, and expect there to be one. Ask for the notes like everybody else.
- Assign note-takers or you will lose the content. This is the most common way Open Space wastes itself.
- Respond within a week. The method generates more goodwill than almost anything else and burns it faster than anything else if the response never comes.
- Do not run it if you are not willing to hear the answer. Better to not ask than to ask and dismiss.

## Display Idea

The agenda wall itself, left up with all the session notes pinned under each topic, plus your written response added alongside within the week.
