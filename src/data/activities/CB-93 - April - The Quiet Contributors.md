# Culture Builder: The Quiet Contributors (April)

Find the people who have never once been named, and name them.

## Objective

Go back through the whole year's recognition, list who was named, and find the people who never were. Then thank those people specifically. Every school has a group of adults who do essential work and have not been mentioned once in a staff meeting, a newsletter, or an announcement all year. This takes 20 minutes and it produces a list that is uncomfortable to look at.

## Time Needed

20 minutes, in April, before Staff Appreciation Week planning starts.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your staff meeting agendas, newsletters, announcements, and social posts from the whole year.
- A complete staff list from payroll. Not from memory, for the same reason as always.
- Your Pass the Baton log from October, if you ran it, which is the best record you have of peer recognition.
- Twenty minutes and no interruptions.

## How It Works

1. **Build the list of who got named (8 minutes).** Go through the year's meetings, newsletters, announcements, and posts, and write down every adult who was publicly recognized. Tally repeats, because the repeats are half the finding.
2. **Subtract it from the payroll list (3 minutes).** What is left is the people who did a full year of work in your building without being mentioned once. Count them. In most schools it is between a quarter and a half of staff, and it skews hard toward classified roles and the quietly competent.
3. **Ask why each one is missing (4 minutes).** Not to assign blame. The honest answers are structural: they are not in the room where recognition happens, their work is invisible when it goes right, or they have never asked for anything. Notice that none of those are about the quality of their work.
4. **Thank them specifically, this month (5 minutes to plan).** Not a group thank-you and not a mention at a meeting they are not at. Individually, by name, for something real, in whatever form suits them. If you cannot name something specific for a person, go find out this week rather than writing something generic.
5. **Fix the mechanism, not just this year (2 minutes).** Whatever your recognition routine is, add the step that catches this: rotate through the payroll list, or check the list quarterly, or ask a colleague to tell you who you are missing. Otherwise you will run this exact audit next April and find the same names.

## Why It Works

- Recognition in schools flows toward visibility, not contribution. The people in the meetings get named; the people covering lunch, driving the bus, fixing the boiler, and answering the phone do not.
- The audit is the activity. Most leaders are certain they recognize people broadly and are genuinely shaken by the subtraction, and that reaction is what changes behaviour.
- Work that is invisible when it goes right is systematically under-recognized, which describes almost every operational role in a school.
- Individual and specific is what makes it land. A group thank-you to "our amazing support staff" is heard as being thanked for a category rather than for anything you did.
- Doing it in April, before Staff Appreciation Week, means the week gets planned around the people who need it rather than defaulting to teachers again.

## Script for the Leader

For the staff meeting after you have done the thanking:

"I went back through every meeting agenda, newsletter and announcement from this year and listed everybody we named out loud. Then I put it next to the payroll list. Nineteen people in this building have not been mentioned once all year. Not for a lack of contribution, mostly because their work is invisible when it goes right. That is my failure and I have spent this week fixing it one conversation at a time. I have also changed how I do this, so I do not stand here next April saying the same thing."

## Facilitator Tips

- Pull the staff list from payroll. Building it from memory reproduces exactly the bias you are auditing.
- Count the repeats too. Discovering that four people were named eleven times is as useful as discovering that nineteen were named zero.
- Do not fix the whole list in one announcement. Nineteen names read aloud is a list, not recognition.
- Ask a colleague who is not an administrator to check your list for names you still missed. There will be some.
- Include the people who are technically employed by the district rather than the school. They are the most invisible of all and they are in your building every day.
- Keep the list. Next April, the comparison is the measure of whether you actually changed the mechanism.

## Display Idea

None for the audit itself. What is worth making visible is the change to your routine, so that staff know recognition is now systematic rather than dependent on who you happened to notice.
