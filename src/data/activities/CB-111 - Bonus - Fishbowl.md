# Culture Builder: Fishbowl (Bonus)

A few people talk properly while everyone else listens, then they swap.

## Objective

Have a real conversation about something difficult in front of the whole staff, with four or five people in an inner circle actually talking and everyone else listening and taking notes. It solves the specific problem that a whole-staff discussion of forty people is not a discussion, it is a series of statements, and the same four voices make all of them. Within the Collaborative Impact Framework this spans Clarify and Collaborate, and it is the protocol for when the group is too large for the real conversation to happen at all.

## Time Needed

30 minutes. Two or three rounds with rotations.

## Supplies / Setup

- Four or five chairs in an inner circle, everyone else seated around them.
- One clear question, chosen in advance.
- Paper for the outer circle. They are working, not watching.
- A first inner circle chosen deliberately, including at least one person who rarely speaks in staff meetings.

## Sample Prompts

Works best on questions with genuine disagreement rather than questions with a right answer:

- "What are we actually doing about the students who have stopped trying?"
- "Where is our discipline approach not working, and for whom?"
- "What do new staff need in their first month that they are not getting?"
- "What are we pretending is fine?"

## How It Works

1. **Choose the first inner circle on purpose (5 minutes of prep).** Four or five people, mixed by role and tenure, including someone whose view the room rarely hears. Tell them beforehand so they are not ambushed.
2. **Set the outer circle a job (2 minutes).** They are not an audience. Their job is to write down what they hear: where the agreement is, where the disagreement is, and what nobody is saying. Having a task is what keeps forty people engaged.
3. **Inner circle talks for about ten minutes.** A genuine conversation, not statements to the room. The outer circle stays silent, including you.
4. **Rotate, once or twice (10 minutes).** Swap two or three people out and new ones in. The incoming people carry the conversation forward rather than restarting it, which is worth saying out loud.
5. **Outer circle reports what they heard (8 minutes).** This is the most valuable part and it is routinely cut for time. Ask specifically for what nobody said. Observers reliably notice the avoided subject, and naming it is usually the actual outcome of the session.

## Why It Works

- Four or five people can have a real conversation. Forty cannot, and pretending otherwise is why whole-staff discussions produce so little.
- Listening is a genuinely different activity from waiting to speak, and most staff never get to do it in a meeting.
- The observer task converts the majority from passive to analytical, and their read on the conversation is frequently sharper than the conversation.
- Rotation stops the inner circle becoming a panel of the usual voices, and it lets a quiet person contribute in a small group rather than to a room.
- "What did nobody say" is the highest-yield question available, and only the observers can answer it.

## Script for the Leader

"Five chairs in the middle. Those five are going to have an actual conversation about this, the kind you can only have with five people. Everybody else is not an audience: your job is to write down where they agree, where they disagree, and what nobody is saying. In ten minutes we swap some people in. And at the end I want to hear from the outside, especially the part about what did not get said, because you will have noticed it and they will not have."

## Facilitator Tips

- Stay in the outer circle. A leader in the fishbowl turns a conversation into a performance for the leader.
- Warn your first inner circle in advance. Being pulled into the middle unprepared is a bad experience and it will make everyone wary next time.
- Give the outer circle a written task. Without it, half of them disengage in four minutes.
- Protect the report-back. It is the first thing to get cut when the clock runs down and it is where the value concentrates.
- Choose a question with real disagreement in it. A fishbowl on a question everyone agrees about is a waste of thirty minutes.
- Follow up on whatever the observers named as unsaid. Surfacing it and then ignoring it is worse than never asking.

## Display Idea

The observers' notes, collected and posted anonymously under three headings: where we agree, where we disagree, and what we are not saying. That third column is the useful one.
