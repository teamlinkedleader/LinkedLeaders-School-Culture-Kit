# Culture Builder: The Chip Bowl (September)

Four chips in your pocket every morning, four real conversations before you go home.

## Objective

Guarantee that every adult in your building gets an unscripted, non-business conversation with you on a rotation you cannot fudge. Write every staff member's name on a poker chip, keep them in a bowl on your desk, and pull three or four every morning. Those are your people for the day. The bowl removes the thing that quietly wrecks staff relationships in every school, which is that leaders unconsciously talk to the same fifteen people all year.

## Time Needed

Thirty seconds to draw. The conversations take about five minutes total, and they happen inside the day you already have.

## Supplies / Setup

- One chip per staff member, every staff member, with their name on it. Poker chips if you own a set. Popsicle sticks, wooden nickels, or card stock circles work identically and cost nothing.
- A bowl that lives somewhere you cannot avoid it. Next to your keys, next to your radio, on top of the thing you pick up every morning.
- A second, empty bowl for the chips you have used.
- A marker.

## How It Works

1. **Write every name, including the ones you would forget (10 minutes of setup).** Teachers, paraprofessionals, office staff, custodians, food service, bus drivers if they are yours, the part-time counselor who is in the building on Tuesdays. If the list is short, the bowl is lying to you.
2. **Pull three or four every morning (30 seconds).** First thing, before anything else touches your day. In your pocket, not on your desk. The chip in your pocket is the reminder, and you will feel it.
3. **Find them and talk about nothing (through the day).** Unscripted is the entire point. No feedback, no ask, no "while I have you." Ask about the kid, the trip, the thing they mentioned in August, the sport their daughter plays. Two minutes is plenty.
4. **Move the chip to the second bowl (5 seconds).** Only after the conversation actually happened. A chip you carried but never spent goes back in your pocket for tomorrow. Do not fudge this, because the second bowl is the only honest record you will have.
5. **Empty the bowl before you refill it (monthly).** When the first bowl runs out, everyone in your building has had a real conversation with you. Note the date, dump the second bowl back into the first, and start over. If the bowl takes six weeks to empty, that is your actual data on how available you are.

## Why It Works

- Every leader has a default rotation of about fifteen people, usually the ones nearest their office and the ones who seek them out. The bowl is a mechanical fix for a bias you cannot see in yourself.
- Randomness protects the staff member who never gets your time. Being pulled from a bowl feels like luck. Being scheduled for a check-in feels like a problem.
- Unscripted and non-business is what makes it land. The moment a chip conversation carries an agenda, staff learn the chips mean trouble, and you have built a surveillance system by accident.
- Three or four a day is small enough to survive a bad week, which is what makes it a habit instead of an initiative.
- The two bowls make your consistency visible to you. A full second bowl in three weeks is proof. A stalled one is a mirror.

## Script for the Leader

You do not announce this to staff, and that is deliberate. If someone asks what the chips are:

"Every one of you is on a chip in that bowl. I pull a few every morning and those are the people I make sure I actually talk to that day, about anything except work. I did it because I noticed I was talking to the same handful of people all the time and it wasn't on purpose. When the bowl's empty I've talked to everybody, and then I start over."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not use it to deliver anything. Not a reminder, not a nudge, not a small correction. One weaponized chip and the whole thing is finished.
- If you pull a chip for someone you are in conflict with, still go. Especially go. But keep it exactly as light as every other chip.
- Keep the bowl visible. Staff notice it, ask about it, and the answer is a good one.
- Assistant principals should run their own bowl with their own chips rather than splitting yours. Staff track whether *you* came.
- The tell that it is working: staff start telling each other they got pulled. When it becomes a small good thing to be pulled, you are done building it.
- Do not digitize this. A spreadsheet with a rotation reminder is technically the same system and it will not survive two weeks, because nothing in your pocket is buzzing at you.

## Display Idea

The two bowls sit on your desk in the open, in front of anyone who comes in. The one that is filling up is the one that says something about you.
