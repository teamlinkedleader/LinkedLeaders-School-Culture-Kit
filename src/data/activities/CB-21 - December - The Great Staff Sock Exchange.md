# Culture Builder: The Great Staff Sock Exchange (December)

Bring some lighthearted fun to your next faculty meeting. Quick, easy, lots of laughs, and everyone walks away with something cozy.

## Objective

Run a low-stakes gift exchange that produces laughter in the month when staff energy is at its lowest. The gratitude element is what keeps it from being purely silly, and the photograph is what makes it last past the meeting.

## Time Needed

20 to 25 minutes at a faculty meeting.

## Supplies / Setup

- One pair of new, wrapped socks per person. Funny, festive, fuzzy, themed, the more creative the better.
- A big basket.
- Numbers to draw for the order.
- A phone for the group photo.

## Sample Prompts

- **Warm-up as people arrive:** "If you were a pair of socks, what pattern would you be today?" Silly answers encouraged.
- **Before each person opens:** "Something warm that happened this week."

## How It Works

1. **Set the invite** and be explicit about the price ceiling, so nobody escalates. Ask each staff member to bring one pair of new, wrapped socks.
2. **Warm-up check-in.** As people arrive they put their wrapped socks in the basket and answer the sock-pattern question. It is deliberately ridiculous and it does the job.
3. **The exchange.** Everyone stands in a circle, draw numbers for order, and on your turn choose one wrapped pair from the basket.
4. **Add the gratitude twist.** Before opening, each person shares a small warm thing that happened that week. This is the difference between a sock exchange and a culture activity.
5. **Show and share.** Once everything is open, take a group photo of everyone holding up their new swag, and crown a few lighthearted titles: Coziest Socks, Most Outrageous Socks, Most Likely to Violate Dress Code.

## Why It Works

- Low prep and high joy, in the month where elaborate ideas die.
- Naturally sparks conversation and laughter without requiring anyone to be vulnerable or perform.
- Builds connection during a season when staff energy dips and the calendar is at its most relentless.
- The gratitude element keeps it meaningful rather than just silly, and it costs nothing to add.

## Script for the Leader

"Everybody brings one pair of wrapped socks to Thursday's meeting. Cheap and ridiculous is the target, not thoughtful and expensive. We're drawing numbers, picking from the basket, and before you open yours you have to tell us one warm thing that happened this week. Then we're taking a photo of all of us holding up our socks, which I fully intend to use against you in June."

## Facilitator Tips

- **On cost:** this one asks staff to buy something, which breaks the collection's usual free rule. Name a hard low ceiling, say a few dollars, and mean it, or it escalates and becomes a burden on the people who can least afford it. **No-cost version:** everyone brings a wrapped pair of socks they already own and have never worn, which is a drawer most people have, and it is funnier.
- Make participation genuinely optional and say so once, plainly. Someone will be having a bad December.
- Bring spares. There will be someone who forgot, and they should not have to sit out.
- Do not let the titles get pointed. Affectionate only, and you are the one who has to steer it.
- Take the photo. It is the artefact and it takes ten seconds.
