# Culture Builder: One Thing By March (January)

Every student names one specific thing they will do by March, and one adult who knows about it.

## Objective

Have every student set one small, specific, checkable goal with a March deadline and give it to an adult who will ask about it. Not New Year's resolutions, which are annual, vague, and dead by the 15th. One thing, eight weeks, and a named human who knows. The adult is the mechanism; the goal is almost secondary.

## Time Needed

20 minutes of class time in the first two weeks of January. About 10 minutes of leader prep.

## Supplies / Setup

- One index card per student.
- A March date, decided in advance and the same building-wide, so the follow-through is scheduled rather than hoped for.
- Your own card, filled in, with your own goal on it.
- A plan for where the cards live for eight weeks. The adult keeps them, not the student.

## How It Works

1. **Go first, with something real and slightly uncomfortable (5 minutes of prep, then shown in every room).** Write your own card and put it where students see it. Make it specific and make it something you could genuinely fail at. "Learn 100 more student names by March 6th" beats "be a better principal." Students calibrate the whole activity off how honest yours is.
2. **Set the three rules (3 minutes of the block).** It has to be small enough to do in eight weeks. It has to be checkable, so somebody could tell whether it happened. And it cannot be about being a better person in general. "Be nicer" is out. "Sit with someone new at lunch twice a week" is in.
3. **Write the card (7 minutes).** Three lines: the one thing, the date, and the name of the adult in this building who will ask. Students choose the adult, and they do not have to choose their teacher.
4. **The adult accepts it out loud (5 minutes).** Cards get delivered to the chosen adults, who say some version of "I've got it, I'll ask you in March" directly to that student. The acceptance is the part that makes it real, and it is the part that gets skipped when cards go in a folder.
5. **Adults hold the cards and ask in March (5 minutes, in March).** On the named date, every adult finds their students and asks. Whether the goal happened is genuinely not the point. Being asked is the point, and a student who missed their goal but got asked has learned something better than one who hit it unnoticed.

## Why It Works

- A goal with a named human attached behaves completely differently from a goal written on a worksheet. The accountability is relational, which is the only kind that works on adolescents.
- Eight weeks is short enough to stay real. A goal aimed at June is abstract by February.
- Letting students pick the adult surfaces the relationship map of your building for free. Which adults get chosen, and which students cannot think of anyone, is information you cannot get any other way.
- The leader's card being genuinely fallible is what sets the honesty level. A principal's goal that is safe produces thirty safe goals.
- The March follow-through is the whole activity. A goal-setting exercise nobody returns to teaches students that adults ask for things and then forget, which is a worse lesson than not asking.

## Script for the Leader

For the announcement:

"Everybody in this building writes down one thing they're going to do by March 6th. Small, specific, and something a person could actually check. Mine's on my door: I'm learning 100 more of your names by March 6th, and right now I genuinely don't know if I'll make it. You pick one adult here who'll hold your card and ask you about it. They don't have to be your teacher. And on March 6th they're going to come find you, whether you did it or not."

## Facilitator Tips

- Watch for the student who cannot name an adult. That is the most important thing this activity produces, and it needs a quiet fix that week, not a note in a file.
- Do not grade the cards, collect them centrally, or put them in a data system. The moment it becomes tracked it becomes an assignment.
- Watch the distribution of chosen adults. If four adults hold two hundred cards, you have learned something about your building's relationship load.
- Help students shrink goals rather than rejecting them. "Get better at math" becomes "ask one question in math class every week."
- Put the March date in every adult's calendar yourself. Left to memory, half the follow-through evaporates.
- If a student's goal is about something outside school, that is fine and often better. Let it stand.

## Display Idea

Nothing on a wall while it runs. In March, once the conversations are done, students who want to can post their card with a mark for done or not done. Both marks displayed side by side is the point, and it only works if the not-done cards are genuinely welcome.
