# Culture Builder: Staff Appreciation Week, Planned in April (April)

Ask them what they actually want, then give them time instead of trinkets.

## Objective

Plan Staff Appreciation Week in April, from what staff actually asked for, and build it around time rather than objects. The default version is a themed day, a branded pen, a decorated door, and a cafeteria lunch, all of which cost money and land as a gesture. The versions that land cost nothing and give something back. This is 25 minutes and one anonymous question.

## Time Needed

25 minutes to plan in April. Two minutes of staff time to answer the question.

## Supplies / Setup

- One anonymous question sent to staff two weeks out.
- The master schedule, so you can see where time can actually be found.
- Your admin team, your office manager, and whoever normally organises this, in the room.
- No budget assumed. If money appears, it is a bonus rather than the plan.

## How It Works

1. **Ask the question two weeks out, anonymously (2 minutes to send).** One question: "If we did one thing for you that week, what would actually help?" Anonymous, free text, and say explicitly that "nothing, please just leave me alone" is a legitimate answer, because for some staff it is the honest one.
2. **Read the answers before planning anything (5 minutes).** They will cluster, and they will be about time. Cover my duty. Let me leave at contract time without it being noticed. Cancel a meeting. Give me my planning period back. Do not make me stand up in front of people.
3. **Build three of the five days out of time (8 minutes).** Duty covered by administrators all week. One meeting cancelled outright. An early release or a late start if you can get it. These cost nothing and they are the ones people remember, because they are the only ones that reduce the load rather than adding to it.
4. **Make the other two days genuinely no-cost and no-obligation (5 minutes).** Food someone donates, a note, a small thing done for them. The rule is that nothing on the plan requires staff to attend, dress up, perform, compete, or reciprocate. Appreciation with a participation requirement is another task.
5. **Check the week against the invisible staff (5 minutes).** Go down the payroll list from the Quiet Contributors audit and ask whether each person is actually reached by this plan. A week built around teacher planning periods does nothing for the custodian or the front office.

## Why It Works

- What exhausted people want is less work, and almost every default appreciation activity adds a small amount of work while signalling gratitude.
- Asking anonymously gets you the honest answer. Asked publicly, staff say something modest and gracious that is not what they want.
- Time given back is the only form of appreciation that is also relief. It is also free, which means it does not compete with the budget.
- Removing participation requirements matters more than it sounds. For an introverted or overloaded staff member, a week of optional-but-expected events is a week of dread.
- Planning in April rather than the week before is what makes the schedule changes possible. Coverage and early releases need lead time; pens do not.

## Script for the Leader

For the anonymous question:

"Staff Appreciation Week is the first week of May. Before anybody plans anything, one anonymous question: if we did one thing for you that week, what would actually help? Be blunt. 'Cover my duty' is a real answer. So is 'cancel the Thursday meeting.' So is 'nothing, please just leave me alone,' and if that is your answer I will take it seriously and you will be left alone."

## Facilitator Tips

- Administrators cover duty all week, personally. That single move outperforms everything else on a typical plan and it is visible every day.
- Do not make anyone attend anything. Not a breakfast, not an assembly, not a photo.
- Kill the theme days that require staff to buy or wear something. They are the same closet-test problem as spirit week, applied to adults, several of whom are on tight budgets.
- If food happens, make sure it reaches people who cannot leave their post. The kitchen staff and the front office rarely get to the staff room while the food is still there.
- Read the anonymous answers yourself and do not skip the sour ones. The person who wrote something bitter is telling you something accurate.
- Whatever the three time-based things are, do them again in November. Appreciation in the month it is scheduled is a formality; appreciation in a random hard month is real.

## Display Idea

Post the week's plan in advance with the three time-based items at the top, so people can see that the week is built out of relief rather than events, and can plan around the coverage.
