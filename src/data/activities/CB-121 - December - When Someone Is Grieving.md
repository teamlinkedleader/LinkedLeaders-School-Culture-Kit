# Culture Builder: When Someone Is Grieving (December)

Decide now what your school does when someone loses somebody, so you are not deciding it that morning.

## Objective

Write down what your school does when a student loses a parent, a staff member loses someone, or the community loses a child. Not a crisis policy for the building. The human part: who makes contact, what gets said, what happens on the day they come back, and what happens three months later when everyone else has moved on. Every school improvises this, and improvising it while shaken is how people get hurt by a school that meant well.

## Time Needed

25 minutes with your leadership team and counselor, in a calm week.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your counselor or social worker, who has almost certainly done this before and been given no framework.
- An honest recollection of the last loss the school handled, and what went wrong.
- Whatever your district requires, so you know what is mandated and what is yours to decide.
- Somewhere to keep the result that a new leader could find.

## How It Works

1. **Recall the last one honestly (6 minutes).** What actually happened. Who found out first, how did the information travel, who was left telling thirty children something they had not been prepared to say, and who was forgotten. Almost every school has a story here where somebody found out from a student. Name it without blame.
2. **Decide who makes the first contact, by name (4 minutes).** One person. Not "the school". A named human who calls or visits within 24 hours, and a named backup for when that person is the one grieving.
3. **Write what happens on the day they return (6 minutes).** This is the part schools get most wrong. Who meets them at the door. Whether the class has been told, and by whom, before they walk in. Where they can go if they need to leave a room, and who they tell. Whether staff have been briefed to say something rather than nothing, because a corridor of adults avoiding eye contact is its own injury.
4. **Write what happens at three months and at the anniversary (5 minutes).** Everyone is kind for a fortnight. The hard part starts when the casseroles stop. Put a name and a date in a calendar now for a check-in at three months, and note the anniversary, because the school forgetting it is the thing people remember for the rest of their lives.
5. **Agree the two sentences (4 minutes).** What a staff member says when they do not know what to say. Something plain: "I heard about your dad. I am so sorry. I am not going to make you talk about it, but I wanted you to know I know." Rehearse it out loud. Adults freeze because they are afraid of saying the wrong thing, and a rehearsed sentence prevents the silence that reads as indifference.

## Why It Works

- Decisions taken during a crisis are taken by people with no capacity, and they default to doing nothing because doing nothing requires no decision.
- The day someone returns is the moment the school either helps or harms, and it is entirely designable in advance.
- Three months out is where schools fail almost universally. A diary entry made today is the whole intervention.
- Giving staff a sentence removes the freeze. Most adults say nothing not because they do not care but because they are frightened of getting it wrong.
- December is a deliberate month for this. Grief is heaviest around the holidays, and a plan written in a calm week is worth ten written in a hard one.

## Script for the Leader

Introducing it to the team:

"We are going to write down what this school does when someone loses somebody. Not the safeguarding policy, the human part. I want to do it now, in a quiet week, because the last time it happened I made three decisions in ten minutes while I was shaken and I got at least one of them wrong. A boy in Year 5 found out his classmate's mother had died because he overheard two adults in a corridor. That was on me and I am not doing it again."

## Facilitator Tips

- Put your counselor at the centre of writing this. They have done it before and have almost certainly never been asked to formalise it.
- Name individuals, not roles. "The pastoral lead" fails on the day the pastoral lead is the person grieving.
- Include staff loss, not only student loss. Schools are far better at supporting a bereaved child than a bereaved colleague.
- Do not write anything that requires a grieving family to ask for it. Anything gated behind a request will not be requested.
- Diarise the three-month check the day it happens, not later.
- Review it once a year and never in the week you need it.

## Display Idea

None. This is not wall material. It lives in the staff handbook and somewhere a successor will find it, because the worst version of this is a new leader improvising the same mistakes five years from now.
