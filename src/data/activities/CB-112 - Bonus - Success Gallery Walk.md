# Culture Builder: Success Gallery Walk (Bonus)

Every team posts one thing that is genuinely working, and everyone leaves with one thing to steal.

## Objective

Get the practices that are already working in your building out of individual classrooms and into general circulation. Schools are full of solved problems that nobody else knows about, because the person who solved it does not consider it noteworthy. The gallery format moves it without requiring anyone to present, which is the barrier. Within the Collaborative Impact Framework this spans Collaborate and Commit: it surfaces what already works and ends with each person naming one practice they will adopt and when.

## Time Needed

30 to 40 minutes.

## Supplies / Setup

- Chart paper or a sheet per team, prepared in advance to a fixed format.
- Wall space enough for every sheet to be read simultaneously.
- Sticky notes for questions.
- Your own sheet, on something you have changed about how you lead.

## Sample Prompts

Each sheet answers the same four things, and the format matters because it makes the sheets comparable:

- What we tried.
- What problem it solved.
- What it costs, in time or money.
- What we would tell someone starting it Monday.

## How It Works

1. **Assign the sheets a week ahead with the fixed format (5 minutes of prep).** Ask for something small and real that is working now. Explicitly rule out initiatives and rule in the mundane: a routine, a phrase, a seating change, a way of handling a particular parent conversation.
2. **Post everything and walk in silence, 12 minutes.** No presenting. People read at their own pace and write questions on sticky notes and leave them on the sheets. Silence is what makes it fast and what lets people actually read rather than listen.
3. **Second pass for conversation, 10 minutes.** Now people find the author of whatever interested them and talk. This is where the transfer happens, and it happens in pairs rather than to the room.
4. **Everyone writes down the one thing they are taking, 5 minutes.** One practice, named, with when they will try it. Not three. One, with a date.
5. **Close by naming what appeared more than once, 5 minutes.** If four teams have independently solved the same problem, you have found something worth making school-wide, and you have found it from evidence rather than from a decision.

## Why It Works

- The best practices in your building are already there and unshared, because the people using them do not think of them as practices.
- Removing the presentation removes the barrier. Most staff will write a sheet and would never volunteer to present.
- Silence during the first pass means forty people read twelve sheets in twelve minutes, which no presenting format can match.
- Requiring the cost on every sheet kills the answer that only works with resources nobody else has.
- Committing to exactly one thing with a date is what converts a pleasant session into a change. Three commitments is zero commitments.

## Script for the Leader

"Every team has a sheet up, and mine is up there too. Four things on each: what you tried, what it fixed, what it costs, and what you would tell someone starting it Monday. Nobody presents. First twelve minutes you read in silence and leave questions on stickies. Then go find whoever wrote the one you care about and talk to them. Before you leave, write down the one thing you are stealing and when you are going to try it. One thing, not three, because three means none."

## Facilitator Tips

- Rule out initiatives explicitly in the ask, or you will get a wall of programmes rather than a wall of usable practices.
- Post your own sheet and make it about your own behaviour rather than a school-wide policy.
- Enforce the silence in the first pass. It feels strange for about ninety seconds and then it works.
- Insist on the cost line. Practices that only work with a full-time aide are not transferable and should be labelled as such.
- Collect the one-thing commitments and follow up in a month, lightly, with a question rather than a check.
- Keep the sheets. Next year's version starts from them and you can see which ones survived.

## Display Idea

Leave the whole gallery up for two weeks with the sticky-note questions still attached, and add the answers next to them. The questions and answers together are more useful than the original sheets.
