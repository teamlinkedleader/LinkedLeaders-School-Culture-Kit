# Culture Builder: The New Kid in January (January)

Build the arrival routine for the student who joins when every friendship group is already closed.

## Objective

Write one routine that every mid-year arrival gets, so it does not depend on which adult happens to be at the desk that morning. A student arriving in January walks into a school where every friendship group formed in September, every routine is assumed, and nobody explains anything because everybody already knows it. Many of them are also arriving because something went wrong: a move, a placement, a family breaking up. They are the most likely students in your building to disappear, and the arrival routine is the cheapest intervention you have.

## Time Needed

20 minutes to design. About 15 minutes per arrival to run.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your enrolment process as it currently stands, honestly described.
- The counselor, and whoever in the office actually handles new families.
- A simple one-page routine anyone can follow.
- Nothing bought. This is all structure.

## How It Works

1. **Describe what happens now, truthfully (5 minutes).** Walk it through end to end. In most schools a family arrives, does paperwork, and a child is walked to a classroom and introduced to a teacher mid-lesson. That is the whole arrival. Say it out loud, because it sounds worse said out loud, which is the point.
2. **Give them a day before the first day if you can (3 minutes).** Twenty minutes the afternoon before: see the room, find the toilets, meet the teacher with no audience, work out where to go at break. Almost all first-day panic is logistical, and it is solvable the day before for free.
3. **Assign two classmates, not one (4 minutes).** One is a single point of failure and also a burden. Two students, chosen by the teacher, briefed properly: sit with them at lunch for a week, walk them to specialist lessons, tell them the unwritten rules. Two means it survives an absence and it stops being one child's job.
4. **Assign one adult who is not their teacher (3 minutes).** Somebody who says their name in a corridor for the first month. The teacher is already responsible; this is a second, no-stakes adult, and it is exactly the thing that stops a student vanishing.
5. **Check in at two weeks with the student, not about them (5 minutes).** Two minutes, in person, asking one real question: who do you sit with at lunch. The answer tells you more than any settling-in form, and if the answer is "nobody" you have found it in week two rather than in March.

## Why It Works

- Mid-year arrivals are among the highest-risk students in any school, and often arrive because something has already gone wrong at home.
- Almost every barrier on day one is logistical rather than social, and logistics can be solved the afternoon before at no cost.
- Two buddies rather than one is the difference between a system and a favour. It survives absence, and it does not make one child responsible for another's wellbeing.
- A second adult with no stake is what catches the student who is quietly not settling. Their teacher is too close and too busy to see it.
- Asking who they sit with at lunch is the single most informative question available, and no form asks it.

## Script for the Leader

To the two assigned classmates:

"I am asking you two because you are good at this. Priya starts Monday and she does not know a single person here. For the next week, she sits with you at lunch, you walk her to music and PE, and you tell her the stuff nobody tells you, like which door is always locked and what actually happens at break. You are not babysitting her, you are just making sure she is not on her own while she works this place out. I will check in with you on Friday."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do the day-before visit whenever you can. It removes most of the first-day fear and costs twenty minutes.
- Brief the buddies properly and thank them afterwards. An unbriefed buddy waves at the new student once and considers the job done.
- Never introduce a new student to the class as a curiosity. Say their name, say one ordinary true thing, and move on.
- Ask the family what the child is worried about, because it is usually specific and usually fixable.
- Watch the second month, not the first. New students are novel in week one and forgotten by week five.
- This pairs directly with Every Student Claimed. Add every mid-year arrival to that wall the day they enrol.
