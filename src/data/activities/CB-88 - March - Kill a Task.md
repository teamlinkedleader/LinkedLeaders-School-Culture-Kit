# Culture Builder: Kill a Task (March)

Let staff name the work that has stopped mattering, then actually delete it forever.

## Objective

Ask your staff which recurring task they would delete if they could, then delete two of them permanently. Not paused for a month, not made optional. Gone, in writing, and never coming back. Schools accumulate work the way houses accumulate boxes, and nobody has ever been given permission to throw any of it out. March is when the weight of it is most obvious.

## Time Needed

25 minutes in a staff meeting.

## Supplies / Setup

- Chart paper and markers, or sticky notes if you want it anonymous, and in most buildings you want it anonymous.
- Your own honest list of things you require that you suspect are pointless.
- Whoever can tell you what is genuinely mandated by the district or the state, because half of what people believe is required is not.
- The authority to actually kill something, and the willingness to use it in the room.

## How It Works

1. **Name one of your own first (4 minutes).** Say a thing you have been requiring that you now think is a waste, and kill it out loud at the start of the meeting. Weekly lesson plan submissions nobody reads. A form that duplicates another form. The meeting that could be a message. Going first is what makes people believe the exercise is real rather than a survey.
2. **Ask the question plainly, on sticky notes (7 minutes).** "What do you do every week or every month that you believe does not help a single student?" Anonymous, because the honest answers are about things somebody in the room introduced.
3. **Sort into three piles out loud (6 minutes).** Actually mandated and cannot go. Not mandated but serves a real purpose. Not mandated and serves nobody. Check the first pile properly, because a surprising amount of what schools believe is required is local habit wearing a district costume.
4. **Kill two from the third pile, in the room (5 minutes).** Say which two, say they are gone permanently, and write them down with the date. Two real deletions beat a list of twelve candidates for review.
5. **Write the deletion down and tell whoever else needs to know (3 minutes).** A killed task that lives only in people's memory comes back in September when someone new asks why nobody is doing it. Put it in writing, with the reason, where next year's version of this staff can find it.

## Why It Works

- Every school is carrying work that was introduced for a reason that expired, and nobody kills it because killing it requires someone with authority to admit it was never worth much.
- Asking staff rather than auditing yourself gets a better list. They know which tasks are theatre because they perform the theatre.
- The mandated pile is smaller than everyone assumes. Checking it properly is often the single largest source of reclaimed time in the meeting.
- Permanent beats paused. A suspended task hangs over people and returns; a deleted one is genuinely gone and the relief is real.
- March is the right month because the accumulated weight is maximally visible and there is still enough year left for the relief to matter.

## Script for the Leader

"I'll start. Weekly lesson plans submitted to me every Friday. I have not read them properly since October, and asking for something I don't read is a waste of your Sunday and an insult besides. That's dead as of today, permanently, and I'm not replacing it with anything. Now your turn. Sticky notes, anonymous, one question: what do you do every week that you don't believe helps a single kid? We're killing two of them before anybody leaves this room."

## Facilitator Tips

- Kill something of your own first, and make it something people actually resent. Killing a task nobody minded is a tell.
- Verify the mandates rather than accepting them. Ask the district office directly about the two most-cited ones; the answer is often that nobody has required it for years.
- Do not promise to review the rest. A review list is where this goes to die. Two deletions today beats twelve maybes.
- Watch for the task somebody in the room introduced and is proud of. Killing that one is the test of whether this was real, and how you handle that person matters more than the task.
- Write it down the same day, including why. Institutional memory in schools is about eighteen months long.
- Re-run it every March. It never stops producing, because the accumulation never stops either.

## Display Idea

A short list in the workroom headed "Things We Stopped Doing," with the date each one died. It grows by two a year and it is a better statement of what a school values than any mission statement.
