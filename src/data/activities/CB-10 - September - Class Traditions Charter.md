# Culture Builder: Class Traditions Charter (September)

Every classroom names three things students can count on every single week.

## Objective

September is where the year either finds a rhythm or spends the next nine months improvising. This activity has every class name two or three small traditions they will repeat weekly, then post them where students can see them. The point is not novelty. The point is repetition, because a tradition is just a promise a school keeps often enough that kids stop bracing for it to disappear.

## Time Needed

20 minutes of class time in one advisory or homeroom block. About 15 minutes of leader prep the week before.

## Supplies / Setup

- One sheet of chart paper or a half-poster per classroom. Whatever you already have.
- Markers.
- Your one-sentence culture statement, visible in the room while classes work.
- Decide your date first. One block, same day, whole building. Traditions do not get established by a staggered rollout.

## How It Works

1. **Name the school's three first (leader, 15 minutes of prep).** Before you ask a single teacher to do this, write the two or three things your whole school will do every week without fail. Monday morning announcements that name students by name. A Friday all-call that ends the week the same way every time. Something you can actually sustain in February. Post them in the main hallway before the classroom block.
2. **Open with yours (5 minutes of the block).** Teachers show students the school's traditions and say plainly that the building is committing to them. Then the teacher names one tradition they personally will keep in that room, and why it matters to them.
3. **Students brainstorm (7 minutes).** Two questions on the board. What is one thing you want to happen in here every week? What is one thing that would make Monday easier to walk into? Everything goes up, nothing gets judged yet.
4. **Cut to three (5 minutes).** The class votes and keeps a maximum of three. Each one has to pass two tests: it happens weekly, and it takes under five minutes. A tradition that eats a class period dies by October.
5. **Write and sign the charter (3 minutes).** Three traditions, the day of the week each one happens, and every student signs it. Post it at student eye level, not above the whiteboard where only adults look.

## Why It Works

- Predictability is the part of school culture kids feel first. A student who knows exactly how Friday ends has one less thing to manage.
- Students defend what they built. A tradition a class wrote and signed gets enforced by the class, usually harder than the teacher would.
- Three is enforceable. Eight is a list nobody remembers by Halloween.
- Naming the day makes it real. "We celebrate wins" is a value. "Wins go up Friday after lunch" is a tradition.
- The signatures matter more than the words. It turns the poster from decoration into an agreement with names on it.

## Script for the Leader

For the staff meeting where you assign it:

"Every class is going to name three things that happen every week, no matter what. Before I ask you to do that, here's the building's three, and I'm the one on the hook for them. If you catch me letting one slide, say so. That's the whole point of writing them down."

## Facilitator Tips

- Pick school traditions you can keep on your worst week, not your best one. The tradition you drop in November costs you more credibility than the one you never started.
- Walk the halls the following week and read the charters. Mention two of them by name in your next staff communication. That is how teachers learn you actually looked.
- Secondary schools: run it in advisory, and let each teacher's sections combine into one charter per teacher. Six competing charters in one room does not work.
- If a class picks something you cannot support, negotiate the shape rather than vetoing it. "Food every Friday" becomes "one shared thing every Friday, and it does not have to cost anybody money."
- Take a photo of every charter. May, Week 2 uses them to show students how much of the year they actually kept.

## Display Idea

One hallway board, the school's traditions at the top, and a photo of every class charter underneath it. Kids look for their own room's poster, which means they read the school's three on the way to finding it.
