# Culture Builder: Bring the Graduates Back (May)

Get last year's leavers in front of this year's, and let them say the true thing.

## Objective

Invite back five or six students who left last year and let them talk honestly to the students about to leave. Not a motivational assembly. A plain conversation about what the next place is actually like, what they wish they had known, and what turned out not to matter. Your leaving students are about to hear a great deal of reassurance from adults, and almost none of it lands, because adults are not credible on this topic and a fifteen-year-old who did it last September is.

## Time Needed

25 minutes. About 15 minutes to organise, and one session.

## Supplies / Setup

- Five or six former students, chosen for range rather than for success.
- No slides and no script. A script produces a performance and the value is in the unpolished version.
- Three questions, given to them in advance so they have thought about it.
- Somebody to write down or record the answers, because they are reusable.

## How It Works

1. **Invite for range, not for glory (7 minutes).** Not only the ones who are thriving. Include someone who found it hard, someone who changed course, someone who was not a star here. A panel of successes tells your students that the next place is for people unlike them.
2. **Give them three questions in advance (2 minutes).** What surprised you. What do you wish you had known. What did you worry about here that turned out not to matter. That third one is the question that does the most work and nobody asks it.
3. **Let them answer honestly, including unhelpfully (12 minutes).** They will say things you would rather they did not. That the work was harder, that they were lonely for a term, that a thing your school insisted on turned out to be irrelevant. Do not correct them in the room. The credibility of the whole exercise rests on them being allowed to say the true thing.
4. **Let your students ask anything (5 minutes).** The questions will be practical and small: what the lunch queue is like, whether anyone actually gets in trouble for uniform, how much homework there really is. Small practical fears are what the anxiety is made of.
5. **Write it down and keep it (2 minutes).** Same three questions every year. Within three years you have a genuinely useful record of what leaving this school is actually like, in students' own words, and it doubles as feedback on your own school that no survey will produce.

## Why It Works

- Adults are not credible on this subject and students know it. Someone who did it eleven months ago is the only source with standing.
- Including students who found it hard is what makes the panel believable. Six thriving graduates confirm the fear that the next place is for other people.
- "What did you worry about that turned out not to matter" is the single most anxiety-reducing question available, and it is almost never asked.
- The small practical questions are where the fear actually lives. Lunch queues and toilets, not curriculum.
- The accumulated answers are honest feedback on your school, delivered by people with nothing to lose by saying it.

## Script for the Leader

Introducing the panel:

"These six left here last summer. I asked them back and I asked them to be honest rather than encouraging, including about us. If they say something about this school that I would rather they did not, I am not going to jump in and correct it, because then none of you would believe the rest of it. Ask them anything. They know things I do not."

## Facilitator Tips

- Choose for range and say so to the returning students, or they will assume they were picked because they are doing well and perform accordingly.
- Do not brief them to be positive. The single fastest way to waste this is to ask for encouragement.
- Sit at the side, not at the front. Your presence at the front turns it into a school event.
- Expect criticism of your school and take it without responding in the room. Write it down and use it.
- Ask the same three questions every year. The comparison across years becomes genuinely useful.
- Thank them properly afterwards and tell them what changed because of what they said, if anything did.

## Display Idea

The six-word version. Ask each returning student for six words about the year since they left, and post those alongside the leaving group's own six words. Two cohorts, same wall.
