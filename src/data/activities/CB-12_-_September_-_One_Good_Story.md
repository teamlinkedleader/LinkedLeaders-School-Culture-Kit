# Culture Builder: One Good Story (September)

Every staff member calls one family with one specific good thing, before anyone has to call about a problem.

## Objective

Get a positive first contact to as many families as possible in one week, in September, before the first behavior call or grade concern goes out. Most families' entire relationship with a school is set by whether the first phone call was good news or bad news. This activity moves that call up in the calendar and puts a name and a face behind it. It takes 20 minutes per staff member and it is the highest-return thing on this list.

## Time Needed

20 minutes per staff member. Give them the time in a staff meeting rather than adding it to their evening.

## Supplies / Setup

- Current family contact info, verified. Nothing kills this faster than fifteen minutes of wrong numbers.
- A shared tracker: student name, staff member, date contacted, method. One shared sheet, visible to everyone.
- A quiet-ish room and about 20 minutes of protected time.
- Decide the week. Late September, after teachers know kids well enough to be specific, before the first round of grades goes home.

## How It Works

1. **Make your own calls first (leader, the week before).** Pick five families. Not the easy five. Call the family of a kid who had a rough year last year, and lead with something real that happened this month. Then tell staff you did it and roughly how it went, including any call that was awkward.
2. **Assign the list (5 minutes of the block).** Every staff member takes 3 to 5 students. Divide so that every family in the building gets exactly one call, not so that the popular kids get four. Deliberately assign the students who usually generate the calls home.
3. **Write the one sentence before dialing (5 minutes).** Each staff member writes the specific thing they are going to say about that kid. Specific means an observed moment. "He stayed after to help stack chairs and nobody asked him to" works. "She's a pleasure to have in class" is noise and families hear it as a form letter.
4. **Make the calls (10 minutes).** Sixty to ninety seconds each. Say who you are, say the good thing, say you just wanted them to hear it, end the call. Do not add a request. Do not mention a missing assignment. This call has one job.
5. **Log it and post what came back (2 minutes).** Tick the tracker. Anything a family said worth repeating goes on a wall in the staff room. By Friday that wall is the argument for doing it again in February.

## Why It Works

- The first contact sets the frame for every contact after it. A parent whose first call was good news answers the phone in March. A parent whose first call was a discipline report starts every conversation defending their kid.
- Specificity is the whole thing. Families can tell instantly whether you know their child or are reading off a roster, and a generic positive call does real damage because it proves you do not.
- Ninety seconds is short enough that staff will actually do it, and short enough that it does not turn into a conference.
- Doing it in a staff meeting with the time given rather than assigned as homework is what gets you 90 percent participation instead of 30.
- The wall of responses is what makes it a tradition instead of a one-time push. Staff who see what came back will do it again unprompted.

## Script for the Leader

For assigning it:

"Everybody's making one phone call in the next twenty minutes, and it's good news. I made five last week, including one to a family I know was dreading hearing from us, and it went better than I expected. Rules: sixty seconds, one specific thing you actually saw this month, and you don't ask them for anything. Not a missing assignment, not a permission slip. Nothing. We're just going to be the school that called with something good first."

## Facilitator Tips

- Sort the language needs before the block, not during it. A staff member stuck without an interpreter will quietly skip the call and the family that most needed it gets nothing.
- Text or email is a fine fallback for families who do not answer phones, but the voice call lands harder. Try the call first.
- Watch for the staff member who says they will do it later. Later means never. The point of the protected block is that it gets done in the room.
- Some calls will be awkward. A family may assume something is wrong and take a minute to believe you. Warn staff so the first ten seconds of silence does not spook them.
- Repeat it in February, when everyone is worn down and the families have mostly heard from you about problems since.

## Display Idea

A staff room wall headed "What Families Said Back," with quotes written on notecards as they come in. It fills up over the week, and it is the only proof anyone needs that this was worth twenty minutes.
