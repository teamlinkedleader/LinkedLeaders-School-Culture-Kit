# Culture Builder: The Five Calls (July)

Five phone calls in July, no agenda, to the people who will carry the year.

## Objective

Call five staff members during the summer, for no reason. Not about scheduling. Not about the room change. Not to ask them to do anything. Five minutes each, to say you were thinking about the year and wanted to hear how they are. It is the single cheapest thing a leader can do in July and almost nobody does it, because there is no task attached to it.

## Time Needed

25 minutes total. Five calls, about five minutes each.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your staff list and phone numbers.
- A note of who you called and roughly when, so the pattern is deliberate rather than accidental.
- Nothing to ask for. If you have a request, this is a different call and you should make it separately.

## How It Works

1. **Choose the five deliberately (5 minutes).** Not the five you talk to most. One person who had a hard year. One new hire who has not started yet. One classified staff member. One veteran who has stopped being asked anything. And one person you find difficult, because that call is the one that changes something.
2. **Open by saying there is nothing wrong (during).** Say it in the first sentence. A summer call from the principal reads as bad news, and you have about four seconds before their stomach drops.
3. **Ask one real question and then listen.** "How has your summer actually been?" is enough. So is "what are you not looking forward to in August?" Then stop talking. The silence is where the useful part is.
4. **Do not solve anything on the call.** If something comes up that needs action, say you have heard it and you will come back to it, then write it down. Solving it live turns a human call into a work call.
5. **Write down what you heard, the same day (5 minutes).** Not for a file. For you. In August you will have forgotten that one of them is caring for a parent, and that is exactly the thing worth remembering in October when they seem short with someone.

## Why It Works

- A call with no agenda is unusual enough to be remembered for years. Most staff have never had one from a school leader.
- July is the only time of year you can do it without the conversation being about something operational, which is precisely what makes it land.
- Choosing across roles and temperaments, rather than calling your favourites, is what stops this becoming a nice thing you do for the people you already like.
- The person you find difficult is the highest-value call on the list. A five-minute human conversation in July changes how a hard conversation goes in February.
- Five is achievable. Twenty is a project, and a project in July does not happen.

## Script for the Leader

Opening the call:

"Hi Denise, it is Mike. Nothing is wrong, I promise, this is not that kind of call. I have been thinking about the year and I wanted to hear how your summer has actually been. That is genuinely the whole reason I rang."

## Facilitator Tips

- Say "nothing is wrong" first. Every time. Their stomach drops otherwise and they will not hear the rest.
- Keep it to five minutes unless they want longer. A call that overruns feels like an obligation next time.
- Do not do this by text or email. The point is your voice, and a text achieves almost none of it.
- Never follow a call with a request within the same week. Once staff learn the friendly call precedes an ask, you cannot make one again.
- If somebody does not pick up, leave a short voicemail saying nothing is wrong and you were just checking in. Do not chase.
- Five in July, five more in October, five in February. Over a year you reach a lot of a building without it ever becoming a programme.
