# Culture Builder: Re-Recruit the People You Cannot Lose (April)

Tell your best people you want them back next year, before they decide for themselves.

## Objective

Name the staff whose departure would genuinely damage your school, then go and ask each one to come back next year, out loud, individually, in April. January's stay conversations were diagnostic. This is persuasion, and it happens in the weeks when contracts are signed and resignations are filed. The single most common regret in school leadership is assuming a great teacher knew they were valued.

## Time Needed

20 minutes to build the list. Then about ten minutes per person, across two weeks.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your staff list, and an honest answer to one question: whose leaving would actually hurt.
- Your notes from January's stay conversations, if you ran them.
- Your district's resignation and contract deadlines, so you know how much runway you have.
- An honest sense of what you can and cannot offer, before you promise anything.

## How It Works

1. **Name the ten you cannot lose (7 minutes).** Not the ten you like. The ten whose absence would visibly damage the school next year. Include the paraprofessional who holds a classroom together and the office manager who is the actual institutional memory, not just teachers.
2. **Check the calendar against the deadlines (3 minutes).** Find your district's resignation deadline and work backwards. A re-recruiting conversation after somebody has already decided is a nice conversation with no effect.
3. **Ask directly, in person, one at a time (10 minutes each).** Say three things: that you want them back, one specific reason why this school is better with them in it, and a direct question about whether anything would stop them. Do not send it in an email and do not do it in a group.
4. **Listen for the thing they name, and answer honestly (during).** Most will name something concrete: a schedule, a room, a class assignment, a duty, a colleague. Some of it you can fix. Say which you can and which you cannot, in the room, rather than promising to look into everything.
5. **Do the fixable thing within two weeks (10 minutes to plan).** A promise made in a re-recruiting conversation and not delivered before June is worse than never having the conversation, because it converts a good moment into evidence.

## Why It Works

- Strong staff frequently leave without ever being asked to stay, and they routinely report afterwards that being asked would have changed the decision.
- April is the operative month. Late enough that people are deciding, early enough that the decision is not filed.
- Being told specifically why you matter to a place is qualitatively different from being praised. Praise is about your performance; this is about the school's need for you, and it is the thing people say they never heard.
- Asking what would stop them surfaces the fixable problems while there is still time to fix them, which is exactly what an exit interview does two months too late.
- Including classified staff on the list is where most schools miss. A school that loses its office manager loses more operational knowledge than it loses when a teacher leaves, and nobody ever re-recruits the office manager.

## Script for the Leader

"I want to say something directly rather than assume you know it. I want you back next year. Not as a formality, I mean I have thought about what this place looks like in September without you and it is noticeably worse. The way you handle the kids nobody else can reach is not a nice extra, it is a thing this school depends on. So I am asking: is there anything that would stop you coming back? I would rather hear it now while I can still do something about it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Say the words "I want you back next year." Leaders reliably think they have communicated this and reliably have not said it out loud.
- Do not do this by email, and do not do it in a group. Both convert it into an announcement.
- Include classified staff, paraprofessionals, and the office. They are the most likely to have never been asked and the most costly to replace quietly.
- Do not promise what you cannot deliver. "I cannot change that and here is why" is respected; a vague promise that dissolves in June is not.
- If somebody tells you they are already leaving, thank them for saying so and ask what would have changed it. That answer is worth as much as a retention.
- Keep the list and what each person asked for. In June it is a checklist of whether you did what you said.
