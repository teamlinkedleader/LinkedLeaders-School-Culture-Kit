# Culture Builder: Welcome the Mid-Year Hire (January)

Give the person starting in January the welcome August staff got, because they need it more.

## Objective

Build a proper first week for anyone joining mid-year. A January starter walks into a building where every relationship, in-joke, routine and unwritten rule was formed without them, often replacing someone the students liked, frequently into the hardest assignment in the school. They get none of the pre-service week, none of the icebreakers, and usually a login and a room key. It is the hardest way to join a school and it is entirely fixable in 20 minutes of planning.

## Time Needed

20 minutes to plan. The week itself runs on what you set up.

## Supplies / Setup

- The first-30-days card from August, which already exists and can be reused unchanged.
- A partner chosen the same way as in August: for generosity, not seniority or proximity.
- Ten minutes of the next staff meeting.
- The honest context of what they are walking into, which somebody has to tell them.

## How It Works

1. **Tell them the truth before day one (5 minutes).** Whatever the situation actually is. The class had three supply teachers. Their predecessor was much loved, or was not. There is a group of students who will test them for a fortnight. A new person who discovers this in week two feels deceived; one who is told in advance feels trusted and prepares.
2. **Assign a partner and the same six human tasks (3 minutes).** Reuse the August card exactly. Eat lunch together twice, walk the whole building, meet the custodian and office manager by name, sit in each other's rooms, name the three unwritten rules, tell them the story of one thing this school does that nobody explains to outsiders.
3. **Introduce them properly to staff (4 minutes).** Not a line in a bulletin. Two minutes at a staff meeting, with something human about them that is not their job title. Then say out loud that starting in January is hard and ask the room to go to them rather than waiting to be approached.
4. **Introduce them to their students deliberately (3 minutes).** If they are replacing someone, name that person and let the class say something about them first. A class carrying an unacknowledged loss will take it out on whoever arrives next, and thirty seconds of naming it defuses most of that.
5. **Check in at two weeks, then at six (5 minutes).** Two weeks is when the surface politeness ends and the real questions arrive. Six weeks is when a mid-year hire either belongs or has quietly decided they do not. Diarise both now.

## Why It Works

- A mid-year starter joins a school where every norm formed without them, and gets none of the structures August staff got to form theirs.
- Being told the truth in advance is read as respect. Being told nothing and discovering it is read as a trick, and it colours the first year.
- Naming the predecessor to the class is the step everyone skips. Students grieve a teacher who left, and unnamed grief becomes behaviour aimed at the newcomer.
- The August card already exists. Reusing it costs nothing and means the mid-year hire gets the same thing rather than an improvised version.
- Six weeks is the real decision point and almost nobody checks then, because by six weeks the new person looks fine.

## Script for the Leader

Introducing them at a staff meeting:

"This is Anna, who starts Monday in Year 4. Anna has spent six years in schools much bigger than this one and she keeps bees, which I am told is a conversation she is happy to have. I want to say the obvious thing: starting in January is harder than starting in August. All of you found your people in pre-service week and Anna does not get that. So please go to her. Do not wait to be approached, because she has thirty children and no idea where anything is."

## Facilitator Tips

- Tell them the hard context before they accept if possible, and certainly before day one.
- Choose the partner for generosity. The wrong partner teaches a new hire the building's cynicism in a week, and a mid-year hire has no counterweight.
- Give them the same room-setup time an August starter had, even though the year is running. They are doing in a weekend what everyone else did in a week.
- Introduce them to classified staff by name in the first two days. Those are the people who will save them.
- Do not put them on any committee for a term. The instinct to include is right; the effect is more load on the person with the least footing.
- Watch the six-week check-in. That is where you find out, and it is the one that gets skipped because they seem fine.
