# Culture Builder: Thank the Substitutes (November)

Recognise the adults who teach your students and are treated like furniture.

## Objective

Find out who has actually substituted in your building this year, thank them by name, and fix the experience of walking in cold. Substitutes teach your students, absorb your hardest classes with none of the relationships, and are frequently the only adults in a school nobody greets. They are also the people who decide whether your school is one they will accept a booking at again, which in a sub shortage is a real operational matter as well as a human one.

## Time Needed

20 minutes. Ten to build the list, ten to write.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your booking records or whoever arranges cover. The list will be longer than you expect.
- Note cards.
- One index card per classroom for the welcome cards.
- Your office manager, who knows which subs come back and which ones quietly stopped accepting.

## How It Works

1. **Get the actual list (7 minutes).** Every person who has covered a class this year, with names. Ask whoever books cover, because there is no chance you know them all. Include the ones who came once.
2. **Ask which ones stopped coming (3 minutes).** Your office manager knows. A sub who accepted twice and then never again is telling you something about the experience, and it is almost always about being left with nothing, greeted by nobody, or handed the hardest group with no context.
3. **Write to each of them (7 minutes).** Short, specific where you can be, honest where you cannot. "You covered Year 4 twice in October and both times the class was calm when I walked past" beats a generic thank-you and takes the same time to write.
4. **Put a welcome card in every classroom (3 minutes to organise).** Not the emergency folder. A card on the desk with the teacher's name, where the toilets and the staff room are, who to ask next door, what time break is, and the name of one student who will helpfully tell them the truth. Ask teachers to write their own.
5. **Greet the next one yourself.** Find the substitute in the building on the next day you have one, introduce yourself, and tell them where the coffee is. That is the whole intervention and it takes ninety seconds.

## Why It Works

- Substitutes carry your classes with none of the relationships that make the job possible, and are routinely the only adults in a school nobody speaks to all day.
- In a shortage, being a school subs are willing to come back to is an operational advantage, not a nicety. The schools that treat them well get covered.
- The list of who stopped accepting is genuinely diagnostic. It tells you something about your building that no survey will.
- A welcome card written by the class teacher costs nothing and removes most of the first-hour disorientation that makes cover days go badly.
- Greeting them yourself is ninety seconds and it is remembered, because almost nobody does it.

## Script for the Leader

When you meet a substitute in the corridor:

"You must be covering for Ms. Patel today. I am Mike, I am the principal here. The staff room is through that door and the coffee is free, please use it. If anything goes sideways, Rob is next door and he is very good. Thanks for coming in, genuinely."

## Facilitator Tips

- Get the list from whoever books cover. Building it from memory will miss most of them.
- Include long-term substitutes in every staff activity as though they were permanent, because for those students they are.
- Ask a returning sub what would make the day easier, and expect the answer to be mundane: a login, a seating plan, knowing where the toilets are.
- Do not skip the welcome cards because it feels small. First-hour disorientation is what makes a cover day fall apart.
- Watch how your staff talk about subs in front of students. "We have a sub today so I expect you to behave" tells thirty children the adult in front of them is not really a teacher.
- Repeat the thanks in April. The spring booking season is when a school's reputation among subs actually matters.
