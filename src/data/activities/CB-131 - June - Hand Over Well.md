# Culture Builder: Hand Over Well (June)

Write down what the next person needs to know, whether or not you are the one leaving.

## Objective

Write the handover document that does not currently exist anywhere: not the budget codes and key inventory, but the things a successor genuinely needs and that live only in your head. Every leadership transition in a school loses years of accumulated understanding because nobody writes down the part that matters. Do it in June whether or not you are going anywhere, because the value of having written it does not depend on leaving, and the year you do leave is the year you will not have time.

## Time Needed

25 minutes. It will not be finished and that is fine; an unfinished honest handover beats a polished absent one.

## Supplies / Setup

- The July culture statement and both grades from January and June.
- Your keep and kill lists.
- Somewhere permanent that is not your own drive, because a handover in a departing leader's personal folder is not a handover.
- Enough candour to write things you would not say in a staff meeting.

## How It Works

1. **Write the things that are in nobody's file (8 minutes).** Which staff hold the building together regardless of title. Which families have had a bad experience and why, so a new leader does not walk into it blind. Which agreements were made verbally and are still binding on the school's honour. Which decisions look strange and had good reasons, so nobody undoes them in week two.
2. **Write what you tried that failed, and why (5 minutes).** This is the most valuable and least written part of any handover. A successor with no record of failed attempts will repeat every one of them, and the staff will watch them do it knowing exactly how it ends.
3. **Name the traps (4 minutes).** The meeting that always overruns. The parent who goes straight to the board. The tradition that looks trivial and is not. The person who will offer to help and should be politely declined. Every school has these and none of them are written anywhere.
4. **Write what you would protect (4 minutes).** The small number of things that would genuinely damage this school if a new leader removed them without understanding why they exist. Be specific and be short. A long list of untouchables is not a handover, it is an attempt to keep running the place after you have gone.
5. **Store it where a successor will actually find it (4 minutes).** Not in your email. Not on your laptop. Somewhere institutional, with a filename that makes it obvious, and tell one other person it exists.

## Why It Works

- Every school leadership transition destroys accumulated understanding that nobody thought to write down, and the cost lands on students two years later.
- Failed attempts are the highest-value content and the least recorded. Without them a successor repeats the same three mistakes in their first year.
- Writing it when you are not leaving produces a far more honest document, because it is not a farewell and there is nothing to be gracious about.
- The traps are the difference between a competent successor and a competent successor having an unnecessarily hard first term.
- Storing it institutionally is what makes it a handover rather than a private note. Most of these documents are lost because they lived in one person's account.

## Script for the Leader

There is no audience. What to tell the person you store it with:

"I have written a handover and it is in the shared drive under Leadership Handover, not in my email. I am not going anywhere as far as I know. I wrote it because if I got hit by a bus tomorrow, the person who came next would have the budget codes and none of the things that actually matter, and they would spend a year rediscovering all of it while the school paid for the delay."

## Facilitator Tips

- Write it in June whether or not you are leaving. A handover written in a notice period is a valediction, not a document.
- Be honest about people without being unkind or unfair. If you would not want it read aloud, write the version you would.
- Include your own mistakes explicitly. A handover that reads as a record of your successes is useless to whoever follows.
- Keep the protect list short. Three things is credible. Fifteen is you refusing to leave.
- Update it once a year rather than rewriting it. Ten minutes each June keeps it alive.
- Ask your predecessor whether they left one. The answer is almost always no, and it is a useful thing to feel before you decide not to bother.

## Display Idea

None. This is an institutional document, stored where the next person will find it, and read by almost nobody until the day it is the most valuable thing in the school.
