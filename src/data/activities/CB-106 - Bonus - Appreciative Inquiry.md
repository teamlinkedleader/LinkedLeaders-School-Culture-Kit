# Culture Builder: Appreciative Inquiry (Bonus)

Start from a time this school was genuinely at its best, and work out what made it possible.

## Objective

Open a hard conversation by asking what has worked here rather than what is broken. Appreciative Inquiry moves through four stages: Discover, Dream, Design, Deliver. Used honestly it is not positive thinking, it is evidence gathering, because the conditions that produced your school's best moments are usually still available and nobody has named them.

## Time Needed

30 to 45 minutes for the Discover and Dream stages, which is where the value is. Design and Deliver need a second session.

## Supplies / Setup

- Chart paper and markers.
- Small groups of four to six. Mixed across roles and grade levels.
- Your own example of a moment when this school was at its best, prepared in advance and specific.
- A commitment to not let it slide into a complaint session, which is the failure mode.

## Sample Prompts

- **Discover.** Describe a time when our school was genuinely at its best. What was happening? Who was involved? What made that success possible?
- **Dream.** If those conditions were normal here rather than exceptional, what would a Tuesday look like?
- **Design.** Which of those conditions could we recreate deliberately, starting now?
- **Deliver.** Who does what, and by when?

## How It Works

1. **Go first with a real moment, not a general one (5 minutes).** Name a specific time, with a date and people in it, when this school was at its best. Then name what made it possible. Specificity is what stops the room producing platitudes.
2. **Discover, in small groups (12 minutes).** Each person shares their own best-of-this-school moment. The group's job is to listen for what the moments have in common, not to rank them. Capture the common conditions, not the stories.
3. **Dream, still in groups (10 minutes).** If those conditions were ordinary rather than exceptional, what would people notice? Push for observable detail rather than adjectives.
4. **Report the conditions, not the anecdotes (8 minutes).** Each group names the two or three conditions that kept appearing. Write them all up. You will usually see the same three or four across every group: people had time, people were trusted, someone noticed, nobody was alone with it.
5. **Pick one condition to recreate deliberately (5 minutes).** One. Name what it would take and who owns it. Design and Deliver are a second session, and saying so protects this one from collapsing into planning.

## Why It Works

- Starting from what worked produces different information than starting from what is broken, and it is information you can act on, because it already happened here.
- The conditions behind a school's best moments are almost always structural and repeatable: time, trust, attention, and not being alone. Naming them turns a good memory into a design brief.
- It reaches the staff who disengage from problem-solving sessions because those sessions have always felt like blame.
- Focusing groups on shared conditions rather than on individual stories stops it becoming a nostalgia exercise.
- The leader going first with something specific and real is what sets whether the room produces evidence or greeting-card language.

## Script for the Leader

"Before we talk about what is not working, I want twenty minutes on what has. And I do not mean generally, I mean a specific time. Mine is the week in February two years ago when Marcy's team covered all her classes after the funeral, without anybody organising it. What made that possible was that those five people knew each other well enough to just do it. That is the kind of thing I want from you. Then we are going to look at what all our moments have in common, because I suspect it is the same three or four things."

## Facilitator Tips

- Prepare your own example properly. An improvised one will be vague and the room will match it.
- Hold the line against drift into complaints. Acknowledge that the problems are real, name that they get their own session, and come back.
- Ask for conditions, not stories, at the report-back stage. Otherwise you get thirty minutes of anecdotes and no design brief.
- Do not attempt all four stages in one sitting. Discover and Dream in session one, Design and Deliver in session two, or you will rush the part that matters.
- Watch for the staff member whose best moment is from a previous school or a previous leader. That is honest and it is useful; do not deflect it.

## Display Idea

The list of recurring conditions, posted plainly, headed with something like "What it takes for this school to be at its best, according to us." No names, no stories, just the conditions.
