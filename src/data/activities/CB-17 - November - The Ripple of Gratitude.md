# Culture Builder: The Ripple of Gratitude (November)

Help staff feel seen, appreciated and connected by highlighting the small actions that made a big impact this fall.

## Objective

Collect specific, small acts of help from the term, and read a few aloud. The constraint that makes it work is that **big gestures are not allowed**. Gratitude aimed at large visible efforts recognises the people who were already visible; restricting it to small actions surfaces the quiet ones, which is where most of a school's actual support happens.

## Time Needed

10 to 12 minutes.

## Supplies / Setup

- A sticky note for everyone, or a digital note if the meeting is virtual.
- A large sheet of chart paper titled "Small Actions. Big Ripples."
- Pens.
- Your own note, written in advance about a small thing somebody did for you.

## How It Works

1. **Everyone writes one small thing** someone on staff did this fall that positively impacted them. Big gestures are explicitly not allowed. Examples: covered my recess duty unexpectedly, gave me a pep talk after a hard parent meeting, shared a lesson idea that saved my week.
2. **Post them anywhere on the wall.** No sections, no names required on the note itself beyond who is being thanked.
3. **The ripple moment, which is the part that lands.** Invite three or four volunteers to pick a note at random that is not their own and read it aloud.
4. **Pause after each one and name it.** "This is the ripple. One small action, one lasting impact." Say it the same way each time.
5. **Close with the reflection prompt:** "What's one small thing you can do this week to create a positive ripple for someone else?" Do not collect answers.

## Why It Works

- Keeps gratitude grounded in specific behaviours rather than generic praise, and specific is the only kind anyone believes.
- Barring big gestures is the whole design. It moves recognition away from the visible performers and onto the people who quietly cover for others.
- Having someone else read a note aloud separates the thanks from the thanker, so it lands as evidence rather than as flattery.
- Builds relational trust quietly, and it is low prep and high impact in a month when nobody has capacity for anything elaborate.

## Script for the Leader

"One sticky note each. Write down one small thing somebody here did this fall that made a difference to you. Small only, and I mean that as a rule: if it was a big obvious effort that everybody already saw, it doesn't count. I want the covered duty, the pep talk in the hallway, the lesson idea that saved your week. Then a few of us are going to pick a note that isn't ours and read it out."

## Facilitator Tips

- Enforce the small-actions rule when you introduce it, and give three examples so the room calibrates.
- Read yours or have it read, and pick something genuinely small that was done for you.
- Do not have people read their own notes. The distance is what makes it work.
- Say the ripple line the same way every time. Repetition is what makes it a ritual instead of a comment.
- Leave the wall up through November rather than clearing it after the meeting.
- This pairs with CB-68 The Handwritten Five. The wall tells you who is quietly carrying people, which is exactly who to write to.
