# Culture Builder: Classified Staff Appreciation Swap (February)

Strengthen relationships and build empathy across roles by experiencing each other's worlds.

## Objective

Have teachers and administrators symbolically swap roles with classified staff, custodians, food service, paraprofessionals and secretaries, for half an hour, and then invite the swap back. The point is not a gesture of appreciation. It is that most staff genuinely do not know what the rest of the building's jobs involve, and empathy that is not grounded in having seen the work does not last past the assembly.

## Time Needed

30 to 45 minutes per pairing, arranged across a week. This one runs longer than most in this collection, and it needs to.

## Supplies / Setup

- Pairings arranged in advance, with both people asked rather than volunteered.
- Coverage sorted, which is the actual work of organising this.
- A camera, and permission to use the photos.
- Note cards for the thank-yous.

## How It Works

1. **The pairing.** Invite teachers or administrators to swap roles symbolically with a classified staff member for 30 to 45 minutes during the week. A teacher might shadow a custodian during cleanup, or the secretary during the morning rush. A principal could join cafeteria duty or bus loading.
2. **The swap back.** Classified staff are invited to shadow their partner in return: sit in on a class, join a planning meeting. This is not optional garnish. A one-way swap is observation of the lower-status role by the higher-status one, and it reads exactly like that.
3. **The reflection.** End with a short Coffee and Conversation where pairs share one thing they learned about each other's work and one thing they appreciate more after the experience.
4. **The public celebration.** Create a simple display, bulletin board or slideshow, with photos and quotes: "I never realised how many moving parts our lunch team manages in 20 minutes." "Watching Ms. Harris calm a kindergarten meltdown should be a masterclass in patience."
5. **A handwritten thank-you** from each swap partner to their classified colleague. Handwritten, not typed, and specific about something they saw.

## Why It Works

- Builds mutual respect and understanding across roles that share a building and almost nothing else.
- Breaks down the us-and-them silos that every school has and few name out loud.
- Gives classified staff visibility and genuine appreciation from peers rather than from management, which is a different and better thing.
- Creates shared stories and laughter, which is powerful culture glue and outlasts any appreciation week.
- Doing the swap in both directions is what makes it respectful rather than anthropological.

## Script for the Leader

"For one week, some of us are swapping. I'm doing bus loading with Ray on Tuesday morning, and Ray is sitting in on the leadership meeting on Thursday, which he may regret. This isn't a nice gesture toward the classified team. It's because I've been principal here for two years and I could not tell you what the first forty minutes of Ray's day actually involves, and that's a gap in how I make decisions. Both directions, or it doesn't count."

## Facilitator Tips

- Go first and do it visibly, and pick the role furthest from your own.
- **Both directions, always.** A one-way swap where only certified staff shadow classified staff reproduces exactly the hierarchy you are trying to soften.
- Solve coverage before you invite anyone. Asking a paraprofessional to shadow a class without covering their post is asking them to do two jobs.
- Ask permission before photographing anyone, and let people decline without explanation.
- **On the optional gift:** the original suggests a mug or gift card alongside the note. That is fine if the budget exists, but the handwritten note is doing the work. A gift with a generic note is worse than a specific note alone.
- Do not schedule it during a week when the classified team is already stretched, which in most schools rules out the start and end of a semester.

## Display Idea

Photos and quotes on a board in the main entrance rather than the staff room, so families and students see the whole staff being valued, not just the teaching staff.
