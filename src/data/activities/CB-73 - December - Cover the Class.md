# Culture Builder: Cover the Class (December)

You teach the class. They get their period back. No paperwork, no catch.

## Objective

Walk into a classroom in December, take over for one period, and send the teacher out. Not to a meeting. Out. To grade, to nap in their car, to eat a hot lunch, to call the pediatrician they have been meaning to call since October. This is the cheapest and most credible thing a school leader can do in the hardest month of the year, and it costs nothing but your calendar.

## Time Needed

25 minutes of planning. Then one class period per teacher, spread across December.

## Supplies / Setup

- The master schedule, and your own calendar with December's real commitments on it.
- An honest count of how many periods you can actually cover. Three is a real number. Twenty is a fantasy that ends in you cancelling on people.
- Nothing to teach. Ask the teacher what they want you to do with their students, and do that. If they leave you a plan, follow it.
- A posted sign-up sheet, first come first served.

## How It Works

1. **Count what you can honestly give (7 minutes).** Look at December and find the periods you can cover without cancelling something that matters. Be conservative. A leader who promises eight and delivers three has taught the building that their word is soft.
2. **Post the sheet publicly (3 minutes).** Days, periods, and your name on each one. Public means it is real and it means everyone can see it is offered evenly rather than handed to favourites.
3. **Ask what they want, then do exactly that (5 minutes per teacher).** Some will leave you a lesson. Some will say "just show a movie, I don't care." Some will hand you their hardest class on purpose. All three answers are fine. Do not use the period to demonstrate teaching, do not improvise a lesson you think is better, and do not mention anything you observe about their room.
4. **Make them actually leave (during).** Most teachers will hover, or go grade in the back. Send them out. "You have to leave the room or this doesn't count." Say it kindly and mean it.
5. **Say nothing about it afterward except thank you (2 minutes).** No debrief, no notes on the kids, no feedback about their plans. The gift is contaminated the moment it produces information you use.

## Why It Works

- December is the longest unbroken stretch in most school calendars, and time is the only thing teachers actually want. Every other form of appreciation is a substitute for this one.
- It costs you nothing and it costs them nothing, which is why it lands differently than food or a gift card. You spent the one resource you cannot fake having.
- Teaching their class puts you back in the job, in front of their kids, in their room. You will learn more about your building in three periods than in thirty walkthroughs, and you must never say so out loud.
- The public sheet makes it equitable and repeatable. A private favour to two teachers builds resentment in the twenty who did not get asked.
- Following their plan instead of yours is the whole discipline. The moment it becomes a performance of your own teaching, it is about you.

## Script for the Leader

"Sign-up sheet's by the copier. I've got six periods in December I can cover. You tell me what you want done with your kids, I'll do exactly that, and you go do whatever you need to do. Grade, sleep, leave the building, I genuinely don't care and I'm not going to ask. Only rule is you actually have to leave the room."

## Facilitator Tips

- Promise fewer periods than you think you can do. Then add more if December is kind to you.
- Do not save this for teachers who are struggling. Being covered should not be a signal that somebody is worried about you.
- If a teacher hands you their toughest group, take it as the compliment it is. They are testing whether you can still do the job, and passing that test is worth a year of credibility.
- Never mention what you saw. Not their wall, not their seating chart, not the kid who was on his phone. One comment and no one signs up next year.
- Assistant principals and instructional coaches can offer their own periods, but do not fold their coverage into your count. Staff are tracking what you personally gave.
- Keep the sheet up past December. This works in February even better, and February is when it is needed most.

## Display Idea

The sign-up sheet stays posted with the covered periods checked off and dated. By the end of the month it is a visible record of hours you handed back, which is a better piece of evidence about your priorities than anything you could say in a meeting.
