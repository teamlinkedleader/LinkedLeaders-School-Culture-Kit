# Culture Builder: Lunchroom and Playground (October)

Go and watch the twenty minutes where most of your school's conflict actually happens.

## Objective

Spend one full break watching the lunchroom or the playground properly, and map where students actually go. Most behaviour referrals in a school trace back to unstructured time, and most leaders have never observed that time as anything other than supervision. This is 25 minutes of watching rather than policing, and it will change what you think your school's problems are.

## Time Needed

25 minutes. One full lunch or break, watching rather than supervising.

## Supplies / Setup

- A rough sketch of the space on paper, the lunchroom layout or the yard.
- Somebody else covering your usual duty, so you are genuinely observing.
- Nothing to hand out and nobody to correct. If you intervene you stop seeing.
- Your lunch supervisors and paraprofessionals told in advance what you are doing, or they will think they are being inspected.

## How It Works

1. **Map where people are, not where they should be (8 minutes).** Draw the space and mark the clusters. Where is it loud, where is it empty, where do the same groups sit every day, and which parts of the space does nobody use. Adults included: mark where the supervising adults stand, because it is almost always the same three spots.
2. **Find the students who are alone (7 minutes).** Not the ones who look alone for a minute. The ones who are alone the whole time, every day. Write their names down. Your lunch supervisors already know exactly who these students are and have almost certainly never been asked.
3. **Watch one transition end to end (4 minutes).** The queue, the doorway, the bell. Most conflict happens in the transitions rather than in the middle, and transitions are fixable with structure in a way that behaviour is not.
4. **Ask the supervisors what they would change (4 minutes).** They have the best information in the building about this twenty minutes and are almost never consulted about it. Ask what one change would make their job easier, and take it seriously.
5. **Make one structural change, not a behaviour plan (2 minutes to decide).** Move a table. Open a room. Change the queue direction. Put a bench somewhere. Structure beats supervision for this, and one change made this week beats a policy written this month.

## Why It Works

- Unstructured time produces most of a school's conflict, and it is the time leaders understand least because they are usually working through it.
- The students who are alone at break are frequently invisible in every other setting, because classrooms have structure that hides it. Break does not.
- Lunch supervisors and paraprofessionals hold detailed knowledge about student relationships that no one asks for, and asking changes their standing in the building as well as producing better answers.
- Transitions are structural problems dressed as behaviour problems. Doorways, queues and bells can be redesigned. "Being sensible in the corridor" cannot.
- Watching without intervening is the discipline. The moment you correct something you become part of the scene and stop seeing what actually happens.

## Script for the Leader

Telling the supervising team beforehand:

"I am going to be out at lunch on Thursday and I am not there to check on you. Someone else has my duty. I want to actually watch what happens for twenty minutes, because I have never done it, and I suspect you three know more about how this school works socially than the rest of us put together. At the end I am going to ask you what you would change, and I would like a real answer."

## Facilitator Tips

- Get your duty covered or you will end up supervising and see nothing.
- Tell the supervising adults first. Unannounced observation reads as inspection and you will get performance rather than reality.
- Write down the names of students who are alone and do something with the list. It pairs directly with Every Student Claimed.
- Do not fix behaviour on the spot. You are there once as an observer; you can be a principal again tomorrow.
- Repeat it in February. The social map of a school changes completely between October and February and nobody notices.
- Bring one student's perspective in if you can. Ask a couple of students where they would sit if they arrived tomorrow knowing nobody.

## Display Idea

Nothing public. The map is working material and it names individual students, so it stays with you and the counselor rather than on any wall.
