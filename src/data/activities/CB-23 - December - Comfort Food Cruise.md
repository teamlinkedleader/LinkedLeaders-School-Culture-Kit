# Culture Builder: Comfort Food Cruise (December)

An end-of-semester potluck where every dish has a story.

## Objective

Run the end-of-semester potluck with one change that turns it into a culture activity: everyone brings the thing they personally eat when they are exhausted, celebrating, or just trying to make it to Friday, and writes down why. The food is the excuse. The index cards are the activity.

## Time Needed

25 minutes, at the end of the semester.

## Supplies / Setup

- A simple sign-up with loose categories: Savoury, Sides, Sweets, Drinks. No micromanaging.
- Index cards and pens, one card per dish.
- Somewhere to plug in a slow cooker, which somebody will need.
- Optional and worth it: a hot cocoa station, a chill playlist, and a "leave a note of appreciation" board.

## Sample Prompts

- **On the index card beside each dish:** "Why is this your comfort food?"
- Real answers this produces: "My grandma made this every Christmas." "Survival fuel when my seniors turn in 150 late assignments."

## How It Works

1. **Send a simple sign-up with loose categories.** Savoury, Sides, Sweets, Drinks. Resist organising it further; the looseness is what keeps it from becoming a chore.
2. **Set the brief clearly:** one small dish that represents your personal comfort food. Homemade or store-bought, explicitly both. Mac and cheese, garlic bread, ravioli from Costco, dumplings, nachos, tamales, soup, cookies, breakfast burritos, whatever brings you joy.
3. **Add the ten-second storytelling moment.** An index card next to each dish answering "Why is this your comfort food?" This is the step that does the cultural work and it is the one most likely to get dropped.
4. **Keep the vibe cozy.** Hot cocoa, a playlist, low expectations.
5. **End with gratitude.** Invite everyone to drop a quick shout-out to a colleague who helped them survive the semester.

## Why It Works

- Zero pressure by design: no themed recipes, no cooking competition, and store-bought is stated as equally welcome rather than tolerated.
- Staff love learning small things about each other, and the cards deliver exactly that without anyone having to stand up and share.
- Every dish carries a story, which turns a room of people eating into a room of people learning about each other.
- Comfort food is a genuinely revealing prompt. It surfaces family, history and hard weeks without ever asking about any of them directly.

## Script for the Leader

"End-of-semester potluck, one rule: bring the thing you actually eat when you're wiped out. Not your best recipe, your comfort food. Store-bought is completely fine and I will be bringing something from a freezer. There's an index card next to every dish, and I want you to write why it's yours. That's the bit I'm actually interested in."

## Facilitator Tips

- **On cost:** this asks people to bring food, which is not free. Say plainly and once that contributing is optional and that turning up empty-handed is completely fine. Some of your staff are on tight budgets in the most expensive month of the year, and the ones who most need a warm room are the most likely to quietly not come.
- Provide a few dishes yourself so the table is not thin if fewer people contribute than expected.
- Make sure the kitchen team, custodians and office staff can actually attend, which usually means solving coverage rather than issuing an invitation.
- Label allergens. Ask people to note major allergens on the same index card.
- Do not schedule it in the last two days before break, when everyone is drowning. A week earlier lands better.
- The appreciation board is worth the five minutes it takes to set up.

## Display Idea

Keep the index cards. A board of "why this is my comfort food" in the staff room through January is a quiet, funny, human thing to walk past in the worst month.
