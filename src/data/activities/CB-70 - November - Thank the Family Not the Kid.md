# Culture Builder: Thank the Family, Not the Kid (November)

One note home that thanks the adult raising the child, for something they did.

## Objective

Send every family a message in November that thanks *them*, not their child. Schools praise students to parents constantly and thank parents essentially never, and the ones who most deserve it are usually the ones who hear from school the least often or under the worst circumstances. Twenty minutes of staff time, aimed at the adult, about something that adult actually did.

## Time Needed

20 minutes per staff member, given inside a staff meeting.

## Supplies / Setup

- Contact info, verified.
- The tracker from September's family call, if you ran one, so you can see who has and has not heard anything good from you.
- Note cards, or the phone, or whatever channel each family actually responds to.
- A short list of examples on the board, because this is a genuinely unfamiliar move and staff will default to praising the kid.

## How It Works

1. **Send yours first, to the family you would least expect to thank (leader, before the block).** Usually that is a family you have had a hard conversation with. Find the true thing. They answered the phone when it was hard. They came in when it would have been easier not to. They kept their kid coming to school through something difficult. Then tell your staff you did it and roughly how it went.
2. **Teach the distinction (5 minutes of the block).** The message is about the adult. "Your daughter has been great" is a note about a student. "You got her here every day through October and I know that wasn't nothing" is a note about a parent. Put two or three examples on the board.
3. **Find the specific thing (5 minutes).** Each staff member writes the actual behavior they are thanking. Sending the kid with what they need. Reading with them. Answering an email fast. Showing up to a meeting they did not want to attend. Raising a kid who is kind to other kids, which is a real thing an adult did.
4. **Send it (10 minutes).** Note, call, or text depending on the family. Ninety seconds if it is a call. No requests attached and no mention of anything academic.
5. **Log it and find the misses (2 minutes).** Mark the tracker. The families with nothing next to their name after this and September's round are your list for December, and that overlap is worth looking at hard.

## Why It Works

- Parents hear about their children constantly and about themselves never. The novelty alone makes this land harder than any positive report about a student.
- November is the right timing. It sits before conferences and report cards in most calendars, and it puts something good in the relationship before anything difficult goes into it.
- Thanking a struggling family for something real is disproportionately powerful, because those families have usually only ever been contacted about a problem.
- It changes staff perception too. A teacher who has to find one true thing an adult did for their kid ends up thinking about that family differently, and that shows up in the next hard conversation.
- The overlap with September's tracker exposes the families your school has still never said anything good to, which is information you cannot get any other way.

## Script for the Leader

For setting it up with staff:

"Different from September. In September we called about the kid. This time we're thanking the grown-up, for something they did. I sent mine to the Ramirez family this morning, and yes, that's the family we had the meeting with in October. What I said was that they came to that meeting when it would have been easier to blow it off, and that it mattered. Find one true thing the adult did. If you can't find one, that tells you something worth knowing."

## Facilitator Tips

- Whoever is raising the child is who gets thanked. Grandparents, aunts, foster parents, older siblings, group home staff. Check who the child actually lives with before you write.
- Do not attach anything. Not a reminder, not a form, not an invitation. One purpose per contact.
- Language access gets solved before the block, not during it.
- Watch for the staff member who cannot find anything true to say about a family. That is a conversation, not a failure, and it usually points at a kid nobody has looked at closely.
- Expect a few families to be thrown by it. Some will assume it is a setup for bad news. Warn staff to say early in the call that nothing is wrong.

## Display Idea

None. This one stays private. If a family writes back, ask permission before repeating any of it.
