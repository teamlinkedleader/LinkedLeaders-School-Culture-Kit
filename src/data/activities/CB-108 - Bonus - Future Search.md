# Culture Builder: Future Search (Bonus)

Describe the school you want in three years as if it already exists, then work backwards to Monday.

## Objective

Build a shared picture of the school three years from now, in the present tense and in concrete detail, then reason backwards to what has to be true at two years, one year, and this term. The reason most school visions do nothing is that they are written as aspirations rather than as descriptions, and aspirations have no first step.

## Time Needed

60 to 90 minutes. Works best as a retreat or PD block, with a cross-section of the community rather than staff alone.

## Supplies / Setup

- Chart paper, a long wall, and markers.
- A genuine cross-section in the room: teachers, classified staff, a few families, and students if they are old enough. This is what distinguishes Future Search from a leadership planning meeting.
- A three-year horizon. Not five, which is fantasy, and not one, which is just next year's plan.
- The rule that everything is written in the present tense.

## Sample Prompts

- **The past.** What has shaped this school into what it is now? What do we carry, for better and worse?
- **The present.** What is true right now that everyone in this room would agree on?
- **The future, present tense.** It is three years from now and this school is working. Describe a Tuesday. What do students do? What do staff do? What does a visitor notice in the first ten minutes?
- **Backwards.** For that Tuesday to be real, what has to be true in two years? In one year? By the end of this term?

## How It Works

1. **Name the history first, honestly (15 minutes).** What has shaped this school, including the hard parts. Skipping this makes the future work feel unmoored, and the people who have been here twenty years will not engage with a vision that pretends the past did not happen.
2. **Agree on the present (10 minutes).** Only what everyone in the room would sign. This is short and it is worth doing, because it establishes shared ground before anyone imagines anything.
3. **Describe the future in the present tense (25 minutes).** Groups write a Tuesday three years out as though it is happening. Present tense is the mechanic: "students eat lunch with adults" rather than "we will encourage." Concrete and observable, no adjectives.
4. **Find the common ground across groups (15 minutes).** Post the descriptions and read across them. The elements that appear in most or all of them are your actual shared vision. The idiosyncratic ones are worth noting but are not it.
5. **Work backwards and date the first step (15 minutes).** From the common picture, what must be true in two years, in one year, by the end of this term. Then name the single first action, with an owner and a date inside the next month. Without that last step this is a nice afternoon.

## Why It Works

- Present tense forces specificity. "We will build a culture of belonging" survives no scrutiny; "every student eats lunch with at least one adult each week" can be checked.
- Including families, classified staff and students changes the picture substantially, and it is the difference between a shared vision and a staff vision presented as shared.
- Naming the history first is what lets long-serving staff participate rather than sit out. A vision that ignores the past reads as a criticism of everyone who built it.
- Reading across the groups produces genuine common ground rather than a consensus negotiated in the room, which usually means the loudest position.
- Backwards planning produces a first step. Forwards planning produces a wish list.

## Script for the Leader

"We are going to describe this school three years from now as though we are standing in it. Present tense, no 'we will,' no adjectives. Tell me what a Tuesday looks like. What are the kids doing at 10:40. What does somebody walking in the front door notice in the first ten minutes. Then we are going to look at what all our descriptions have in common, and work backwards until we get to something one of us can start on before the end of the month."

## Facilitator Tips

- Get families and classified staff in the room. A Future Search with only certified staff is a strategy meeting with a different name.
- Enforce present tense relentlessly. It is the single mechanic that makes the outputs usable.
- Ban adjectives at the description stage. "Joyful" tells you nothing; "you can hear music in the hallway between classes" tells you what to build.
- Three years, firmly. Five years is unaccountable and one year is an operations plan.
- Do not close without a dated first action and a named owner, or the room will correctly read the whole thing as decorative.
- Bring the description back in six months and check it against reality. That is the only thing that makes the next one credible.

## Display Idea

The common-ground description, written out in the present tense on a single sheet, posted in the entryway with the date it was written. Add a mark next to each element as it becomes true.
