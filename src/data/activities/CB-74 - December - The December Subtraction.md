# Culture Builder: The December Subtraction (December)

Cancel three things instead of adding a holiday event nobody has energy for.

## Objective

Go through December and take three things off your staff's plate. A meeting, a duty, a deadline, a report, a required form. Then tell them, specifically, what you cancelled. Every instinct in December says add something festive. Your staff do not need one more thing on the calendar, even a fun one. The most generous thing available to you this month is subtraction, and it is free.

## Time Needed

20 minutes, in the first week of December.

## Supplies / Setup

- December's calendar, all of it. Staff meetings, PLCs, duty rosters, deadlines, data submissions, required trainings, evening events.
- The list of things you personally have asked for this month that nobody has questioned.
- Your assistant principal or office manager, who knows what is actually eating people's time better than you do.
- A pen, and a willingness to cancel something you like.

## How It Works

1. **List everything you are asking of staff in December (6 minutes).** Every meeting, every deadline, every form. Include the small ones. Especially include the ones that exist because you asked for them once and nobody ever stopped.
2. **Mark each one: does this have to happen in December (5 minutes).** Three answers only. Yes, legally or logistically. No, it could be January. No, it could not happen at all and nobody would notice. Be honest about how many land in the third column.
3. **Cut three. Actually cut them (4 minutes).** Not shorten, not combine, not "make optional." Optional means everyone still comes because they are afraid not to. Cancel a staff meeting outright and give the time back with no substitute. Push a deadline to January. Kill a duty for the month.
4. **Tell staff what you cancelled and why, plainly (3 minutes).** Name each of the three. Do not attach a request, an exchange, or a reminder about anything else. This is not a trade.
5. **Resist adding anything (2 minutes).** Look at the calendar with the three gone and leave the gap alone. The urge to fill it with a party, a potluck, or a wellness activity is the urge that created the problem.

## Why It Works

- December in a school is a long unbroken run with rising student dysregulation, illness, family stress, and no break until it ends. Adding a festive obligation on top of that reads as tone deaf no matter how good the intention.
- Cancelling a meeting outright and giving the time back is the clearest signal a leader can send that they know what the month actually costs. It cannot be misread as anything but relief.
- Most of what fills a December calendar is there through inertia. Nobody chose it this year; it just carried over.
- Naming the three specifically is what makes it count. Staff who get a vague "I've tried to lighten December" do not experience anything. Staff who hear "Thursday's meeting is cancelled and I'm not replacing it" do.
- Doing it in the first week matters. Cancelling something on the 18th is triage. Cancelling it on the 2nd changes how the whole month feels.

## Script for the Leader

"Three things are off the calendar for December. The 12th staff meeting is cancelled and I'm not replacing it with anything, so that's ninety minutes back. Winter data entry moves to January 9th. And morning duty is covered by me and Rob for the rest of the month. I'm not asking for anything in exchange and there's no catch. This month is long enough already and I'd rather give you time than throw you a party."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not announce it as a gift or attach it to the holidays. It is not a present, it is a correction, and framing it as generosity invites the reply that it should not have been there in the first place.
- Cancel at least one thing that you personally like or that was your idea. Staff will notice whether the cuts cost you anything.
- Never make something "optional" instead of cancelling it. Optional attendance at a leader's meeting is not optional and everybody in the building knows it.
- If a mandate genuinely cannot move, say so and name who requires it. Staff can accept a real constraint; what corrodes them is a fake one.
- Watch what nobody misses. Anything cancelled in December that nobody asks about is a candidate for permanent deletion. June, Week 3 asks exactly that question.
- Run it again in March, which is the other month where the calendar quietly fills with things nobody chose.
