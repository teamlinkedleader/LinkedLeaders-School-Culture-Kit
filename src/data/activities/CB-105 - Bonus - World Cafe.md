# Culture Builder: World Café (Bonus)

Three rotating conversations that build on each other instead of starting over.

## Objective

Get an entire faculty thinking about two or three real problems, in small groups, in a way where each conversation inherits the last one rather than repeating it. The reason most staff discussions produce nothing is that every table starts from zero and the loudest voice sets the direction. World Café fixes both with a host who stays put and a summary that carries forward.

## Time Needed

45 to 60 minutes. Three rounds of about 12 minutes plus setup and a close.

## Supplies / Setup

- One table per topic, two or three topics maximum. More than three and no idea gets developed.
- Chart paper and markers on every table.
- A table host per topic, named in advance and briefed. This is the role the method turns on.
- A visible timer and something to signal rotations.

## Sample Prompts

Run the same three rounds at every table, changing only the topic:

- **Round 1, current reality.** What do we know to be true here? What patterns do we notice?
- **Round 2, vision.** If we made real progress on this, what would students, staff and visitors actually notice?
- **Round 3, high-leverage actions.** What are the few actions with the greatest potential impact, and which of them should become consistent across the whole school?

## How It Works

1. **Pick two or three topics that are genuinely open (10 minutes of prep).** Not topics where you already have the answer. Staff can tell, and a rigged table produces polite silence. Good ones: student engagement, staff morale, academic consistency.
2. **Brief the table hosts properly (5 minutes of prep).** The host does not rotate. Their job is to welcome each new group, summarise the previous conversations in two minutes, push people to build rather than restart, and capture themes on the chart paper. A weak host turns the method back into three unrelated discussions.
3. **Run three rounds of about 12 minutes.** Everyone except the hosts moves to a different table each round. By round three, a participant is joining a conversation that already has two groups' thinking in it.
4. **Hosts summarise, they do not defend (throughout).** The two-minute recap at the start of each round is what makes the accumulation real. Remind hosts that summarising an idea they disagree with is part of the job.
5. **Close by naming what came up everywhere (8 minutes).** Read across the three sheets and name the themes that appeared at every table. Those are your faculty's actual priorities, arrived at by everyone rather than asserted by you.

## Why It Works

- The rotating structure means every participant contributes to every topic, so nobody sits through a discussion they have nothing to offer on.
- The stationary host is what makes conversations compound. Without it you get three parallel first drafts and no depth.
- Small groups surface the voices that never speak in a full staff meeting, which is usually where the accurate information is.
- Naming the cross-table themes at the end gives you a priority list your staff cannot dismiss as management's agenda, because they wrote it.
- It scales. The same structure works with 15 staff or 90.

## Script for the Leader

"Three tables, three rounds, and you are going to visit all of them. Your host stays put, and their job is to tell you what the last two groups said before you start talking, so we build on it instead of starting over. I am not at any of the tables and I am not steering this. At the end we are going to read across all three sheets and name what came up everywhere, and that is what we will work on."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not host a table yourself, and do not sit at one for long. Your presence changes what gets said.
- Brief the hosts separately and in advance. The method lives or dies on the two-minute recap.
- Three topics maximum. Five topics produces five shallow sheets.
- Do not let round three drift back into complaining. The question is what to do, and the host's job is to hold it there.
- Report back within two weeks on what happened to the cross-table themes. A café that produces no visible action teaches staff not to bother next time.

## Display Idea

Post all the chart paper together on one wall after the session, with the cross-table themes written on a separate sheet in the middle. It is a visible record that the priorities came from the room.
