# Culture Builder: Solve One Real Problem Together (October)

Team building that builds a team, because the team actually fixes something.

## Objective

Replace the team-building activity your staff quietly dreads with 25 minutes of mixed-group problem solving on a real, small, annoying thing in your building. Adults bond by doing something together that matters, not by catching each other or building a tower out of spaghetti. This gives you connection across groups and a fixed problem, which is a better trade than an icebreaker and a sore back.

## Time Needed

25 minutes in a staff meeting.

## Supplies / Setup

- Three or four genuinely annoying, genuinely small problems. Dismissal takes eleven minutes too long. The copier area is chaos. Nobody knows where the wheelchair-accessible route is. The staff bathroom on the second floor. Small enough to fix, real enough to matter.
- One problem per table, chart paper, markers.
- Mixed groups, assigned in advance, same principle as the potluck: nobody sits with their own team.
- Your genuine authority to say yes. If you cannot actually approve the solutions, do not run this.

## How It Works

1. **Bring problems you are honestly willing to have solved (5 minutes of setup).** The fastest way to poison this is to pick problems where you already have the answer you want. Pick things you find annoying too, and be ready to be told the cause is something you do.
2. **Name the constraints out loud (3 minutes).** Every group gets the same three: it costs nothing, it does not add work to anyone's plate, and it can start Monday. Those constraints are what make this produce a real answer in 25 minutes instead of a wish list.
3. **Mixed groups work the problem (12 minutes).** Each table gets one problem. The mix is the team building. A first-year teacher, a custodian, and the office manager working on dismissal will produce a better answer than any of them alone, and they will know things about each other by minute six that no icebreaker would have surfaced.
4. **Two-minute pitches (7 minutes).** Each group pitches their fix. Not a discussion, not a debate. Just the fix.
5. **Say yes to at least one, in the room, out loud (3 minutes).** Pick one and approve it on the spot with a start date and a named owner. This is the step the entire activity depends on. Approve nothing and you have taught your staff that being asked for input is theater, which costs you more than the meeting was worth.

## Why It Works

- Adults connect through shared work far more reliably than through games. A group that fixed dismissal together has a real relationship. A group that did a trust fall has a memory of being embarrassed together.
- The constraints are what make it work in 25 minutes. Without them, every group produces "we need more staff" and the meeting dies.
- Mixed groups put people in contact across the exact lines where school conflict lives, and they do it in a context where everyone has something useful to contribute.
- Approving something on the spot is the credibility engine. Staff will bring you real problems for years if the first one got solved by Monday, and never again if it did not.
- The custodian, the office staff, and the paraprofessionals usually know precisely why the annoying thing is annoying, and almost no school has ever asked them.

## Script for the Leader

"We're not doing an icebreaker. I've got four things about this building that annoy me, and I'm guessing they annoy you more. You're in mixed groups, one problem each, three rules: it's free, it doesn't add work to anybody, and it can start Monday. At the end I'm approving at least one of these out loud, today, with a start date. If one of these problems turns out to be caused by something I'm doing, say that. I'd genuinely rather know."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not include a problem you have already decided about. Staff can smell a rigged question and the whole meeting deflates.
- Put classified staff on every table on purpose. On operational problems they are the actual experts and being treated that way once will change how they show up all year.
- Resist solving it yourself. Your job in the twelve minutes is to walk around and stay quiet.
- Report back within two weeks on the one you approved, including whether it worked. A fix nobody hears about again reads as a fix that quietly died.
- Run it again in March with new problems. By then the staff will bring their own, which is the actual goal.
