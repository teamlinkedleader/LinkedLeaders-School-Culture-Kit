# Culture Builder: The Front Office Is the Front Door (September)

Design the first thirty seconds a family experiences, because it sets everything after it.

## Objective

Sit down with your office team and design what happens in the first thirty seconds when someone walks in or calls. Your office staff have more first contacts with families than every teacher in the building combined, and in most schools nobody has ever discussed with them what those contacts should feel like. They are left to invent it, usually while doing four other things.

## Time Needed

25 minutes with the office team. Not a staff meeting.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your office team, all of them, with the phones covered by someone else for 25 minutes. If you cannot cover the phones, you have found the actual problem.
- Every notice currently visible from the front counter, taken down and put on the table.
- The phone greeting currently in use, whatever it is.
- Coffee, because this is a group who are almost never given a meeting of their own.

## How It Works

1. **Ask them what the hardest contacts are (7 minutes).** Not what the rules are. What is genuinely hard. The angry caller at 8:05. The parent who does not speak English and the interpreter line takes nine minutes. The child who has been sick in reception. They will tell you things no one has asked them before, and this is most of the value of the session.
2. **Agree one opening line for the phone, and one for the counter (5 minutes).** Their words, not yours. Warm, short, and the same every time. Consistency here matters more than cleverness, because a family who calls three times should get the same school each time.
3. **Read the notices out loud (6 minutes).** Every sign visible from the counter. Most front offices accumulate a wall of prohibitions written to prevent one incident years ago. Read each one as a nervous parent and cut or rewrite anything that sounds like it is addressed to a suspect.
4. **Decide what they are allowed to do without asking (5 minutes).** Can they let a late child straight to class? Give a plaster? Tell a parent the answer rather than taking a message? Every decision they must escalate is a delay for a family and a small statement that they are not trusted.
5. **Say it to the rest of the staff (2 minutes to plan).** The office cannot hold a warm front door on their own if teachers send parents to them as a punishment or a dumping ground. Name that in the next staff meeting.

## Why It Works

- The office team has more first contacts with families than the entire teaching staff, and almost no school has ever designed those contacts deliberately.
- A consistent opening line makes the school feel like one organisation rather than a lottery depending on who picked up.
- Notices written to prevent an incident are read by hundreds of people who were never going to cause it, and they set the tone before anyone speaks.
- Widening what the office can decide without asking removes delay for families and is read by the team as trust, which it is.
- This is a group that is regularly given instructions and almost never given a meeting. The act of holding it is a large part of the effect.

## Script for the Leader

Opening the session:

"You three speak to more families in a week than I do in a term, and I have never once sat down with you to talk about what that should feel like. That is my mistake. I am not here with a script for you. I want to know what the hard calls actually are, and then I want us to agree how this place sounds when somebody rings it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Cover the phones properly. Holding this meeting while they answer calls tells them exactly how much you meant it.
- Let them write the opening line. A line handed down by a principal is recited; a line they wrote is used.
- Expect the notices conversation to surface something you personally put up. Take it down anyway.
- Ask directly what they are not allowed to do that they think they should be. Then change at least one of those things this week.
- Follow up in a month by ringing your own school from a number they will not recognise, and listen to what happens.
- Do this again in January when a term of new notices has appeared on the counter.

## Display Idea

The agreed opening line, written on a card and kept by the phone. Not for the family's benefit, for the team's, so a new person or a temp answers the phone the way the school has decided to sound.
