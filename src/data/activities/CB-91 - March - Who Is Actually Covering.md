# Culture Builder: Who Is Actually Covering (March)

Count who has lost their planning period this year, and find out it is the same six people.

## Objective

Add up who has actually covered classes this year, in hours, by name. Then look at the distribution, say it out loud, and change how the next one is assigned. Coverage is the largest invisible inequity in most schools. It falls on the agreeable, the competent, and the people physically nearest the office, and because each instance is small, nobody notices that a handful of staff have lost forty hours.

## Time Needed

25 minutes. Most of it is the counting, which somebody else can do for you.

## Supplies / Setup

- Whatever record exists of coverage assignments. Sub records, the daily coverage sheet, your office manager's memory, emails.
- A simple tally: name, number of periods covered this year.
- Your own number, counted honestly and including the ones you delegated at the last minute.
- Willingness to be embarrassed by what you find.

## How It Works

1. **Count it, by name, for the whole year (10 minutes, or hand it to your office manager).** Every period covered by a staff member instead of a sub. If the records are poor, reconstruct what you can and say the number is approximate. Approximate is enough; the distribution will be obvious.
2. **Look at the shape before you look at the names (3 minutes).** In most schools it is not close. A small group carries most of it, a middle group carries a little, and a third of the staff have covered nothing. Notice that before you start rationalising individual cases.
3. **Ask why those names (5 minutes).** The answers are almost never about need. They are about who says yes, who is near the office, who teaches a subject the schedule treats as flexible, and who is new enough not to refuse. None of those are fair reasons and all of them are the actual reasons.
4. **Show the staff the numbers, with your own included (4 minutes).** Anonymised as a distribution, not a leaderboard. Say plainly that it has not been fair and that you did not notice, which is worse than deciding it.
5. **Change the rule for the next one (3 minutes).** A rotation with a posted order, or a rule that nobody covers twice until everybody has covered once. Whatever it is, make it public and make it mechanical, because discretion is what produced the current distribution.

## Why It Works

- Coverage is invisible because each instance is trivial. Nobody resents one lost planning period; people resent the twenty-second, and by then they have stopped telling you.
- A planning period is not a break, it is when the job gets done. Taking it means the work moves to that person's evening, uncompensated and unrecorded.
- The people who carry the most are usually the most reliable staff in the building, which means the current system taxes competence and reliability specifically.
- Making the rule mechanical fixes it permanently. Any system that runs on who is willing will return to the same six people within a month.
- Admitting you did not notice is more credible than claiming it was a considered decision, and staff already know which one is true.

## Script for the Leader

"I counted coverage for the whole year. Twelve of you have covered nothing. Six of you have covered more than fifteen periods each, and two of those are over twenty. That is not a scheduling artefact, that is you six being reliable and me taking advantage of it without noticing, which is honestly worse than if I'd decided it. Starting Monday there's a posted rotation, everyone is on it, and nobody covers a second time until everybody has covered once. If you're on the high end of that list, you're at the back of the rotation until it evens out."

## Facilitator Tips

- Count your own coverage honestly and include it in the numbers. A principal who has covered zero periods and is presenting a fairness audit will not be taken seriously.
- Show the distribution, not a ranked list with names. Public leaderboards create a different problem.
- Expect the high-coverage staff to downplay it. They will say they did not mind. Do not accept that as evidence; they said yes for the same reason they always say yes.
- Include genuine exemptions openly rather than quietly. Certain roles cannot cover, and saying so publicly stops it reading as favouritism.
- Post the rotation where anyone can check it. A private fair system is indistinguishable from an unfair one.
- Recount in June. If the distribution has flattened, say so; if it has not, the rule is not mechanical enough.

## Display Idea

The rotation posted in the workroom with names crossed off as they come up, so anyone can see where they are in the order and that the order is being followed.
