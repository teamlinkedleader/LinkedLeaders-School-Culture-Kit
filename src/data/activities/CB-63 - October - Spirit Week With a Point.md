# Culture Builder: Spirit Week With a Point (October)

Five themed days that cost nothing, exclude nobody, and put adults in it too.

## Objective

Rebuild spirit week so it does what people think it already does. Most spirit weeks quietly sort students by what their family can buy, get about 30 percent participation, and involve three adults. This is 25 minutes of planning that produces five days every student can do with what is already in their closet, with every adult in the building participating and the leader going hardest.

## Time Needed

25 minutes to plan, two to three weeks before the week itself.

## Supplies / Setup

- Last year's spirit week themes, if you ran one.
- A calendar with the week you are targeting.
- The zero-cost rule, written at the top of the page before any theme goes on it: if a student needs to buy, borrow, or ask a parent for anything, the day is out.
- Somebody in the room who will tell you the truth about which themes flop.

## How It Works

1. **Run every theme through the closet test (7 minutes).** For each idea, ask whether a student with nothing extra at home can participate fully. Pajama day passes. Dress like a celebrity fails. Twin day fails harder, because it sorts by who has a friend. Class color day passes if the colors are common ones, which means no gold and no purple.
2. **Build days around identity, not costume (7 minutes).** The best themes ask students to show something true rather than acquire something. Wear the shirt of a place you have been. Wear something a family member gave you. Dress like your future job. Hat day with the story of the hat. These get higher participation than costume days and they produce actual conversations.
3. **Put every adult on the list, by name (5 minutes).** Not "staff are encouraged to participate." A sign-up with every adult's name, posted, including office, custodial, and food service. Full participation from adults is the single biggest driver of student participation, and it is entirely under your control.
4. **Assign one act of kindness or connection to a day (3 minutes).** One of the five days carries a second, quiet job: everyone writes a note to somebody, or every class does one thing for another class. It costs nothing and it is the difference between a fun week and a week that means something.
5. **Plan the capture and the close (3 minutes).** Decide who is taking photos each day and where they go. End the week by naming, out loud, the three moments that were actually good. Without the close, the week evaporates by Monday.

## Why It Works

- The closet test is the whole thing. Every spirit week has one day that quietly tells the poorest kids in your building to sit this one out, and nobody on the planning side notices because they are not the ones sitting out.
- Themes about identity get better participation than themes about costume, because they ask for something every student already has.
- Adult participation is the multiplier. Students read a building full of participating adults as permission, and a building where three adults dressed up as evidence that the whole thing is for someone else.
- Attaching one act of kindness turns a spirit week from a break in the routine into a piece of your culture, at zero additional cost.
- The named close is what makes it repeatable. A week nobody debriefs is a week that gets planned from scratch next year.

## Script for the Leader

For the announcement launching the week:

"Spirit week starts Monday, and here's my one rule: nobody needs to buy anything. Not one thing. Every day works with what's already in your house, and if it doesn't, that's my mistake and I want to hear about it. Every adult in this building is signed up, all five days, including me, and I've seen Mr. Alvarez's Thursday outfit. You're not going to want to miss it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Ask two students directly, before you publish, whether any day would be hard for someone with nothing extra at home. They will tell you faster and more accurately than adults will.
- Do not run a competition between grade levels with a prize. It turns participation into pressure and it always ends with one group feeling cheated.
- Have a small stash of spare items for whatever the week needs, offered quietly and without announcement. The offer has to be invisible or it defeats the purpose.
- Go hardest yourself on the day you would least want to. It buys more goodwill than anything you will say all month.
- Do not schedule spirit week against a testing window or the week before conferences. A tired staff running a spirit week produces a bad spirit week.

## Display Idea

A photo wall in the main hallway, five columns, one per day, filled in as the week goes. Include the adult photos in the same columns as the students rather than a separate staff section.
