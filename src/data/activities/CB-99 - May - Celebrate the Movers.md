# Culture Builder: Celebrate the Movers (May)

End the year celebrating distance travelled, not final position.

## Objective

Build the end-of-year celebration around how far students moved rather than where they finished, and give it the same weight and the same room as the academic awards. Most end-of-year ceremonies are a public restatement of the ranking that already existed in September. This is 25 minutes of planning that makes the ceremony reachable for the students who worked hardest.

## Time Needed

25 minutes to plan, in early May.

## Supplies / Setup

- Whatever growth data you have: reading levels, attendance, behaviour records, work samples from September.
- Teachers who can name a student who moved a long way without arriving anywhere impressive.
- The current awards plan, and the audit from April.
- The same stage, the same programme, and the same amount of time as the academic awards get.

## How It Works

1. **Ask staff for movers, not achievers (8 minutes).** One question: which student in your room travelled the furthest this year, regardless of where they finished. Teachers answer this instantly and it is almost never the same list as the honour roll. Push for the specific distance: from what, to what.
2. **Check the list against your quietest students (4 minutes).** Growth in behaviour, attendance, confidence, and willingness to try counts as much as academic growth. The student who came to school ninety days after coming forty last year moved further than anyone on the honour roll.
3. **Name the distance, not the level, when you announce it (5 minutes).** "Started the year unable to get through a paragraph, finished it reading a novel to the class." Never say what level they reached if the level is still behind, because that turns a celebration into an exposure.
4. **Give it the same weight as everything else (5 minutes).** Same ceremony, same stage, interleaved with the academic awards rather than in a separate section. A growth section at the end, after the real awards, teaches everyone which ones count.
5. **Tell the family in advance (3 minutes).** Call the family of every mover before the ceremony, so they come, and so they hear it from you first. For many of these families this will be the only positive ceremony their child has ever been in.

## Why It Works

- End-of-year ceremonies typically re-announce the existing ranking, which means the students who worked hardest to move often leave with nothing while the students who arrived already ahead collect everything.
- Growth is the one measure where the students furthest behind can genuinely outperform everyone, because they have more distance available to them.
- Naming the distance rather than the level protects the student. "Reads at a fourth grade level" said about a sixth grader in public is a humiliation dressed as praise.
- Interleaving is what makes it real. A separate growth section reads as a consolation category and students decode it immediately.
- The advance call is what gets the family in the room, and a family in the room for a good reason changes the relationship for the following year.

## Script for the Leader

For the ceremony:

"This next one is for Jasmine. In September, Jasmine could not get through a paragraph without stopping. Last week she read a chapter out loud to her class, by choice, because she wanted to. I do not care what level that is on anybody's chart. Nobody in this building travelled further this year than she did, and that includes me."

## Facilitator Tips

- Ask teachers for movers before you show them any data. Their instinct is better than the spreadsheet and it catches growth no test measures.
- Never say the ending level out loud if it is still behind grade. Name where they started and how far they came.
- Do not create a separate ceremony for growth. Separate is a demotion, however well intended.
- Make sure every classroom is represented. A growth list drawn only from the intervention groups tells everybody who it is really for.
- Call the families yourself. This is the highest-yield ten phone calls you will make all year.
- Keep the September work samples next year. This activity, and March's Growth Made Visible, both depend entirely on something having been filed in month one.

## Display Idea

A hallway display of before-and-after pairs, with the student's permission and without any levels, numbers or grades attached. Distance only.
