# Culture Builder: Name the First Win (September)

Find the three things already working after 30 days, say them out loud, and lock one in permanently.

## Objective

Thirty days into the school year, stop and name what is already working, specifically enough that people believe it, then make one of those things permanent before it gets buried under October. Schools are relentlessly good at cataloguing what is broken by the end of September and almost never pause to notice what took hold. This is a 20-minute staff meeting block that turns early momentum into identity.

## Time Needed

20 minutes, in a staff meeting around day 30.

## Supplies / Setup

- Chart paper, three columns: Working / Why / Keep or Let Go.
- Your own list of three wins, written in advance with names attached.
- Attendance, discipline, or survey data from the first month if you have anything clean. Optional, but a number next to a story is powerful.
- Your one-sentence culture statement on the wall.

## How It Works

1. **Go first with three specific wins (5 minutes).** Not "great energy." Name the moment, the person, and what it produced. "Third week, Damon noticed the new kid in his fourth period wasn't eating lunch with anyone and moved his own seat. Two other kids followed by Friday." Specificity is what makes the room believe you are actually watching rather than performing positivity.
2. **Collect from the room (7 minutes).** One question: what is working that we did not have last year? Write everything up. Push every vague answer for a moment with a name in it. "Morale is better" becomes a real win only when someone can say what happened on which day.
3. **Ask why each one worked (4 minutes).** Middle column. This is the part everyone skips, and it is the only part that transfers. A win you cannot explain is luck, and you cannot repeat luck in February.
4. **Pick one to make permanent (3 minutes).** Third column. One thing goes from "happening because everyone is fresh" to written into the schedule, the calendar, or the job description of a named person. Everything else stays on the list without a commitment.
5. **Tell students and families about one of them (1 minute to decide).** Pick the win that families would care about and put it in the newsletter or on the announcements this week. A school that only tells families about problems trains them to dread hearing from it.

## Why It Works

- Day 30 is the exact moment early wins are still visible and not yet routine. Wait until October and they have already been absorbed as normal, or lost under the first real crisis.
- Specific, named praise reads as attention. General praise reads as management technique, and staff can tell the difference instantly.
- The why column is what turns a nice meeting into a repeatable practice. Naming the mechanism is how a lucky September becomes a deliberate February.
- Making exactly one thing permanent is the discipline. A meeting that celebrates ten things and changes nothing is a pep talk, and staff stop showing up to those honestly.
- Sending one win outward costs nothing and starts building the family relationship you will want when something does go wrong.

## Script for the Leader

"Thirty days in. Before anybody tells me what's broken, and I know the list, we're spending twenty minutes on what's actually working, and I want it specific enough to have a name in it. I'll go first with three. Then we're picking one thing off this wall and making it permanent today, so it's still here in February when we're all tired and it would otherwise quietly disappear."

## Facilitator Tips

- Write your three in advance. Improvising this in the room produces exactly the generic praise you are trying to avoid.
- Do not let the meeting drift into problems. Name that the problems list is real and getting its own time, then hold the line for twenty minutes.
- If nothing is working, say so honestly rather than manufacturing wins. A fabricated celebration costs you more credibility than an honest "this month has been rough, here's the one real thing I saw."
- Watch who gets named. If your three wins are all the same visible people, you have found something out about your own attention.
- Repeat a version of this in January and again in May. The January one is the hardest and the most necessary.

## Display Idea

The three wins go up in the staff room with the permanent one circled and the date it was locked in. In February, when someone asks why the schedule has that block in it, the answer is on the wall.
