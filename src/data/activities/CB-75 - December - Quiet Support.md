# Culture Builder: Quiet Support (December)

Get help to the families who need it over break without making them ask or be seen.

## Objective

Work out which of your families will struggle over the winter break, and get them what they need in a way that costs them no dignity. Two weeks without school means two weeks without meals, structure, warmth, and adults for some of your students. Most schools respond with a visible charity drive that quietly humiliates the recipients. This is 25 minutes of designing the help so nobody has to be identified as needing it.

## Time Needed

25 minutes to plan, in the first two weeks of December.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your counselor, social worker, nurse, and secretary in one room. Between them they know almost everything.
- Your homeless and foster liaison data, and your free and reduced numbers.
- A list of what is actually available: district resources, food bank hours, community partners, the church that does coats, the family resource center.
- No sign-up sheet. That is the point.

## How It Works

1. **Ask the four people who know (10 minutes).** Counselor, social worker, nurse, front office. Ask directly: which families are you worried about over break? They will name them fast, and their list will be better than any data pull. Write names privately, not on chart paper in a shared room.
2. **Name what each one actually needs (5 minutes).** Not a generic hamper. Food that does not need cooking if the utilities are off. A coat in a specific size. Diapers. A gas card so a parent can get to work. Somebody to call on the 27th. Specific need beats generic gesture every time.
3. **Design the delivery so it is invisible (5 minutes).** Two rules. Nobody has to ask, and nobody can tell who received. The mechanisms that work: send an identical break packet home with every single student, with the extra help slipped into the bags of the families who need it. Or hand it to a parent at their car. Or let the counselor deliver it as a routine visit rather than a special one.
4. **Give every family the same thing publicly (3 minutes).** One break packet for all: the food bank hours, what is open, the school's emergency number, the counselor's line, and two easy things to do with a bored kid. The universal version is what makes the targeted version invisible, and it also catches the families nobody flagged.
5. **Assign the check-in and put it in a calendar (2 minutes).** Decide who is calling which families during the break, on which day, and what they will say. Then actually put it in their calendar, because a break check-in that lives in someone's memory does not happen.

## Why It Works

- Break is when the school's support structure vanishes for the students who depend on it most, and it is the longest such gap outside summer.
- A visible charity drive gets the goods to the family and takes something in exchange. Kids know which classmates got the hamper. Designing for invisibility costs nothing extra and preserves the relationship you will need in January.
- The universal packet is the mechanism that makes discretion possible, and it also reaches the families your staff did not know about. There are always some.
- The four people you ask in step one carry this knowledge informally and are almost never asked for it systematically. Asking dignifies their work and gets you a far better list than a data report.
- A planned check-in call during break is often the only contact a struggling family has with any institution for two weeks.

## Script for the Leader

For the staff meeting where you set up the universal packet:

"Every kid goes home with the same packet. Same one, no exceptions, so nobody can look at a bag and tell anything about anybody. Some families are getting more than that, and how that happens is being handled by Ms. Boyd and me, and it isn't going to be discussed in this building. If you're worried about a specific family, tell me privately and I'll add them. Nobody signs up for anything and nobody gets thanked publicly."

## Facilitator Tips

- Never photograph this, never mention it in a newsletter, and never let it become a story about the school's generosity. If it appears anywhere public, it has failed.
- Keep the list of named families off shared drives and out of email. Verbal or on paper, held by the two or three people who need it.
- Ask what they need rather than assuming. A family with no working stove does not want a turkey.
- Include your own staff quietly. Paraprofessionals and food service workers are often on the same income as the families you are helping, and they will never say so.
- Do not let the universal packet turn into an academic packet. Adding worksheets to a break packet converts care into an assignment.
- Repeat a lighter version before spring break, which gets none of December's attention and has the same problem.
