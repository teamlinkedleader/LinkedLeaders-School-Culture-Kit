# Culture Builder: The Fresh Eyes Walk (July)

Walk your own building as a stranger, before you stop being able to see it.

## Objective

Go through your school in July as though you have never been in it, and write down what a first-time visitor actually experiences. You lose the ability to see your own building after about six weeks. Everything becomes furniture. The peeling sign, the locked front door with no instructions, the wall of awards from 2014, the room that smells. None of it registers any more, and all of it registers to a family walking in for the first time in August.

## Time Needed

25 minutes, alone, in an empty building.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your phone, for photographs. You will not remember the list otherwise.
- Park where a visitor would park, not in your usual spot.
- Enter through the front door as a visitor would, not the staff door.
- Nothing else, and deliberately nobody with you.

## How It Works

1. **Start in the car park, at a visitor's space (3 minutes).** Can you tell which door to use? Is there a sign, and does it face the way people arrive? Photograph what you can see from a car.
2. **Walk to the front door and stop (4 minutes).** How long does it take to work out how to get in? What does the entry system say, and what tone does it take? "ALL VISITORS MUST REPORT" reads differently at seven in the morning to a nervous parent than it does to you.
3. **Stand in the entrance for two full minutes (4 minutes).** Do not do anything. What can you see, what can you hear, and what would you assume this school cares about from this spot alone? The first ten feet tell a family more than the website does.
4. **Walk the main corridor slowly and photograph everything dated (8 minutes).** Awards, notices, photographs, displays. Anything more than a year old is telling visitors that nothing has happened here since. Anything hand-made and current is telling them the opposite.
5. **Pick three things and fix them before staff arrive (6 minutes to list).** Not thirty. Three, chosen because a stranger would notice them in the first minute. Then do them, because a list of observations that nothing happens to is just a walk.

## Why It Works

- Familiarity blindness is real and it is fast. You genuinely cannot see your own building by October, which is exactly when you most need to.
- Doing it in July is the point. The building is empty, you are not being interrupted, and there is still time to change anything you find.
- The entrance carries disproportionate weight. It is the only part of the school every single family and visitor experiences, and it is usually the part nobody has looked at in years.
- Photographs beat memory. You will walk past the same peeling sign for three more years without the picture.
- Three fixes get done. Thirty get written on a list that survives until August and then dies.

## Script for the Leader

There is no audience for this one. The version worth saying to staff in pre-service week:

"I walked the building in July as though I had never been here. Parked where you park, came in the front, and stood in the entrance for two minutes. Three things I found: the visitor sign faces the wrong way, the awards case stops in 2014, and the first thing anyone reads on our door is a list of rules. All three are fixed. I would like your version of that walk too, because you have gone blind to different things than I have."

## Facilitator Tips

- Go alone. A colleague will explain away everything you notice, and explaining is the enemy here.
- Bring somebody genuinely new later, a new hire or a friend who has never visited, and ask what they noticed. They will find things you still cannot see.
- Photograph everything, including the things you think you will obviously remember.
- Check the accessible route while you are there, and actually use it rather than glancing at it.
- Read every sign out loud. Signs written to prevent a problem almost always sound hostile to the ninety-nine percent who were never going to cause it.
- Repeat it in January. The building will have accumulated a whole term of stuff nobody sees.

## Display Idea

Keep the July photographs. Take the same shots in June and put them side by side. It is the cheapest possible record of whether the year changed anything a visitor would notice.
