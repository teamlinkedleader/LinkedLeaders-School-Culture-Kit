# Culture Builder: Attendance Is a Belonging Problem (February)

Ask the ten families with the worst attendance what is actually happening, before sending another letter.

## Objective

Call the ten families whose children are missing the most school and ask, without threat, what is actually going on. Attendance is treated as a compliance problem and answered with letters, and the letters do not work because they address a decision nobody is making. Chronic absence is almost always a barrier or a belonging problem wearing the costume of a choice, and you cannot find out which from a spreadsheet.

## Time Needed

25 minutes to prepare and make the first calls. The calls themselves run across a week.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your chronic absence list, worst ten.
- Whatever letters have already gone home to these families, read in order. It is a sobering document.
- Your counselor or social worker, who already knows several of these families.
- Translation arranged in advance for any family who needs it.

## How It Works

1. **Read the letters they have already received (5 minutes).** All of them, in order, as that parent. Three letters escalating in legal tone, and none of them asking a question. This is the step that changes how you make the call.
2. **Call and ask, without any threat in the first sentence (10 minutes to start).** Open by saying you are not calling about the letters and this is not a warning. Then ask one question: what is making it hard to get to school at the moment. Then stop talking.
3. **Write down the actual reasons (5 minutes).** They cluster, and almost none of them are indifference. No transport since the car went. A younger sibling with no childcare. A child being bullied who has not told anyone. Anxiety that started in November. A parent working nights and asleep at seven. A uniform that no longer fits and no money to replace it.
4. **Fix what is fixable, this week (3 minutes to triage).** A surprising amount of it is: a bus pass, a later start agreed formally, a uniform quietly sourced, a change of seat, an adult to meet them at the door. Removing one concrete barrier does more than a fourth letter.
5. **Tell the child they were missed (2 minutes).** Separately from the family. Not "your attendance is a concern", which they have heard. "It was not the same without you" from an adult who means it. Chronic absence and not being missed feed each other, and one of them is free to fix.

## Why It Works

- Letters address a decision that is usually not being made. They are answering a compliance question when the actual question is what is in the way.
- The reasons cluster into a small number of concrete barriers, most of which a school can move at little or no cost.
- Reading the existing letters first is what makes the call sound different, because you will hear how they land.
- Families who have only ever been contacted about a problem expect the next call to be another one. Opening by saying it is not changes what you get told.
- Telling the child they were missed addresses the belonging half. A student who believes the building does not notice them absent has no reason to be in it.

## Script for the Leader

Opening the call:

"Mrs. Doyle, it is Mike from school. I want to say straight away that this is not about the letters and you are not in trouble. I have read those letters and honestly they are not much use to anybody. I am ringing because Callum has missed a lot this term and I do not actually know why, and I would rather ask you than keep guessing. What is making it hard to get him here at the moment?"

## Facilitator Tips

- Read the letters first. It changes your tone more than any script will.
- Say "you are not in trouble" in the first breath. These families brace for the call, and everything after that first sentence is heard through it.
- Do not ask for a commitment on the first call. You are gathering, not negotiating.
- Sort translation before you dial. A call in a language a parent cannot follow confirms everything they already fear about school.
- Watch for safeguarding disclosures and follow your process the moment one appears. This conversation surfaces them.
- Repeat with the next ten. There is no version of this that works as a one-off campaign.
