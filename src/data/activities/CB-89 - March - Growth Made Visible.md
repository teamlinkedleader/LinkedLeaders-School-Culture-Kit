# Culture Builder: Growth Made Visible (March)

Show every student physical proof of how far they have come, in the month that only measures what they cannot do.

## Objective

Put a piece of each student's own work from September next to the same kind of work from March, and let them see the distance for themselves. March is testing season, which means every measurement a student encounters this month is a comparison against a standard they have not met yet. This is twenty minutes of the only comparison that reliably motivates: the student against their own past self.

## Time Needed

20 minutes of class time in March. Requires that something was kept in September.

## Supplies / Setup

- A piece of work from every student from September or early October. If nothing was kept, start the practice now and run this in May instead. Say that honestly rather than faking it.
- The same task, or a close cousin of it, done this month.
- Nothing else. No rubric, no growth chart, no data talk.
- Your own example: something you were bad at in September and are better at now.

## How It Works

1. **Show them yours (3 minutes).** A real one. Your first attempt at something this year next to your current one. A leader who can only demonstrate mastery is not demonstrating growth, and the students who most need this activity are the ones who assume adults were always good at things.
2. **Hand back the September work cold (4 minutes).** No preamble, no instruction. Just give it to them and let them look. The reaction in the room is the activity; some students will laugh, some will be genuinely startled, and a few will not want anyone to see it.
3. **Put the two side by side and say nothing (5 minutes).** Students look at both, alone, without discussion. Do not narrate it, do not praise, do not point out what improved. The evidence works better without an adult interpreting it.
4. **One sentence, written on the September piece (5 minutes).** "One thing I can do now that I could not do then." Written on the old work, in their own words. Specific, not "I got better."
5. **They keep both (3 minutes).** Stapled together, taken home, not collected and not displayed unless the student chooses. This is their evidence, not the school's data.

## Why It Works

- Every measurement in March compares a student to a standard. For students below the line, that is the fourth or fifth message this year telling them where they fall short, and the cumulative effect is what makes kids stop trying in March specifically.
- Self-referenced growth is the one comparison that motivates rather than deflates, and it is available to every student including the ones furthest behind, whose growth is often the largest.
- The evidence is physical and it is theirs. A number saying they improved is an assertion; their own handwriting from September is proof they cannot argue with.
- Staying quiet is the discipline. Adults narrating the growth converts a private realisation into a compliment, which students discount.
- The leader showing their own bad early attempt does more for the students who have given up than any amount of encouragement, because it makes competence look like a process rather than a trait.

## Script for the Leader

For introducing it to staff:

"Everything we measure this month tells kids how far they are from where they're supposed to be. For a lot of them that's the fifth time this year. So one day in March we show them the only comparison that actually helps, which is them against themselves in September. Hand the old work back cold, put it next to the new one, and then say nothing. Do not tell them what got better. Let them find it. And show them something of yours that was bad in September."

## Facilitator Tips

- This only works if something was kept. If your building has nothing from September, say so plainly, start a folder this week, and run it in May. Do not manufacture a September artefact.
- Handle the student whose work has genuinely not changed, and there will be one or two. Find another domain where growth is visible, and do it privately.
- Do not display the pairs on a wall. Growth displayed publicly turns into growth compared publicly, which recreates the exact problem.
- Do not attach it to test prep or use it to predict scores. The moment it becomes about the test, it becomes another measurement.
- Tell staff to keep a September artefact next year for every subject. This activity is only ever as good as the thing that was filed in month one.
- Pair it with the leader's own example every year. That is the part students report back on.

## Display Idea

Nothing student-facing on a wall. The one display worth doing is in the staff room: teachers put up their own September-to-March pair from something they are learning, which quietly makes the point to the adults too.
