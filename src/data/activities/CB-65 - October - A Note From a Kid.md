# Culture Builder: A Note From a Kid (October)

Students write to the adults who are not their teachers, and those adults never forget it.

## Objective

Get a specific, handwritten note from a student into the hands of every adult in your building, with a deliberate bias toward the adults who never receive one. Teachers get thanked, occasionally. The bus driver, the night custodian, the lunch cashier, the paraprofessional, and the office manager get thanked essentially never, and they are often the adults a struggling kid trusts most. This is one 20-minute block that fixes that.

## Time Needed

20 minutes of class time. About 15 minutes of leader prep to build the list.

## Supplies / Setup

- A complete list of every adult who works in your building. Every one. Pull it from payroll rather than memory, because memory is exactly what causes the problem this activity solves.
- Note cards or half sheets of paper.
- Assignments made in advance, so no adult gets forty notes and no adult gets zero.
- Your own note, already written and delivered.

## How It Works

1. **Write yours first, to the adult you are most likely to overlook (leader, before the block).** Then tell staff you did it and who it went to. This is not a symbolic step. The whole activity turns on the building believing recognition applies to everyone, and staff will test that by watching who you chose.
2. **Assign adults to classes deliberately (15 minutes of prep).** Distribute the full staff list across classrooms so every adult is covered and no adult is buried. Assign the invisible adults to the classes most likely to write something real. Every class gets a mix of adults they know well and at least one they only know by sight.
3. **Teach the difference between a thank you and a note (5 minutes of the block).** "Thank you for your hard work" is a form letter and everyone can feel it. A note names a specific moment. Give students the frame: something you saw them do, something they did for you, or something you noticed about them. One moment, two or three sentences.
4. **Write (10 minutes).** Every student writes one. Students who do not know the adult they were assigned are given ninety seconds to go find out one true thing, from a teacher or from the adult themselves, and that ninety seconds is often the best part of the activity.
5. **Deliver by hand (5 minutes).** Students deliver in person wherever it is possible. A note left in a mailbox is nice. A fourth grader handing a card to the custodian and standing there while he reads it is a different order of thing entirely.

## Why It Works

- The adults with the least formal status in a school are frequently the ones with the most informal relationships with students, and they are almost never recognized for it.
- Specific beats grateful. A note that names a moment proves the student actually saw the person, and that is the whole payload.
- Hand delivery is what makes it land. The delivery is a face-to-face moment of connection that the note by itself does not create.
- Assigning coverage deliberately is what prevents the usual outcome, where the popular teacher gets thirty notes and the person who cleans the cafeteria gets none.
- The leader writing to the most overlooked adult first sets the standard for who counts here, and it will be noticed and repeated.

## Script for the Leader

For the staff meeting where you set it up:

"Every adult in this building is getting a handwritten note from a kid this week. Every adult. I pulled the list from payroll instead of writing it from memory, because when I wrote it from memory the first time I left four people off, and that's exactly the problem. Mine's already written and it went to Ray, who's here at five in the morning and who half of us have never talked to. The rule for the kids is one specific moment. No 'thanks for all you do.'"

## Facilitator Tips

- Pull the staff list from payroll or HR, not from your head. Every leader who does this from memory leaves someone off, and it is always the same kind of person.
- Include part-time staff, itinerant staff, the speech therapist who is in on Wednesdays, and long-term subs.
- Do not have students sign with only a first name if the adult will not know who they are. Name, grade, and teacher.
- Watch the delivery. It is the part that will make your week, and it is also the part that tells you which adults are startled to be thanked.
- Some adults will be visibly uncomfortable being recognized. Let it be brief and do not make a ceremony of it.
- Repeat a version in April, when the tank is emptier and the notes matter more.

## Display Idea

Do not display the notes. They belong to the person who received them, and a wall of them turns a private moment into a campaign. If you want something visible, post a photo of the deliveries instead.
