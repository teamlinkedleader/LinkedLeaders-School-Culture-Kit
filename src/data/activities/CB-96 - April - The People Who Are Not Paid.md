# Culture Builder: The People Who Are Not Paid (April)

Thank the volunteers by name, including the ones who have never set foot in the building.

## Objective

Build a complete list of everyone who gave your school unpaid time this year and thank them individually. Not a generic volunteer appreciation tea. The named list is the work, because the people most likely to be missed are the ones who never come to the school: the parent who sewed costumes at home, the grandparent who drives the team, the local business that quietly covered something.

## Time Needed

20 minutes to build the list. Thanking happens across the following week.

## Supplies / Setup

- Your sign-in records, which will be incomplete.
- Your office manager, PTO lead, athletic director, librarian, and whoever ran the school play, all asked directly.
- Note cards.
- A wall or the newsletter for the finished list.

## How It Works

1. **Ask the five people who know, not the sign-in book (8 minutes).** The visitor log catches the people who came in the door. It misses everyone who helped from home, drove somewhere, donated something, or did a job by phone. Ask the office manager, the PTO lead, the AD, the librarian and the play director for names, and expect to double the list.
2. **Write what each one actually did (4 minutes).** Specific and factual. "Drove the seventh grade team to eleven away games." "Sewed thirty costumes." "Reads with Mrs. Patel's class every Tuesday since October." A thank-you that cannot name the contribution proves nobody was paying attention.
3. **Include the businesses and the invisible ones (3 minutes).** The place that donated food, printed something at cost, or fixed something free. And the parents who volunteered once, whose one afternoon was harder to give than someone else's ten.
4. **Thank each one individually and in writing (5 minutes to start).** A card each, by name, with the specific thing. Hand delivery where possible, mail where not. This is the whole activity and it does not scale to a group email.
5. **Post the complete list publicly (2 minutes).** In the newsletter and on a wall, every name, no ranking and no hours counted. Publishing it also surfaces the people you missed, because someone will tell you, and that correction is useful.

## Why It Works

- Volunteer effort in a school is systematically undercounted because the counting mechanism is a sign-in sheet at the front door, and much of the work happens elsewhere.
- The people who give the least time are often giving the most, relative to what they have. A parent working two jobs who came in for one afternoon did something harder than a retiree who comes weekly.
- Naming the specific contribution is what distinguishes a thank-you from a mailing. Volunteers can tell instantly whether the school knows what they did.
- Publishing the full list recruits next year's volunteers more effectively than any appeal, because people see the range of what counts as helping.
- Businesses that were thanked by name, publicly, help again. Businesses thanked in a group line do not remember being thanked.

## Script for the Leader

For the newsletter that carries the list:

"Here is everyone who gave this school time they were not paid for this year. Some of them you will know. A lot of them you will not, because they never came through the front door: they drove, they sewed, they printed, they read to a class every Tuesday since October, they covered something for us without being asked. Every one of them got a note from us this week. If your name should be on this list and is not, that is our mistake and I would genuinely like you to tell me so I can fix it."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do not rely on the visitor log. It is the single biggest source of missed names.
- Ask the office manager last and specifically, after you think the list is finished. They will add four people.
- Invite the correction publicly. Someone always gets missed, and asking to be told turns an omission into a relationship rather than a slight.
- Do not rank by hours or name a volunteer of the year. It converts giving into competing and it discourages the small contributors.
- If you hold an event, hold it at a time working parents can attend, or accept that you have built it for the retired volunteers only.
- Keep the list. It is next year's recruitment list and it is more accurate than anything you will build from scratch.

## Display Idea

The full list posted in the entryway and printed in the newsletter, alphabetical, with the specific contribution next to each name and no hours totals anywhere.
