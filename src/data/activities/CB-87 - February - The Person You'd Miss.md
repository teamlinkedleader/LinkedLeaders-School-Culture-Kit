# Culture Builder: The Person You'd Miss (February)

Tell a colleague to their face what you would miss about working with them.

## Objective

Put staff in pairs and have them say out loud, to each other, what they would miss if the other person left. Not written on a card, not anonymous, not posted. Said, face to face, in February, when the building is at its lowest and a meaningful number of your staff are quietly deciding whether to come back next year. Twenty minutes, no materials, and it is the most uncomfortable and most effective thing in this collection.

## Time Needed

20 minutes in a staff meeting, ideally late February.

## Supplies / Setup

- Pairs, assigned by you in advance. Do not let people choose.
- A room where pairs can spread out enough not to be overheard.
- Your own example, ready, about a specific person, said publicly at the start.
- Nothing written. No cards, no prompts on the table, no notes taken.

## How It Works

1. **Go first, out loud, about one person in the room (4 minutes).** Name them and be specific. Not "Dana is a great teammate." Say the thing: "If Dana left, the thing I'd miss is that she tells me when I'm wrong in front of other people, and she's the only one who does, and this school would get worse without it." The room has to see you do the uncomfortable version before they will.
2. **Pair people who are not close (2 minutes).** Assign the pairs. Someone from another grade level, another department, or another job type. Two friends will do this well and learn nothing. Two people who work near each other without knowing each other is where the value is.
3. **Two minutes each, one direction at a time (6 minutes).** Person A tells person B what they would miss. Then swap. Time it and enforce it, because two minutes is long enough to get past the first polite answer, which is the point.
4. **The listener says only thank you (during).** No deflecting, no returning the compliment, no joking it away. Say this rule out loud before starting, because deflection is the default response and it robs the speaker.
5. **End it without processing (5 minutes).** No sharing out, no debrief, no asking how that felt, no writing anything down. Close the meeting. The instinct to process it publicly will cheapen it, and what people do with it afterwards is theirs.

## Why It Works

- Late February is when teachers quietly decide about next year. What holds people in a hard job is rarely the pay or the building; it is specific colleagues, and almost nobody has ever told them so.
- Spoken beats written for this. A card can be read as a gesture. A person looking at you and saying a true thing for two minutes cannot be dismissed.
- Assigning pairs across the usual lines is where the surprise lives, and surprise is what makes it land. It also reaches the staff who have no close friend in the building, which is the group most at risk of leaving.
- The no-deflection rule is doing real work. Adults in schools are practised at refusing praise, and refusing it takes the moment away from the person offering it.
- Not processing it afterwards is what keeps it from becoming a team-building exercise. The absence of a debrief is what makes it real.

## Script for the Leader

"I'll go first, and then you'll see why I'm not making this optional. Dana, if you left this building, the thing I'd miss is that you tell me when I'm wrong in front of other people. You're the only one who does it and this school would get measurably worse without you doing it. That's it, that's the whole exercise. You've got a partner. Two minutes each, tell them what you'd actually miss. And when it's your turn to listen, the only thing you're allowed to say is thank you. No jokes, no changing the subject."

## Facilitator Tips

- Do your example about someone genuinely, and pick a person whose contribution is real but not obvious. Choosing the most popular person in the building makes it a performance.
- Assign the pairs yourself and do not take requests. Ten minutes of thought on the pairings is what determines whether this works.
- Enforce the two minutes. People will finish in thirty seconds and stand there awkwardly, and the useful part happens after that.
- Do not run it in a meeting that has other business. Put it at the end and let people leave straight afterward.
- Include everyone. Custodial, office, food service, paraprofessionals. The staff least likely to have ever been told this are the ones not usually in the room.
- Do not repeat it more than once a year. Its power is entirely in being unexpected.

## Display Idea

None, and be explicit about that. Nothing said in this activity goes on a wall, into a newsletter, or into a social media post. If it appears anywhere, it was a performance.
