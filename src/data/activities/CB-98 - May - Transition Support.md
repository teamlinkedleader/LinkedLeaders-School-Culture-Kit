# Culture Builder: Transition Support (May)

Hand your leaving students over to the next school like people, not like files.

## Objective

Do a proper handover for every student leaving your building for a new one, and give each of them something to take with them. The record transfer happens automatically and it carries grades, attendance, and incidents. It carries nothing about who the child is, what works for them, or which adult they trusted. That information dies in your building every June unless somebody deliberately moves it.

## Time Needed

25 minutes with staff, in May. Plus one call or meeting with the receiving school.

## Supplies / Setup

- The list of students leaving for a new school: the top grade, plus any family that has told you they are moving.
- The staff who know those students best, which is often not their current teacher.
- A contact at each receiving school. Get a name, not a general office line.
- One index card per transitioning student.

## How It Works

1. **Ask the question the file cannot answer (10 minutes).** For each student, two things: what works with this kid, and who did they trust here. "Give her a job and she is fine." "He shuts down if you correct him in front of people, but he will do anything if you ask him privately." That is the handover worth making, and it exists only in adults' heads.
2. **Get it to a named human at the receiving school (5 minutes).** Not into the file, not in an email to a general address. A call or a coffee with the counselor or the grade lead who will actually have these students. Twenty minutes on the phone moves more useful information than a year of records.
3. **Flag the three or four who need a warm handover (4 minutes).** The students for whom a bad first month at the new school will be decisive. Those get a specific conversation, by name, and if possible an introduction before the year ends.
4. **Give each student something in writing to take (5 minutes).** One card from an adult here: something true about them, and one sentence about what they are walking in with. They keep it. A student arriving at a new school in September with a card in their bag from someone who knew them is carrying evidence that they were known.
5. **Tell them the door stays open (1 minute).** Say plainly that they can come back and visit, and that if the new place goes badly they can call here. Then honour it in September when a few of them do.

## Why It Works

- The information that helps a child settle into a new school is almost entirely absent from the official record, which is optimised for compliance rather than for the kid.
- A named human at the receiving school is what makes the handover real. Information sent to an institution goes into a system; information given to a person gets used in the first week.
- Transitions are one of the highest-risk moments in a student's school career. Students who struggle in the first month of a new school frequently never recover the ground.
- The card matters because students arriving somewhere new have no evidence that anyone has ever thought well of them there. Carrying that evidence in from outside changes how they walk in.
- Saying the door stays open costs nothing and is disproportionately important for students whose adults do not stay.

## Script for the Leader

For the conversation with the receiving school:

"I know the records are already coming. This is the part that is not in them. Marisol needs a job in the room or she will find one, and it will not be the one you want. Devon reads three years below and will hide it brilliantly, so do not let his confidence fool you into missing it. And Anthony trusted exactly one adult in this building, our custodian, and if somebody over there notices him in the first two weeks you will have a completely different kid by October."

## Facilitator Tips

- Ask the adults who know the student, not the ones who are supposed to. The paraprofessional and the lunch supervisor often know more than the teacher.
- Do not put the sensitive version in writing. This is a conversation, deliberately, and it should stay one.
- Push for a named contact. If the receiving school will not give you one, ask your own district for the introduction.
- Do not turn the card into a report card. One true thing, not a summary of their year.
- Include students moving out of district, whom schools routinely stop caring about the moment the address changes.
- In September, notice which of them came back to visit. That is the feedback on whether any of this worked.

## Display Idea

Nothing displayed. The cards belong to the students, and the handover conversations should stay private by design.
