# Culture Builder: This Year, I'm All In On (August)

A visible, year-long commitment wall that starts with individual reflection and ends with shared accountability.

## Objective

Give every staff member a chance to publicly declare one professional intention for the school year, something they're committing to grow in, lean into, or prioritize. Create a living display that reminds the whole team what they're working toward, together.

## Time Needed

15 minutes to launch. The display stays up all year.

## Supplies / Setup

- Half-sheets of cardstock, or pre-printed cards with the prompt at the top.
- Markers in school colors.
- A dedicated wall or bulletin board in the staff lounge or hallway, titled "This Year, We're All In On…"
- Your own card, written in advance.

## How It Works

1. **Distribute a card to each staff member** during pre-service week.
2. **Ask them to complete the sentence** "This year, I'm all in on…" with one professional focus. Encourage specificity over generality. Examples: "building real relationships with my most disengaged students," "asking for help before I hit a wall," "getting better at running data meetings," "making every classified staff member feel like a full member of this team."
3. **Give 5 minutes to write, then pair-share before posting.**
4. **Post all cards on the display wall.** The leader posts theirs first, visibly and publicly.
5. **Revisit at the 90-day mark.** Ask staff to find their card and write one sentence on the back about where they stand.

## Why It Works

- Public commitment increases follow-through, and that is backed by behavioral science rather than by hope.
- It invites staff into a culture of growth, not just compliance.
- When the leader posts first, it signals that vulnerability and learning are for everyone, not just new teachers.
- The display becomes a natural conversation starter all year long.
- The 90-day revisit builds in accountability without making it feel like evaluation.

## Script for the Leader

"Before we dive into the logistics of the year, I want us to do something a little different. I want us each to name one thing we're genuinely all in on this year, not because it's on an observation rubric, but because it matters to you. I'll go first."

Then share yours. Be specific, be honest, and be brave.

## Facilitator Tips

- Post yours before anyone else writes theirs, and make it something you could genuinely fail at. A safe leader card produces a wall of safe cards.
- Push for specificity. "Being a better teacher" is not a commitment; "asking for help before I hit a wall" is.
- Diarise the 90-day revisit the day you launch it. Left to memory it does not happen, and the revisit is where the accountability lives.
- Do not turn the revisit into an evaluation conversation. One sentence on the back of a card, written by them, read by nobody unless they choose.
- Include every role. A wall of teacher cards tells the rest of the building who this was for.

## Display Idea

A large banner or butcher paper header reading "We're All In On…" with the school name and year. Arrange the cards in a mosaic pattern. Photograph the completed wall and share it in your first parent-facing newsletter as a window into your school culture.
