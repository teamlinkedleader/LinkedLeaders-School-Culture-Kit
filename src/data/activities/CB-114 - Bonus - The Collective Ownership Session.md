# Culture Builder: The Collective Ownership Session (Bonus)

Ninety minutes that ends with team commitments and dated first actions, not a list of ideas.

## Objective

Build collective ownership across three priority areas in a single 90-minute faculty session, and end with commitments rather than conversation. This chains four methods deliberately: Appreciative Inquiry to open, World Café to develop, 15% Solutions for individual ownership, and team commitments to convert it into implementation. Each stage exists because the one before it is not enough on its own. Within the Collaborative Impact Framework this session runs Clarify, Collaborate and Commit in one 90-minute block. It deliberately does not include Coach, which is a separate follow-up and the phase this design most depends on. See [[CB-115 - Bonus - The Collaborative Impact Framework]].

## Time Needed

90 minutes. A faculty meeting block, a PD half-day, or the first session of a retreat.

## Supplies / Setup

- Three tables, one per priority area. Suggested: Student Discipline and Engagement, Staff Morale and School Culture, Academic Excellence. Change them to your school's real three.
- A named table host per table, briefed in advance. This is the role the middle 45 minutes depends on.
- Chart paper and markers on every table.
- Grade-level or department teams identified in advance for the final stage.
- Your own answer to the opening prompt, prepared and specific.

## Sample Prompts

- **Appreciative Inquiry opener.** Describe a time when our school was at its best. What made that success possible? What conditions should we recreate?
- **World Café round 1, current reality.** What do we know to be true? What patterns do we notice?
- **World Café round 2, vision.** If we made significant progress, what would students, staff and visitors notice?
- **World Café round 3, high-leverage actions.** What are the few actions with the greatest potential impact? Which practices should become consistent across the school?
- **15% Solutions.** What is one action you can personally take this week without needing additional permission or resources?
- **Closing reflection.** Complete the sentence: one commitment I will make this year is…

## How It Works

1. **Welcome, 5 minutes.** State the purpose plainly: to learn from one another and build collective ownership. Say what will happen and say that the session ends with commitments, so nobody is surprised by the last twenty minutes.
2. **Appreciative Inquiry, 15 minutes.** Open on when this school was at its best and what made it possible. You go first with something specific. This stage exists to establish that the conversation starts from evidence of capability rather than from deficit.
3. **World Café, 45 minutes.** Three tables, three rounds of about 15 minutes. Everyone rotates, hosts stay. Hosts welcome each group, summarise the previous conversations in two minutes, push people to build rather than restart, and capture themes on chart paper. Round 1 is current reality, round 2 is vision, round 3 is high-leverage actions.
4. **Gallery walk and 15% Solutions, 20 minutes.** Ten minutes reading all three tables and marking the most impactful ideas. Then ten minutes where every person writes one action they can take personally this week, with no permission and no resources required. Individual ownership before team commitment, in that order, because a team commitment made before anyone has claimed anything personally tends to belong to nobody.
5. **Team commitments, 15 minutes, and this is the stage that cannot be cut.** Grade-level or department teams name one collective commitment, one measurable indicator of success, a first action inside two weeks, and how progress will be monitored. All four parts, in writing. Close with the reflection sentence.

## Why It Works

- Appreciative Inquiry builds optimism and it gives the room evidence that improvement is possible here, not just in theory.
- World Café creates broad participation and shared understanding. Every person contributes to all three priorities rather than to the one they were assigned.
- The gallery walk lets the whole faculty see the whole picture before anyone commits, so commitments are made against the full set of ideas.
- 15% Solutions creates personal ownership. It is the stage that stops the session's outcomes belonging entirely to the leadership team.
- Team commitments convert conversation into implementation and accountability. Without the measurable indicator and the two-week first action, the previous 75 minutes produce good feeling and no change.

## Script for the Leader

"Ninety minutes, and I want to be clear about how it ends: with commitments, not with ideas. We start with when this place was at its best, because I want us reasoning from something real. Then three tables, three rounds, and you visit all of them. Then everybody writes one thing they can personally do this week without asking anyone's permission. And we finish with your team naming one commitment, how you will know it is working, and what you are doing about it in the next two weeks. I am not steering the tables and I am not going to tell you what the priorities should be."

## Facilitator Tips

- Keep the focus on curiosity rather than debate.
- Invite every voice, and use the table hosts to do it rather than doing it yourself.
- Capture ideas visually throughout. Chart paper on every table, not notes on a laptop.
- Celebrate the patterns that emerge, especially where the same theme appears at all three tables.
- End with commitments rather than simply ideas. If you are running short, cut the gallery walk, never the commitments.
- Brief your table hosts properly and separately. Three weak hosts turns the strongest 45 minutes of this design into three unrelated discussions.
- **Book the Coach session before you run this one.** The framework's fourth phase is where this design either lands or evaporates, and it is the phase every school skips. Put a date in the calendar during planning, not afterwards.
- At that follow-up, work the Coach questions rather than asking for status: what progress have we observed, what evidence shows we are improving, what barriers need removing, where is coaching needed, and what should we celebrate. A status round produces reassurance; those questions produce information.
- Report back within two weeks regardless. This is a high-investment session and staff will read your follow-through as the verdict on whether it was real.

## Display Idea

All three tables' chart paper posted together, with the team commitments written on a separate sheet alongside, each showing its measurable indicator and its two-week action. Add the outcome next to each commitment as it lands.
